Our verdict

The short answer: Both party-capable, both airport islands, wildly different bills. Mykonos (4.3) is the international scene: 5.0 nightlife, the celebrity beach clubs, and Delos half an hour away by boat for the best archaeological day trip in the Aegean — with affordability of 1.0, which is not a typo. Kos (3.7) parties too — Nafklirou, the bar street in Kos Town, is infamous — but it's a package island at package prices (3.8), with better swimming than it gets credit for (4.0) and the Asklepion, the most important ancient medical sanctuary in the Greek world. Mykonos is a three-day island; Kos is a four-day one.

Choose Kos if…

  • You want to swim more than you spend. Tigaki is ten kilometres of fine sand with a cycle path running its full length and flamingos on the salt pan behind it in spring.
  • Your budget is finite. Kos takes 3.8 for affordability against Mykonos's 1.0; beers on Nafklirou are €4–6, while sunbeds at Nammos on Psarou start at €100 per pair.
  • You want antiquity you can walk into. The Asklepion is three terraces of temples and healing rooms 4km from town, where Hippocratic medicine was taught — Kos scores 4.2 for culture, Mykonos 3.0.
  • You'd rather not drive. Kos Town is perfectly flat and best explored on a rented bicycle, and the paths run out along the north coast to the Tigaki salt pans.
  • You want side trips. Nisyros's active crater is a boat from Kardamena with five hours on the island, and the summer ferry to Bodrum in Turkey takes thirty minutes.

Choose Mykonos if…

  • Nightlife is the reason you're going. 5.0 against Kos's 4.0: the Matogianni cluster — Astra, Semeli, Void — from midnight, then Cavo Paradiso, the cliff superclub that doesn't fill until 2am.
  • You want Delos. The boat leaves the Deliana Pier in Chora at 09:00 for the Terrace of the Lions and the House of Dionysus mosaics, with the Naxian Lions in the site museum. Back on the 13:30.
  • You want a beach with a scene attached. Kalo Livadi is 800 metres of golden sand with Nice n Easy on it; Psarou is small, sheltered and full of people who came to be seen.
  • You're going in July or August and want that to be the point. Both months rate "perfect" here — most crowded, most expensive, most Mykonos — and the crowd is the experience.
  • You still want somewhere to hide. Fokos is an end-of-the-road bay on the north coast: one taverna, no sunbeds, no music, reached on a rough dirt track from Ano Mera.

The bill is the whole comparison

Affordability is where these islands separate, and 1.0 against 3.8 is about as wide as our scale goes. It shows up in specific numbers rather than a vague premium: sunbeds at Nammos on Psarou start at €100 per pair and run into four figures for the front row, while a beer on Nafklirou costs €4–6. Kos runs on charter traffic — Ryanair, Aegean, Sky Express into an international airport — and prices like the package island it is. Mykonos's own calendar describes August as the most crowded and most expensive month and does not apologise for it. If you would be checking the bank app on Wednesday, you have already answered the question.

There is one place Mykonos wins on value, and it's the archaeology. Delos costs a boat ticket from the Deliana Pier and a morning, and buys you the mythical birthplace of Apollo and Artemis, the Terrace of the Lions, the Dionysus mosaics and a site museum holding the original Naxian Lions — the finest ancient site in the Cyclades. Kos answers with more breadth for less effort: the Asklepion on its three terraces above the town, the plane tree where Hippocrates is said to have taught, and the Knights of St John castle at the harbour mouth, all within a flat morning's cycling. Kos's 4.2 is the wider score; Mykonos's 3.0 is one extraordinary day.

Doing both, and which month to pick

There is no ferry between them worth planning a trip around. Kos is Dodecanese, reached from Piraeus in 11–14 hours on the overnight into Kos Town; Mykonos is Cyclades, 2.5 hours from Rafina into Tourlos. Both have international airports, so the honest answer to "can I do both" is that you route through Athens and treat them as two separate holidays — three days on Mykonos and four on Kos, which is what the guides suggest anyway. If you have one week, don't try. The transfers will eat a day at each end, and a Mykonos budget stretched over two islands buys you a worse version of both.

Month matters more here than on most pairs. June is the shoulder that still delivers on both: Mykonos has the full scene running with crowds "not yet suffocating" and prices below peak, while Kos has swimmable sea and the Asklepion walkable in daylight without melting. September is better still — Mykonos calls it the sweet spot, water at its warmest and prices starting to drop, and Kos calms down as the charters thin from the third week. The divergence is at the edges of the year: Mykonos is "avoid" from November through March, with most things shut by mid-November, while Kos stays mild, quiet and rideable, and rates February good for cycling.

If nightlife means table service and DJs you've heard of, Mykonos is simply the product, and you budget accordingly — our Budget page will be blunt with you. If it means bars, beach days and not checking the account on Wednesday, Kos wins on points. There is no shame version of this choice: both are doing exactly what they advertise. Verdict: Mykonos for the scene, Kos for the season — decide with your bank app open.

Common questions

Can I visit both Kos and Mykonos in one trip?

Only by flying. They sit in different island groups — Kos is Dodecanese, 11–14 hours from Piraeus by ferry; Mykonos is Cyclades, 2.5 hours from Rafina — and there is no sea link worth planning around. Both have international airports, so you route via Athens and treat them as two holidays: three days on Mykonos, four on Kos. In a single week, pick one.

Which is cheaper, Kos or Mykonos?

Kos, decisively — 3.8 for affordability against Mykonos's 1.0, the widest gap on the scale. The concrete comparison: beers on Nafklirou, the bar street in Kos Town, run €4–6, while sunbeds at Nammos on Psarou start at €100 per pair and reach four figures front-row. Kos prices like a package island because it is one.

Which is better for nightlife?

Mykonos, and it isn't close: 5.0 against 4.0. The evening runs from sunset cocktails in Little Venice through the Matogianni bars — Astra, Semeli, Void — from midnight, to Cavo Paradiso, the cliff superclub above Paradise Beach, which doesn't fill until 2am. Kos's Nafklirou is a genuine wall of bars and clubs from June to September, but it's one street.

Which has better beaches?

Mykonos edges it, 4.3 to 4.0, but they're different products. Mykonos gives you Kalo Livadi's 800 metres of golden sand, sheltered Psarou and wild Fokos with a single taverna. Kos gives you scale and quiet: Paradise Beach with volcanic bubbles rising through the sand, Agios Stefanos with its basilica ruins and swimmable islet, and ten kilometres of Tigaki with a cycle path.

Which is better for families?

Kos. The island is flat enough to cycle, Tigaki is very shallow with a gradual entry that's safe for children, and the package infrastructure and 3.8 affordability score take the stress out of a week. Mykonos has family-workable beaches at Kalo Livadi and Kalafati, but the island's whole economy is aimed at a different visitor.
