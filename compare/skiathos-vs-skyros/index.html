Our verdict

The short answer: Skiathos, 3.9 to 3.4, if you are choosing one — but the more useful thing to know is that these two are barely the same trip. Both are officially Sporades, and they are not on the same ferry network: Skiathos runs from Volos and Agios Konstantinos, Skyros from Kymi in Evia. You cannot casually hop between them. Skiathos is 49 km² of pine and sand with an international airport, a beach score of 4.6 and nightlife at 4.7. Skyros is 209 km² of two completely different landscapes, a whitewashed chora that looks Cycladic, and culture at 3.8 against Skiathos's 2.0.

Choose Skiathos if…

  • Beaches decide it. 4.6 against 4.0 — Koukounaries, Banana, Vromolimnos and boat-only Lalaria are a stronger set than anything on Skyros.
  • You want to fly direct from Europe. Skiathos's airport takes international summer traffic; access 4.5 against 2.5.
  • You want an evening. Nightlife 4.7 against 2.8 — this is the only Sporades island with a real season after dark.
  • You want everything within twenty minutes. One main road, a frequent bus, and 49 km² means no long drives.
  • You are travelling with people who expect organised beaches and a choice of restaurants.

Choose Skyros if…

  • You want a chora. Skyros Town spills white below a Byzantine castle and monastery, and it looks and feels more Cycladic than Sporadic.
  • You want living tradition. The Skyros carnival — the Geros in his goat mask and bells, the Korela, the Frangos — is one of the oldest-looking festivals in Greece and nothing about it is staged for tourists.
  • You want culture over sand: 3.8 against Skiathos's 2.0, the lowest culture score in the Sporades.
  • You want the cheaper island — 4.2 for affordability against 3.0, a large gap.
  • You want the ponies, the carved furniture and the ceramics — Skyros has a craft identity that survived rather than being revived.

They are not neighbours in any practical sense

This is the fact worth carrying away. Skyros sits well south of the other three, off the coast of Evia, and its ferry runs from Kymi — not from Volos or Agios Konstantinos with the rest of the group. There is also a small airport with flights from Athens. In practice, a Skiathos–Skyros combination means going back to the mainland and driving a long way, or flying via Athens. If you were imagining island-hopping the Sporades, that plan works for Skiathos, Skopelos and Alonnisos, and it does not include Skyros.

Skiathos is the beach island

4.6 is one of the higher beach scores on this site and it is concentrated: over sixty beaches on 49 km², most on the sheltered south coast, most with pine behind the sand. Koukounaries is the famous one and deserves it, with a protected pine lagoon just inland. Lalaria on the north coast — white pebbles, a rock arch, reachable only by boat — is the photograph. Add a frequent bus, boat trips and watersports, and the practical machinery of a beach holiday is all in place.

What Skiathos does not have is anything much to look at inland: culture scores 2.0, the ruined Kastro and the Bourtzi being about the extent of it. That is fine if you know it going in.

Skyros is two islands stuck together

The north is green, farmed and pine-covered; the south is Mount Kochylas, bare rock and gorse, home to the wild Skyrian ponies — a small endemic breed that has been on the island since antiquity. Between them, the chora climbs a rock under its castle in a tumble of flat-roofed white houses that would not look out of place on Naxos.

The interiors are the surprise. Skyrian houses are traditionally crammed with carved wooden furniture, embroidery and imported ceramic plates displayed on the walls, and the Faltaits Museum in the chora preserves the whole idea properly. Palamari on the northeast coast is a substantial fortified Bronze Age settlement, and the beaches at Magazia and Molos below the town are long and sandy — 4.0, respectable rather than remarkable.

The carnival is a real reason to choose Skyros

For three weekends before Lent, men of the island dress as the Geros — a goatherd hung with dozens of heavy bells, wearing a goat-skin mask — and move through the streets with the Korela and the Frangos in a rite that predates any explanation anyone offers for it. It is loud, physical and completely unpackaged. If you travel in February rather than August, this is the strongest argument on either page.

The honest verdict

Skiathos for a summer beach holiday, unambiguously — better beaches, direct flights, and the only nightlife in the group. Skyros for a slower, stranger, cheaper trip built around a village, a landscape and a craft tradition, ideally outside high season. Do not try to do both in one week. And be honest about which you are actually after: Skyros rewards curiosity and punishes anyone expecting Koukounaries.
