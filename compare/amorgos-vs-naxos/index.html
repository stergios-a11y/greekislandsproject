Our verdict

The short answer: Amorgos and Naxos sit on opposite ends of the Cyclades archipelago, both geographically and in spirit. Naxos (4.5) is the largest and most self-sufficient island of the group — a complete destination with mountain villages, marble quarries, exceptional beaches (4.8), and the easy infrastructure that comes from being a major regional capital, airport included. Amorgos (4.0) is long, narrow, mountainous, and magnificently remote — seven to nine hours by ferry from Piraeus on the Small Cyclades line, an island that has stayed authentically itself. The Hozoviotissa Monastery, plastered white against a 300-metre cliff above the Aegean, is the single most dramatic image in the Cyclades. The beaches are fewer but unspoiled (4.0). There are no large resorts, no significant nightlife, and only one main road.

Choose Naxos if…

  • You're travelling with children. Plaka is 5 km of fine white sand with a very gradual entry and tamarisk shade; Agios Prokopios is 10 minutes from town with the best facilities on the west coast.
  • You want to fly. Naxos has a domestic airport (Olympic, Sky Express); ferries from Piraeus are about 3.5 hours fast or 5–6 on Blue Star, multiple daily year-round, and Rafina serves it too.
  • Villages and food are half the trip. Halki's Vallindras distillery for Kitron, Apeiranthos's marble streets and five small museums, Panagia Drosiani's 7th-century frescoes, graviera and patatato in Filoti.
  • You want a real hike with a summit. Mount Zas at 1,001 m is the highest point in the Cyclades — a 3-hour return through fir forest past the Cave of Zeus, with the whole archipelago below on a clear day.
  • You plan to hop. Paros is 30 minutes away, the port is in Naxos Town, and Naxos is the transfer point for Amorgos and the Small Cyclades — the base from which the eastern Cyclades open up.

Choose Amorgos if…

  • The monastery is the trip. Hozoviotissa is 11th century, reached by 300 steps cut into the rock; the monks serve loukoumades and rakomelo. Go at 9am — in high season it's a queue by mid-morning.
  • You hike. The paths between Tholaria and Lagkada above Aegiali are the finest on the island, the trail network is nearly empty even in August, and the long route from Aegiali to Hozoviotissa opens up in September.
  • You want Agia Anna at 8am — the Big Blue cove below the cliff, 100 m of pebble, cold clear water, no facilities at all. Maltezi, a short path south, is usually empty.
  • You're happy with one good bar. Jazzmin in Chora — a serious wine list, jazz on the speakers, open till 2am — is the nightlife (3.5), and psimeni raki after dinner at Tranzistoraki is the rest of it.
  • You've done the popular Cyclades and want the earlier version. Amorgos is open May through October only, has two ports, Katapola and Aegiali, and fewer ferries — the effort is the filter.

Ferry logistics, and doing both in one trip

This is the axis that actually decides most people. Naxos scores 3.8 for access and Amorgos 2.5, and the difference is the whole story. Naxos: airport, fast ferries from Piraeus in about 3.5 hours, Blue Star in 5–6, multiple daily all year, docking in Naxos Town five minutes from most accommodation. Amorgos: no airport, direct from Piraeus 7–9 hours on the Small Cyclades line, limited frequency in winter, and two ports to choose between — Katapola is the main one, Aegiali in the north is closer to Hozoviotissa and Tholaria. Pick your port before you book a room, not after. Fares are similar (Naxos €38–55, Amorgos €40–55); time is the cost.

The good news is that the two connect directly — Amorgos's own guide says the faster route is via Naxos, and the departure from Katapola serves Piraeus, Naxos or the Small Cyclades. So doing both is natural: fly into Naxos, spend four nights on the 4-Day Itinerary (Portara and the Kastro, Plaka and Kastraki, the marble villages and Zas, the Kouros of Apollonas), ferry on to Amorgos for another four (Chora and Minoa, the monastery and Agia Anna, Aegiali and the northern villages, Kalotaritissa), then either ferry back to Naxos and fly out or take the slow line straight to Piraeus and watch the Cyclades go by. Both islands score 4.0 for car need; rent on each. Do Naxos first — it is the easier landing, and Amorgos is the island you want to end on.

Which one in September, and what the wind does

September is the right month for both, and the reasons differ. On Amorgos it is the best month outright: the meltemi softens, the sea is at peak warmth, the monastery and the beach fit in the same day without crowds, and the long hiking trail from Aegiali to Hozoviotissa becomes accessible. On Naxos it is simply perfect — warm, the west-coast beaches emptying, the Portara at sunset without the August crush. If you can only give one of them a September week, give it to Amorgos; Naxos is generous in May and June too, when the beaches are empty and the days are long, while Amorgos in May is still pre-season opening up.

High summer is where they part. Amorgos in July is great — hot, the meltemi reliable, Big Blue fans arriving from across Europe, Agia Anna packed at noon so you go at 8am — and in August merely ok, with the wind at its strongest and meanest and the monastery climb a queue by mid-morning; the escape is the trail network, long and hot but nearly empty. Naxos is big enough to absorb August: Plaka goes towel-to-towel and the hilltop villages bake, but Apeiranthos and Halki stay breathable, the south-coast beaches stay sheltered when the meltemi blows, and the 15 August panigiri in Filoti runs three days into the small hours. Easter is a draw — Filoti's panigiri on Naxos, the monastery on Amorgos — and both are great in April. Winter belongs to neither.

The honest verdict

Choose Naxos if you want a complete island experience with everything in reach: family-friendly beaches, hiking, mountain villages, an airport, and easy ferry access for hopping. Choose Amorgos if you specifically want remoteness — couples seeking genuine quiet, hikers who want the Fotodotis trail and the Hozoviotissa pilgrimage, or travellers who have done the popular Cyclades and want to know what came before tourism. With eight days and a September to spend, the honest answer is both: Naxos first, Amorgos last, and the ferry between them is the point, not the price.

Common questions

Can I do Amorgos and Naxos in one trip?

Yes, and it's the natural pairing. Naxos is the transfer point for Amorgos — the faster route from Athens is via Naxos rather than the 7–9 hour direct line from Piraeus — and Amorgos's departures from Katapola serve Naxos directly. Fly into Naxos, spend four nights (beaches, marble villages, Zas), ferry on to Amorgos for four more (Hozoviotissa, Aegiali, the hiking), then ferry back to Naxos to fly out, or take the slow line straight to Piraeus. Naxos first, Amorgos last.

Which is cheaper, Amorgos or Naxos?

Naxos, a little. It scores 4.0 for affordability against Amorgos's 3.5, and it's one of the few Cycladic islands where a normal traveller can stay a full week. Ferry fares are nearly identical (Naxos €38–55, Amorgos €40–55), so the difference is rooms and the cost of getting there in time rather than money — Amorgos has no airport and the direct ferry is 7 to 9 hours. Both need a rental car (4.0), so budget the same for that on either.

Which is better for families, Amorgos or Naxos?

Naxos, and it isn't close. Plaka is 5 km of fine white sand with a very gradual entry and natural tamarisk shade; Agios Prokopios, 10 minutes from town, has the best facilities on the west coast; Agios Georgios is the town beach with everything behind it. There's an airport, so no long ferry with a pushchair. Amorgos's family beach is Aegiali — sandy, shallow and calm — but the island's set pieces are a 300-step monastery climb and a pebble cove with no facilities.

Which is better for hiking, Amorgos or Naxos?

Amorgos, for the trails; Naxos, for the summit. Amorgos's network above Aegiali — the path between Tholaria and Lagkada, the long route from Aegiali to Hozoviotissa that opens in September — is world-class from March through November and nearly empty even in August. Naxos has Mount Zas, at 1,001 m the highest point in the Cyclades, a 3-hour return through fir forest past the Cave of Zeus. If walking is the reason for the trip, choose Amorgos and stay at the Aegiali end.

Which is better in September, Amorgos or Naxos?

Amorgos. September is its best month outright — the meltemi softening, the sea at peak warmth, the monastery and Agia Anna in the same day without crowds, and the long Aegiali-to-Hozoviotissa trail accessible. Naxos in September is also perfect, but Naxos is equally good in May and June when the beaches are empty, while Amorgos in May is still pre-season. If you have one September week, spend it on Amorgos; give Naxos your early-summer week instead.
