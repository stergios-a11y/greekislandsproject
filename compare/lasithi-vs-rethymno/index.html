Our verdict

The short answer: Lasithi edges it, 4.0 to 3.8, and the two are the quieter alternatives to Chania and Heraklion — the halves of Crete people pick once they have stopped defaulting to the famous corners. Lasithi wins on beaches (4.0 to 3.8) and has its own airport at Sitia. Rethymno wins on culture (4.5 to 3.5) with the better-preserved old town in Crete, and on price (3.7 to 3.2). Both are four-day propositions. The split is beaches east against a town in the middle.

Choose Lasithi if…

  • You want the east's singular places: Vai's palm forest, Spinalonga, Chrissi island, and the Zakros gorge down to a Minoan palace.
  • Beaches are the priority — 4.0 against 3.8, and Lasithi's are more varied: palm-backed sand, shell beaches, and Kouremenos for windsurfing.
  • You want to fly close. Sitia has an airport; Rethymno has none at all.
  • You want the Lasithi plateau, its windmills, and the Dikteon Cave where Zeus was supposedly hidden.
  • You want real emptiness. The far east around Xerokambos and Zakros clears out completely.

Choose Rethymno if…

  • You want the best old town in Crete, and that is the honest claim: Rethymno's Venetian-Ottoman core is more intact than Chania's and far less trampled, under the huge Fortezza.
  • Culture matters — 4.5 against 3.5, a full point. Add Arkadi Monastery, site of the 1866 explosion and one of the most emotionally charged places in modern Greek memory.
  • You want the cheaper trip — 3.7 for affordability against Lasithi's 3.2, which is the most expensive corner of Crete.
  • You want to walk from your room onto a long sandy town beach, rather than drive to one.
  • You want mountain villages and gorges: the Amari valley, Spili's lion-head fountain, Argyroupoli's springs, and Preveli where a palm-lined river meets the sea.

One thing that applies to every corner of Crete: car reliance scores 5.0 here, the maximum we give. The island is 260 km long, the bus network serves the north coast and little else, and every recommendation on this page assumes you are driving.

They are not near each other

Rethymno town to Agios Nikolaos is about 145 km, and to Sitia closer to 240 km. That is two to three and a half hours, so this is a genuine either-or for a week rather than a two-base itinerary. Both are, however, easy to reach from Heraklion airport — Rethymno about 80 km west, Agios Nikolaos about 65 km east — which is the practical way most people arrive at either.

Rethymno's old town is the strongest single argument here

It is worth being direct: Rethymno has the best-preserved Renaissance-era town in Crete. The Venetian street plan survives largely intact, the Fortezza above it is one of the largest Venetian fortresses anywhere, and Ottoman additions — the Neratze minaret, wooden overhanging balconies — sit on top rather than replacing what came before. Chania's harbour is more photographed; Rethymno's town is more coherent and much less crowded.

Beyond it, this is the prefecture for inland Crete. The Amari valley below Mt Kedros is a chain of traditional villages with almost no tourism; Spili's Venetian fountain runs from twenty-five lion heads; Argyroupoli sits on Roman Lappa among waterfalls and plane trees. And Preveli, where the Megalopotamos river reaches the sea through a palm grove at the mouth of the Kourtaliotiko gorge, is the beach most people remember.

Lasithi's case is the coast and three set-pieces

Vai is the largest natural palm forest in Europe and looks like nowhere else in Greece. Spinalonga — the Venetian sea-fort that served as a leper colony until 1957 — carries a hard, recent history and has become heavily visited since Victoria Hislop's novel, so go early in the day. Chrissi, an hour by boat from Ierapetra, is a protected islet of Lebanon cedar and shell sand.

Add Kato Zakros, where a gorge walk ends at a Minoan palace on the shore, and the beaches at Voulisma, Mochlos and Xerokambos, and the 4.0 is fair.

Where each one is weakest

Lasithi is the most expensive part of Crete at 3.2, driven by the luxury cluster at Elounda, and its culture score of 3.5 is the lowest of the four Crete regions — the Minoan sites here are minor compared with Knossos or Phaistos. Rethymno has no airport at all, giving it 3.5 for access, and its beaches, while pleasant and immediately available, are the least dramatic of the four regions apart from Preveli.

The honest verdict

Rethymno if you want a town you can live in for a week, inland Crete, and lower bills — for most travellers I think that is the better base, and the 0.2 in Lasithi's favour does not capture it. Lasithi if the images that made you want to come are Vai, Spinalonga or Chrissi, or if you specifically want the emptiest end of the island. Neither is a mistake, and both are quieter and cheaper propositions than the Chania and Heraklion defaults.
