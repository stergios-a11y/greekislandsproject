Our verdict

The short answer: The two best "old town plus everything" islands in Greece, close to a tie on our board (Chania 4.8, Rhodes 4.4) that breaks on specifics. Chania has the better beaches by a distance — Balos, Elafonisi and Falasarna are why it scores 5.0 against Rhodes' 4.2 — plus the White Mountains rising over 2400m, the Samaria gorge, and a harbour town that's beautiful without being a museum. Rhodes has the greater monument: the walled medieval city is the real thing, lived-in and vast, and its season runs longer with cheaper package infrastructure. Both demand a car (5.0), both are five-day islands, both have international airports. Nightlife is a wash at 4.0 against 4.1.

Choose Chania if…

  • Swimming is the point of the trip. Balos by the morning ferry from Kissamos (return at 16:30), Elafonisi's pink-tinged sand before noon, Falasarna's 1km Pachia Ammos for the sunset — a 5.0 beach score earned three times over.
  • You want a serious walk. Samaria is the deepest gorge in Europe: 16km from Xyloskalo at 1200m, four to five hours, the Iron Gates narrowing to 3.5 metres with 300m walls, then a ferry out from Agia Roumeli.
  • You want the Venetian harbour and its 16th-century lighthouse, the leather market on Skridlof Street, the covered market, and Nea Chora town beach fifteen minutes' walk away for an evening swim.
  • You eat seriously. Chaniotiko boureki, apaki cured and smoked over herbs, sfakianopita, and four PDO cheeses — graviera, mizithra, anthotyros, malaka — all under one roof at the cheese cooperative in town.
  • You'd rather move than stay put. A Chania week runs west to Kissamos and Balos, southwest through the Topolia gorge to Elafonisi and Kedrodasos, then over the mountains to Paleochora for a night on the south coast.

Choose Rhodes if…

  • The medieval city is the reason you're going. The Street of the Knights is the best-preserved medieval street in Europe and the Palace of the Grand Master the finest Gothic architecture in Greece — both want an early-morning visit.
  • You travel in May or October. Rhodes gets more than 300 days of sunshine, the Old Town is at its best October to April, and the Medieval Rose festival takes over the walls on the second weekend of October.
  • You want beaches you drive up to rather than expeditions. Tsambika's golden sand with full facilities, Agathi beneath Feraklos castle, Glystra in the quiet south — none needs a boat or a dirt road.
  • Lindos is on your list. A 3rd-century BC acropolis with the Doric temple of Athena Lindia above a sheer 116-metre cliff, white cubist lanes below, and St Paul's Bay around the headland.
  • You want add-ons. The summer hydrofoil to Marmaris takes about an hour; Prasonisi at the southern tip, an extra 80km round trip from Glystra, is world-class for windsurfing.

The beach gap is really an access gap

Chania's 5.0 and Rhodes' 4.2 don't mean Rhodes' beaches are weak — they mean Chania's are exceptional and Rhodes' are convenient, which are different virtues. Balos is a lagoon you reach by morning ferry from Kissamos port with a fixed 16:30 return, or by dirt road and a twenty-minute hike; there's a snack bar and no sunbeds. Elafonisi is a long drive from Chania town through the Topolia gorge, and the advice is to arrive before noon if you want an umbrella. Kedrodasos, arguably the best of the three, is a further 1km on foot through protected juniper forest with no facilities whatsoever. These are day-long commitments, and in July and August you share them with tour buses.

Rhodes asks far less of you. Tsambika is a long arc of golden sand with sunbeds, water sports, tavernas, showers and parking, shallow enough to be the island's family default, with the Byzantine monastery 300 steps above it if you want a view with lunch. Anthony Quinn Bay is five minutes' walk from Ladiko, Agathi sits under a ruined castle near Haraki, and Glystra in the south is a sheltered cove with a small cafe and almost no crowd. You can swim somewhere excellent every day of a Rhodes trip without planning it the night before. If you're travelling with children, or there's only one driver, or you have a fortnight rather than five days, that convenience outweighs the 0.8-point gap.

Two old towns, two seasons — and can you do both?

Both towns are real, but they peak at different times. Rhodes runs two seasons inverted: the medieval city is at its finest from October to April, when you can walk the Street of the Knights without joining a cruise group, while the beaches and Lindos want May to October. June and September are when both halves work at once, and August turns the Old Town into a queue. Chania's Venetian harbour is a living town rather than a monument — the Old Town tavernas serve all year — and its calendar is more generous: May, early June and September are all scored perfect, with the Samaria gorge open, the sea swimmable and prices halved. Western Crete has the longest swim season in the country, May through October and sometimes either side.

Doing both in one trip is a flight, not a ferry. Neither island's getting-there notes list a direct connection between them: Chania's ferries come overnight from Piraeus to Souda, 7km east of town (€40–120), and Rhodes' come from Piraeus in 13–18 hours (€55–180). Both have international airports with summer charters, so the only sensible version routes through Athens — five days in Chania, then a hop east for five in Rhodes — and that is a fortnight, not a week. If you have seven days, pick one. These are both five-day islands by our own count and neither shrinks gracefully: cutting Chania means dropping Samaria or Elafonisi, cutting Rhodes means dropping Lindos or the south.

Chania, narrowly — the beach gap matters more often than the castle gap. Rhodes if you travel in May or October. If your trip is built around swimming, Chania wins outright; if it's built around wandering stone streets at dusk, Rhodes edges it. On history the split is clean: Rhodes for the middle ages, Chania for everything from the Minoans to the Battle of Crete, commemorated across the region each late May.

Common questions

Can I visit both Chania and Rhodes in one trip?

Only with a fortnight and a flight. Neither island's ferry page lists a direct link between them — Chania is served overnight from Piraeus into Souda, Rhodes from Piraeus in 13–18 hours — so any combination routes through Athens. Both have international airports, which makes the hop easy but still costs a day. With seven days, pick one: they're both five-day islands and neither shrinks well.

Which has better beaches, Chania or Rhodes?

Chania, 5.0 against 4.2 — but read that as quality versus convenience. Balos needs the Kissamos ferry, Elafonisi a long drive through the Topolia gorge, Kedrodasos a 1km walk through juniper forest with no facilities. Rhodes gives you Tsambika, Agathi and Anthony Quinn Bay a short drive from any east-coast base, with sunbeds and parking. Best beaches: Chania. Easiest beach days: Rhodes.

Which is better for families with young children?

Rhodes. Tsambika is the island's family default — long golden sand, exceptionally shallow water, sunbeds, tavernas, showers and parking — and Glystra in the south is a sheltered, uncrowded cove. Chania's headline beaches are day-long expeditions by boat or dirt road, which is hard work with small kids; its best family option is Nea Chora, the town beach fifteen minutes' walk from the Old Town.

Which is better in October?

Rhodes, and it isn't close. The medieval Old Town is at its best October to April, when you can walk the Street of the Knights without a cruise group in front of you, and the Medieval Rose festival fills the walls on the second weekend of the month. Chania in October is still good — mild, warm, last swims — but May and September are its stronger cards.

Do I need a car on either island?

Yes, on both — each scores 5.0 for car-need, the maximum. On Rhodes you can survive a few days car-free in the Old Town with Elli beach and Kallithea nearby, but Lindos, Monolithos and the west coast all need wheels. On Chania it's non-negotiable: Elafonisi, Falasarna and Paleochora are hours apart by mountain road, and the Samaria day depends on buses and a ferry.
