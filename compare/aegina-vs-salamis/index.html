Our verdict

The short answer: Aegina, 3.3 to 2.8. Salamina is closer — fifteen minutes from Perama against forty by hydrofoil from Piraeus — but proximity is the only category it wins. Aegina has the Temple of Aphaia, one of the best-preserved Doric temples anywhere in Greece, a real market town, the PDO pistachio harvest, and fish tavernas at Perdika worth a trip on their own. Neither island is here for its beaches: Aegina scores 2.5, Salamina 2.0. Pick Aegina unless the fifteen minutes genuinely decide it.

Choose Aegina if…

  • You want a serious archaeological site. The Temple of Aphaia (around 500 BC) is largely intact, spectacularly sited, and comfortably the best ancient building in the Saronic.
  • You want lunch to matter. Perdika's waterfront fish tavernas are the reason many Athenians come at all.
  • You want a working market town rather than a suburb — 13,000 people, a proper harbour front, the pistachio trade, and the Fistiki Fest in September.
  • You want the easiest logistics of any Greek island: hydrofoils from Piraeus roughly hourly, 40 minutes, no planning required. Access scores 5.0.
  • You want somewhere that works as a full day or an easy overnight.

Choose Salamina if…

  • You want the cheapest, fastest crossing in Greece — 15 minutes from Perama, running constantly.
  • You are going for the Battle of Salamis specifically, and want to see the strait where it was fought.
  • You want Faneromeni Monastery and its 17th-century frescoes, with almost nobody else there.
  • You want the cheaper island: 4.5 for affordability against Aegina's 3.5, which is a real gap on food and rooms.
  • You are interested in an unpolished, working island of 39,000 people that tourism simply passed by.

Neither is a beach island

This is the honest frame for the whole comparison. Aegina scores 2.5 on beaches and Salamina 2.0, and both numbers are deserved. Aegina's best swimming is at Agia Marina (sandy, shallow, family-friendly, busy) and Marathonas, with Klima and a few small coves for the determined. It is fine. It is not why you go. Salamina's east and north coasts face shipyards and the shipping channel into Piraeus; the decent swimming is at Kanakia and Peristeria on the far west, and you need a car to reach them.

If your day depends on water, neither of these is the right answer in the Saronic — Agistri scores 3.5 and Hydra has better character, and both are within the same hour from Athens.

What Aegina has that Salamina does not

The Temple of Aphaia is the difference. It sits on a pine ridge in the northeast with most of its columns standing, it predates the Parthenon, and it is the sort of place you can have almost to yourself on a weekday morning. Add the single surviving column of the Temple of Apollo at Kolona by the port, a good archaeological museum, and a town that has been continuously important since antiquity — Aegina was briefly the first capital of modern Greece — and the culture score of 4.2 against Salamina's 3.5 is fair.

Then there is the food. Perdika, on the southwest tip, is a row of fish tavernas over the water and one of the better lunch destinations near Athens. The pistachios are a PDO product and genuinely different from the supermarket kind — buy them from a roadside stall, not a shop.

The case for Salamina anyway

Two things. The crossing is trivially short and cheap, which matters if you have half a day rather than a full one. And the history is specific and located: this is where the Greek fleet destroyed a much larger Persian one in 480 BC, and the strait is right there. For anyone who has read about the Persian Wars, standing on that shore is worth more than another temple.

It is also markedly cheaper — 4.5 against 3.5 on affordability, which shows up in taverna prices and the near-absence of tourist mark-up.

The honest verdict

Aegina, for almost everyone. It is a better island on every axis except cost and crossing time: better ancient site, better food, better town, easier ferries. Salamina earns its place only on a specific brief — half a day, an interest in the naval battle, no swimming planned, minimum spend. Choose it deliberately for that, and it delivers. Choose it by accident because it looked closest on a map, and you will spend the afternoon wondering where the island went.
