Skopelos vs Skyros

Our verdict

The short answer: Skopelos, 3.6 to 3.4 — close, and closer than the numbers suggest, because they win in different places. Skopelos is the greenest of the Sporades, has the better beaches (4.2 against 4.0) and the prettier town, and carries the Mamma Mia! association for better or worse. Skyros is cheaper (4.2 against 3.8), stronger on culture (3.8 against 3.2), and has a whitewashed chora that looks Cycladic rather than Sporadic. Critically, they are not on the same ferry route: Skopelos from Volos or Agios Konstantinos, Skyros from Kymi in Evia.

Choose Skopelos if…

  • You want pine forest to the sea. Skopelos is the most heavily forested island in the Aegean and it changes the light, the smell and the temperature of the whole trip.
  • Beaches matter more than villages — 4.2 against 4.0. Panormos, Milia, Kastani and Stafylos are pebble and pine, with genuinely clear water.
  • You want the film. The chapel of Agios Ioannis Kastri on its rock, and Kastani beach, are the Mamma Mia! locations, and people do come for exactly that.
  • You want the better town to walk. Skopelos Town is an amphitheatre of white houses under grey slate roofs, and it is one of the loveliest harbour towns in Greece.
  • You want to hike. Skopelos has real forest trails, a claimed 360 churches, and the monasteries above the town make an easy morning.

Choose Skyros if…

  • You want culture and craft. 3.8 against 3.2: the Faltaits Museum, the castle and monastery above the chora, and the Bronze Age site at Palamari.
  • You want the cheaper trip — 4.2 for affordability against 3.8, which shows in rooms more than food.
  • You want the carnival. The Skyros Geros, masked in goat skin and hung with bells, is one of the most striking pre-Lenten rites in Greece.
  • You want a landscape with two halves: green farmed north, bare mountain south, and the wild Skyrian ponies in between.
  • You want a chora rather than a harbour — Skyros Town sits high and inland-facing, which is unusual for the group.

Green against bare

This is the real distinction. Skopelos is 96 km² and something like two-thirds forested — Aleppo pine down to the waterline on almost every bay, which is why the beaches feel cool and the water reads deep green. Panormos and Milia in the west are the classic pair; Kastani is the one from the film; Velanio below Stafylos is the quiet option and unofficially clothing-optional. Glossa in the north, above the port at Loutraki, is the second town and the better one for a slow evening.

Skyros is bigger — 209 km² — and split. The northern half is farmed and wooded and where almost everyone lives; the southern half is Mount Kochylas, dry rock and low scrub, roamed by the endemic ponies and reached on a rough road. Magazia and Molos below the chora are long and sandy; Agios Fokas and Atsitsa on the west are remote and lovely. 4.0 is fair.

Town against chora

Both are attractive and they are not the same idea. Skopelos Town wraps around its harbour in tiers, with slate-roofed houses, churches on every corner and tavernas along the water — it is a harbour town and it behaves like one, busy at the front and quiet three streets back.

Skyros Town does not face a harbour at all. It climbs a rock inland with the Byzantine castle and the monastery of Saint George at the top, and the houses are cubic and flat-roofed in a way that genuinely surprises people who thought they were going to the Sporades. Inside, the Skyrian tradition of crowding walls with carved furniture, embroidery and imported ceramic plates is still alive, and the Faltaits Museum shows it properly.

Practicalities that decide it

Both score 2.5 for access, but for different reasons and by different routes. Skopelos has no airport: you fly to Skiathos and take a 40-minute boat, or ferry from Volos, Agios Konstantinos or Mantoudi. Skyros has a small airport with flights from Athens, and its ferry leaves from Kymi in Evia — around three and a half hours' drive from Athens, then two hours at sea.

The consequence: if you want to combine islands, Skopelos slots naturally with Skiathos and Alonnisos, and Skyros does not slot with anything. Plan it as a standalone.

The honest verdict

Skopelos for a summer holiday — greener, better beaches, an easier connection via Skiathos, and a town that earns the walk. Skyros if you want somewhere genuinely less visited, with a stronger cultural core, lower prices and a landscape that changes as you drive south; and emphatically Skyros if you can travel at carnival. The 0.2 between them is not the point. The point is that one is a pine-and-swimming island and the other is a village-and-tradition island, and they happen to share an administrative label.
