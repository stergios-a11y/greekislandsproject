Our verdict

The short answer: Mykonos and Naxos are neighbours geographically but worlds apart in character. Mykonos is the Cyclades polished for international consumption; Naxos is the Cyclades as Greeks themselves enjoy them. Mykonos's strengths are its beach clubs, its restaurants and its meticulously maintained tourist infrastructure — it earns its 5.0 for nightlife. Its weaknesses are its prices (1.0 affordability, as low as the scale goes) and the relentless intensity of high-season crowds. Naxos is the largest Cycladic island, with a long western beach coastline, a Venetian-walled old town, mountain villages still working their olive groves and vineyards, and prices (4.0) that allow normal travellers to stay a full week without remortgaging. Overall: Naxos 4.5, Mykonos 4.3 — closer than the price gap suggests, because Mykonos is very good at the one thing it does.

Choose Mykonos if…

  • You're going for the scene, and you know it. Scorpios on Paraga at sunset, the Matogianni bars waking up around midnight, Cavo Paradiso above Paradise Beach not filling until 2am — nothing on Naxos comes close.
  • You want an international airport. Mykonos has direct flights from across Europe on Aegean, EasyJet, Ryanair and charters; Naxos has a small domestic airport, so from abroad you connect through Athens.
  • Delos matters to you. The boat leaves the Old Port at 09:00, 10:00 and 11:00 (€25, plus €20 site entry) for one of the most important archaeological sites in Greece — the Lions, the Hellenistic mosaics, the Sacred Way.
  • You're going in October. The scene is winding down but the weather is still warm and prices halve — the one month Mykonos is both affordable and still open.
  • You have three days, not a week. Mykonos is a 3-day island (85 km²): Chora and the windmills, Delos, one proper beach day at Kalo Livadi, and you've done it well.

Choose Naxos if…

  • You're travelling with children. Plaka is 5 km of fine white sand with water so shallow a four-year-old walks out thirty metres; Agios Prokopios is 10 minutes from town with the best facilities on the coast.
  • You want to eat what the island grows: PDO graviera, Kitron liqueur from the Vallindras distillery in Halki, PGI potatoes, and patatato goat stew in the Apeiranthos and Filoti tavernas.
  • You want to walk. Mount Zas is the highest peak in the Cyclades at 1,001 m — a three-hour return hike past the Cave of Zeus — and the marble villages of the interior link up by road.
  • You're on a normal budget. Naxos scores 4.0 for affordability against Mykonos's 1.0; on Psarou a pair of Nammos sunbeds starts at €100, on Plaka the tamarisk shade is free.
  • You want a week, or a base for hopping. Naxos is a 4-day island by itself, Paros is 30 minutes away, and it has daily connections to most of the Cyclades.

The price gap is the real difference

Strip away the reputations and this comparison is decided by money. Mykonos scores 1.0 for affordability — as low as the scale goes, level with Santorini — and the guide's own numbers explain why: Nammos sunbeds from €100 a pair and into four figures for the front row, €15 for the ten-minute taxi from Chora to Psarou, and a beach-club culture where the DJ starts at lunch. The August advice is "book months ahead". None of this is a scandal; it's what Mykonos is for. But it means the island only makes sense if the scene is the point — pay Mykonos prices for a quiet week and you've bought the wrong product.

Naxos scores 4.0, and the difference shows in how a day is built. Plaka and Kastraki have natural tamarisk shade and camping in the dunes; the market street below the Kastro sells graviera, thyme honey and Kitron for a beach picnic; the mountain tavernas in Kinidaros and Apeiranthos grill meat by the kilo with village wine. The owner's line stands: normal travellers can stay a full week without remortgaging. And Naxos is not the poorer cousin on quality — its beaches score 4.8 to Mykonos's 4.3, its culture 4.4 to 3.0, and Plaka is, on this site's own reckoning, the finest beach in the Cyclades.

Doing both — and which one in September

Combining them is natural: they're neighbours, both are served from Rafina and Piraeus, and Naxos has daily connections to most of the Cyclades. The clean shape is Mykonos first, for two or three nights while your budget and patience are fresh, then Naxos for four or five to decompress — Chora and the Portara, one west-coast beach day, one mountain day through Halki and Apeiranthos, one for the Kouros of Apollonas. Fly into Mykonos's international airport; leave Naxos by domestic flight through Athens or by fast ferry to Piraeus in about 3.5 hours. Doing it the other way round means ending on the expensive island, tired, which is the wrong order.

If you only have one and the month is September, the choice sharpens. September is Mykonos's sweet spot — the scene still in full swing, crowds easing in the second half, water at its warmest, prices starting to drop — and many regulars call it the actual best time. September on Naxos is "perfect" too: warm, the harvest month, the western beaches emptying out after August. So the month doesn't decide it; the purpose does. If you'd resent paying Mykonos prices for a quieter version of Mykonos, you're a Naxos person. If the point is Scorpios at sunset with the summer still on, September is the smartest month to buy Mykonos.

Choose Mykonos if you specifically want the curated party-luxury experience — and go in June or September rather than August, when the crowds are the experience. Choose Naxos for almost any other Cycladic priority: families, week-long stays, food culture, hiking, or simply spending less. Naxos is also a far better base if you plan to ferry-hop, with daily connections to most of the Cyclades.

Common questions

Can I do Mykonos and Naxos in one trip?

Yes, and it's a natural pairing — they're neighbours, both are served from Rafina and Piraeus, and Naxos has daily connections to most of the Cyclades. Do Mykonos first for two or three nights, then four or five on Naxos to decompress and spend less. Fly into Mykonos's international airport; leave Naxos by domestic flight via Athens or by fast ferry to Piraeus in about 3.5 hours.

Is Naxos cheaper than Mykonos?

By a wide margin. Naxos scores 4.0 for affordability, Mykonos 1.0 — as low as the scale goes. On Mykonos a pair of Nammos sunbeds starts at €100 and the ten-minute taxi to Psarou is €15; on Naxos the shade at Plaka is tamarisk trees and camping in the dunes is permitted. The only month Mykonos prices drop sharply is October, when they halve as the season closes.

Which is better for families, Mykonos or Naxos?

Naxos, and it isn't close. Plaka, Agios Prokopios and Kastraki are long stretches of shallow, calm white sand where small children can wade out thirty metres; Agios Georgios is the town beach for families staying in Chora. Mykonos's family option is Kalo Livadi — 800 m of golden sand with shallow water — but it's an island built around beach clubs and midnight bars, not children.

Which has better nightlife?

Mykonos, decisively: 5.0 against Naxos's 3.5. Scorpios on Paraga, the Matogianni bar cluster, and Cavo Paradiso, the cliff superclub above Paradise Beach that doesn't fill until 2am. Naxos does have a real evening — the waterfront Paralia is one long bar-hop, with 520 and Swing for cocktails and Ocean Club and Galle for DJs till morning — but nobody flies to Naxos for the night.

Which is better in September?

September is a 'perfect' month on both. On Mykonos the scene is still in full swing, crowds ease in the second half, the sea is at its warmest and prices start to drop — regulars call it the best time. On Naxos it's warm, the harvest month, with the western beaches emptying. Decide by purpose: if the point is the scene, September is the smartest month for Mykonos; for anything else, Naxos.
