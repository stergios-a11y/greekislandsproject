Our verdict

The short answer: This is the battle of the two most photographed beaches in Greece: Zakynthos's Navagio versus Lefkada's Porto Katsiki. Both islands score within a tenth for beaches (4.8 against 4.9); the real difference is what surrounds them. Zakynthos is the bigger tourism product — Laganas's party strip (4.5 nightlife), turtle-spotting in the marine park, the Blue Caves, and a south geared to package holidays, with prices to match (3.5 affordability) and some areas best avoided in August. Lefkada is quieter, cheaper to reach (drive across the bridge — no flights needed), windier, and more Greek in feel; its nightlife is a beach bar, not a strip (3.2). Both need a car (4.0) to reach their famous west coasts, both want four days, and both are at their best in June and September.

Choose Zakynthos if…

  • You want the shipwreck photo. Navagio — the rusting Panagiotis, stranded in 1980, on white sand below 200-metre cliffs — is boat-only from Porto Vromi or Agios Nikolaos, twenty minutes; go before noon, or at sunrise in July.
  • Nightlife is the plan. The Laganas Strip is a kilometre of clubs — Rescue with 2,000 capacity, thirty-year-old Cherry Bay, foam parties at Zeros — and nothing starts before midnight. Agios Markos Square in town is the grown-up option.
  • You're travelling with young children. Banana Beach at Agios Nikolaos is a kilometre of shallow, fully organised sand; Tsilivi and Alykes are calm and family-friendly; Laganas itself is nine kilometres of very gradual water.
  • You want wildlife with the swimming. Laganas Bay is the Mediterranean's main loggerhead nesting ground; boat trips from Laganas find turtles in the bay from May to October, and Gerakas closes at sunset so they can nest.
  • You're flying in from northern Europe. Zakynthos has a heavy summer charter airport — Aegean, Sky Express, Ryanair from London and Manchester — and no Piraeus ferry to think about.

Choose Lefkada if…

  • You want the same calibre of beach with the crowds thinned by effort. Egremni is 1.5 kilometres of white pebble under sheer cliffs, reached by 350 steps or a twenty-minute boat from Vassiliki, and almost empty as a result.
  • You're driving. Lefkada is the only Greek island you reach by road — about four hours from Athens via Rio-Antirrio and over the floating bridge — and Aktion airport is thirty minutes north for those who fly.
  • Sailing or windsurfing is the point. Nydri's harbour fills with sailing crews every evening, day boats circle Skorpios and cross to Meganisi, and Vassiliki's afternoon thermal draws windsurfers and kitesurfers from across Europe.
  • You'd rather eat in a village than a resort. Agios Nikitas's car-free harbour has the fish tavernas; Athani, on the cliff above Porto Katsiki, does hillside lamb and local wine; Karya's tavernas make sofigado with Vertzami.
  • Budget matters. Lefkada scores 4.0 for affordability against 3.5, there is no ferry ticket or flight in the price, and Kathisma — a kilometre of sand with beach bars and parking — spares you Porto Katsiki's pricey sunbeds.

Nightlife is the real difference

The beach scores are a coin toss, so look at the nights. Zakynthos earns 4.5 because Laganas is a purpose-built strip: Rescue, Cherry Bay, Zeros, foam, a 2,000-person dance floor, and a crowd the guide describes in August as "loud, drunk, young". If that is what you want, no other Ionian island delivers it. The catch is that the same strip is what to avoid if it isn't — the guide's advice is to base yourself in the north or on the Vassilikos peninsula, and to treat Zakynthos Town, with the Agios Markos bars and kantades sung up on Bochali hill, as the adult version.

Lefkada's 3.2 is honest. Nydri has a promenade of bars with happy hours and live music — the Street Bar for cocktails, Excess for house and techno from June to September — and Lefkada Town, twenty minutes away, has the classier rooms: Karma, Baccara, the two-floor Havana Club. That is the whole scene, and most evenings end at a taverna watching yachts come in. Groups of twenty-somethings will find Lefkada quiet; couples and families will find Zakynthos's south loud. Decide this first, and the rest of the comparison mostly follows.

Getting there, and whether you can do both

Access is where the islands genuinely diverge, and it is why Lefkada scores 4.5 to Zakynthos's 3.7. Lefkada is a drive: roughly four hours from Athens via Rio-Antirrio, across a floating bridge, no ferry, no booking. Aktion (PVK) is thirty minutes north with summer charters from London, Manchester and German cities. Zakynthos has the bigger charter airport and Ryanair, but by land it means Kyllini in the Peloponnese — about three hours from Athens — and an hour's crossing at €10–14. There is no Piraeus ferry to either.

Combining them is a road trip rather than an island hop. Our guides list no direct ferry between the two: you leave Lefkada over its bridge, drive south, and cross from Kyllini. With a car and eight days it works — four days each, which is exactly what each island asks for — but it is a driving holiday with two beach halves, not a hop. With a single week, pick one. The August warning applies to both: Porto Katsiki, Egremni and Kathisma are unreachable by car after 10am, and Laganas is at its peak; June and September are "perfect" on both islands.

Choose Zakynthos for the full summer-holiday package — young groups, boat trips, big-resort convenience, and that shipwreck photo. Choose Lefkada for the same calibre of swimming with fewer crowds, lower costs, and the sailing islets next door. Honeymooners actually wanting quiet: Lefkada, clearly.

Common questions

Can I do Lefkada and Zakynthos in one trip, and how do I get between them?

Only as a road trip. Our guides list no direct ferry between the two: Lefkada is reached over its floating bridge from the mainland, Zakynthos by a one-hour crossing from Kyllini in the Peloponnese (€10–14) or by air. So you drive off Lefkada, head south, and cross from Kyllini. Each island asks for four days, which makes it an eight-day driving holiday with a car throughout. With one week, pick one.

Which is cheaper, Lefkada or Zakynthos?

Lefkada. It scores 4.0 for affordability against Zakynthos's 3.5, and getting there is a drive — about four hours from Athens via Rio-Antirrio — with no ferry ticket or flight to add. Zakynthos prices to a package-holiday market, especially around Laganas, and even by land it costs a Kyllini crossing. On Lefkada the one extra is Porto Katsiki's pricey sunbeds; Kathisma and Egremni cost you steps, not money.

Which is better for families?

Zakynthos for young children, Lefkada for older ones. Zakynthos has the shallow, fully organised beaches — Banana at Agios Nikolaos, Tsilivi, Alykes, and nine kilometres of gradual water at Laganas — plus turtle boat trips from May to October. Lefkada's famous beaches mean steps: 100 at Porto Katsiki, 350 at Egremni. Its family beach is Kathisma, a kilometre of sand with beach bars and parking; Nydri's promenade fills with families after dark.

Which has the better nightlife?

Zakynthos, and it is not close: 4.5 against 3.2. The Laganas Strip is a kilometre of clubs — Rescue with a 2,000 capacity, Cherry Bay, foam parties at Zeros — with nothing starting before midnight; Zakynthos Town's Agios Markos Square is the calmer local option. Lefkada's scene is Nydri's bar promenade, the Excess club from June to September, and a few smarter bars in Lefkada Town. Most Lefkada nights end at a taverna.

Which is better in September?

Both are rated 'perfect' in September, but pick Lefkada. The crowds ease fast, Porto Katsiki returns to a sensible visit after the August lockout of the west-coast car parks, and the guide calls the second half of the month the loveliest of the Lefkadian summer. Zakynthos in September is also strong — sea at its peak, Laganas calming, turtles still nesting — so it is the right answer if the shipwreck or the nightlife is the reason you're going.
