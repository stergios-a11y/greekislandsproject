Our verdict

The short answer: Kimolos is what Milos was twenty years ago — literally next door, a 30-minute ferry from Pollonia, and two decades behind in development. That's the whole comparison, and it cuts both ways. Milos (4.7) is the event: Sarakiniko, Kleftiko, the boat trip, seventy beaches, restaurants that book out, an airport, and now the crowds and prices (2.8 affordability) that come with fame. Kimolos (3.6) is the escape hatch: Prassa's white sand and turquoise shallows, the skeleton of its medieval Kastro village, goats on the road, a handful of tavernas where the owner cooks. Its beaches score 4.5 — barely behind Milos's 5.0 — but you'll often have them to yourself. The trade: fewer rooms, fewer ferries, nothing to "do" after dinner (2.5 nightlife against Milos's 3.0).

Choose Milos if…

  • It's your first visit and you want the set pieces: Sarakiniko at sunrise, the Kleftiko boat from Adamas (about 1.5 hours out, book the night before), Firiplaka's coloured cliffs, Klima's painted syrmata at golden hour.
  • You want to fly. Olympic Air runs two to three flights a day from Athens in summer, 40 minutes, typically €60–120 one way — Kimolos has no airport, and its direct Piraeus ferries take 5–9 hours.
  • You have four days or more. Milos is a 4-day island by this site's count — the north coast, the boat day, the south coast, the catacombs — and 151 km² needs a car or ATV (car need 4.0) to cover.
  • You want dinner to be a choice, not a default: Enalion and Gialos in Pollonia, Medusa on the Mandrakia cliff, Archontoula in Plaka, and Akri's sundowner jutting over Adamas bay afterwards.
  • You want to keep moving afterwards. Milos has a seasonal direct fast ferry to Santorini in about 2 hours; from Kimolos the only quick way anywhere is back through Pollonia.

Choose Kimolos if…

  • You've done Milos, or you'd rather have Prassa than Sarakiniko. Prassa is 200 m of chalk-white sand in a lagoon as calm as a pool, sheltered by the Prasonisi islet, with thermal springs bubbling through the sand.
  • Peace matters more than choice. Chorio holds 90% of the island's 800 residents inside a 16th-century Venetian kastro; the bus from Psathi meets every ferry and the whole village is a walk.
  • You'd snorkel over a sunken city. Ellinika, on the west coast, sits above the submerged foundations of an ancient town — house walls and tombs a few metres from shore, and almost always empty.
  • You want to walk rather than drive: 30 minutes on an old mule track to the Skiadi mushroom rock, on to Ellinika and Mavrospilia, then Bonatsa's tavernas and tamarisk shade for the afternoon.
  • You're budgeting. Kimolos scores 3.8 for affordability against Milos's 2.8, the Pollonia shuttle costs €3–5 and takes cars, and a June or September week here stays in the "near-empty" bracket.

The 30-minute shuttle changes the question

This is the rare comparison where "both" is not a compromise but the obvious answer, because the ferry between them is a shuttle, not a voyage. From Pollonia on Milos's north-east corner the boat to Psathi takes 30 minutes, runs multiple times daily, takes cars, and costs €3–5 — the guide calls it one of the best beach-hopping links in the Cyclades. So the practical question isn't Kimolos or Milos; it's where you sleep. Base in Pollonia — Enalion for dinner, an early start — and Kimolos becomes a day: shuttle over, bus up to Chorio, drive the 9 km of road-then-gravel to Prassa, To Kyma at Psathi before the boat back.

The split week is the other shape: five nights on Milos, two on Kimolos, ideally at the end. Milos takes its four days — Sarakiniko and the north, the Kleftiko boat, Firiplaka and Paleochori, the catacombs — and then you cross with the car for the Skiadi walk, the Polyaigos boat from Psathi and a last swim at Bonatsa. The one thing not to do is arrive on Kimolos direct from Piraeus: those sailings exist but take 5–9 hours, against 3–4 by fast ferry to Adamas or 40 minutes by air. Come via Milos, always — and outside June to September check the schedule, because Kimolos's own calendar lists "few ferries" for the winter months.

Beaches: 5.0 against 4.5, and why the gap feels smaller

On paper Milos wins, and it should: seventy-plus coves, Sarakiniko's white rock, Kleftiko's caves, Firiplaka's 600 m of golden sand under coloured cliffs, Paleochori with thermal vents warming the sand. But several of those aren't beaches in the towel-and-swim sense — Sarakiniko is 300 m of rock with no sunbeds, Kleftiko is boat-only, Tsigrado is a rope ladder through a crack in the cliff. Milos's beaches are an itinerary. In July the famous ones are busy by mid-morning, and in August Sarakiniko is packed by 9am, the Tsigrado queue is twenty minutes and prices double.

Kimolos's 4.5 is a different kind of score: fewer places, all of them simple. Prassa is the headline — white sand from the chalk deposits, lagoon-calm, one small bar in summer. Bonatsa on the south coast is 300 m of shallow sand with tamarisk shade and tavernas behind, sheltered from the meltemi and easy to reach. Ellinika and Mavrospilia are a hike or a rough road. The gap in quality is real; the gap in experience runs the other way, because on Kimolos you'll often have the sand to yourself. One caveat from the guide: in July and August water can run short on the smaller beaches, so bring your own.

Choose Milos for the full spectacle — first visit, photography, boat day. Choose Kimolos if you've done Milos, or if peace matters more than choice. Smartest of all: base in Milos's Pollonia and day-trip to Kimolos, or split the week 5–2 — the 30-minute shuttle makes either one trivial.

Common questions

Can I do Kimolos and Milos in one trip?

Yes — this is the easiest pairing in the Cyclades. The shuttle from Pollonia on Milos's north-east coast to Psathi on Kimolos takes 30 minutes, runs multiple times daily, takes cars and costs €3–5. Either base yourself in Pollonia and do Kimolos as a day (Chorio, Prassa, lunch at To Kyma), or split the week five nights on Milos and two on Kimolos, with Kimolos at the end when you want the quiet.

Is Kimolos cheaper than Milos?

Yes. Kimolos scores 3.8 for affordability, Milos 2.8, and the gap widens in August, when Milos's own guide says prices double. Getting there costs almost nothing on top of Milos — €3–5 for the Pollonia shuttle — and Kimolos's tavernas are the kind where the owner cooks. The one thing that isn't cheaper is time: Kimolos has no airport, so you still pay for the flight or the 3–4 hour fast ferry to Adamas.

How do I get from Milos to Kimolos?

From Pollonia, the fishing village on Milos's north-east corner about 15 minutes' drive from Adamas. The boat to Psathi, Kimolos's port, takes 30 minutes, runs several times a day and carries cars, for €3–5. At Psathi the bus to Chorio meets every ferry, and it's 1 km up the hill. Don't take the direct Piraeus–Kimolos ferry unless you have to — it takes 5–9 hours against 30 minutes from Pollonia.

Which is better for a first visit to the western Cyclades?

Milos. The set pieces — Sarakiniko, the Kleftiko boat trip from Adamas, Firiplaka, Klima's painted boathouses, the Christian catacombs — are the reason people come, and they need the four days the site gives Milos. Kimolos is a second-visit island: you appreciate Prassa, Chorio's Venetian kastro and the sunken city at Ellinika most when you know what the crowds on the other side of the strait look like.

Which is better in August?

Kimolos, if you must travel in August. Milos's calendar marks it 'avoid' — Sarakiniko packed by 9am, a twenty-minute queue for the Tsigrado ladder, Kleftiko boats fully booked, prices doubled. Kimolos is 'ok': busier than usual because Greeks visit, and water can run short on the smaller beaches, but nothing like the chaos across the water. The real answer for both is June or September, which both guides rate 'perfect'.
