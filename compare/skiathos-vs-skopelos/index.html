Our verdict

The short answer: Skiathos and Skopelos sit forty minutes apart by ferry and split the Sporades experience between them. Skiathos is the extrovert: an airport, sixty-plus sandy beaches on a tiny island twelve kilometres long, and a party town. Skopelos is the introvert: greener, quieter, no airport, and prettier in the ways that don't photograph from a beach bar. Skiathos earns its 4.7 nightlife score honestly — the Old Port fills up every July night — and Koukounaries and Lalaria are genuinely top-tier beaches (4.6). The price is crowds and a package-holiday feel in August, and an affordability score of 3.0 against Skopelos's 3.8. Skopelos trades all that for pine forests that reach the water, a chora of 123 churches, the Mamma Mia chapel of Agios Ioannis, and evenings that end with dinner rather than a DJ.

Choose Skiathos if…

  • You want to fly straight in. Skiathos has an international airport with summer charters from Europe; the runway ends beside the town port, and planes land metres above the waterfront. Skopelos means a ferry on top.
  • Beaches decide your holidays. Koukounaries is 1.2 kilometres of fine golden sand behind umbrella pines with a protected lagoon; Big and Little Banana sit next door; Agia Eleni on the north coast is reached on foot through the forest.
  • You want the boat day. The full-day tour from the Old Port is the only way to Lalaria — white oval pebbles under limestone cliffs, a natural arch — plus the Blue Cave and the abandoned Kastro on its cliff.
  • Nights matter. Start on the Old Port steps at Rock 'n' Roll Bar, move to Kentavros for blues and cocktails, and finish on Bar Street (Polytechniou) — Spartacus, Admiral Benbow — which runs late. Nightlife 4.7, top of the Sporades.
  • You're going in June or September and want a town with a pulse without the August crush — charters starting or winding down, Koukounaries breathable, the Old Port lively but not suffocating.

Choose Skopelos if…

  • You want the prettier town. Skopelos Town climbs a hillside above a Venetian harbour with 123 churches, a kastro at the top, and dinners under the pomegranate tree at To Rodi or at Kyratso's Kitchen by the old port.
  • You'd rather walk than queue. Limnonari, one of the island's few sandy beaches, is a twenty-minute forest path or a boat away; Milia, north of Panormos, has no facilities at all and dense pine behind it.
  • The film matters — or the view. Agios Ioannis chapel sits on a sheer rock on the northwest coast, 112 steps up; go early, before the Mamma Mia buses. Glossa's square, with Agnanti since 1953, is the coffee stop after.
  • You want the greenest island in the Aegean. Pine, olive and plum orchards cover nearly all of it; September is "perfect" for the plums, and the Evangelistria monastery sits in forest above the town.
  • You're staying more than three nights and want your money to go further. Affordability 3.8 against 3.0, Panormos with a taverna on the beach, Stafylos four kilometres from town — a week here moves at half speed and costs accordingly.

Nightlife and the August question

The scores that decide this pair are nightlife — 4.7 against 2.5 — and access, 4.5 against 2.5. Skiathos Town packs a real scene into a small grid: the Old Port bars, Bar Street running late, and in July, as the guide puts it, "loud till dawn". Skopelos has no equivalent. Its evenings are Kyratso's at the old port, To Rodi in the Hora, tavernas screening Mamma Mia and running tribute nights through the summer, and a walk up through the lanes afterwards. If that sounds like a weakness, choose Skiathos. If it sounds like the point, you already know.

August splits them differently. Skiathos in August is "great" but crowded — every beach full by mid-morning, Old Port chaos nightly, prices at maximum, and on an island twelve kilometres long the crowds are felt everywhere; the guide's advice is to book everything weeks ahead. Skopelos in August is rated "avoid": peak crowds, hot, and boatloads of visitors. July on Skopelos is only "ok" because of the bus tours to the chapel. So the shoulder is where Skopelos wins outright — June before the buses, September for the plums — while Skiathos holds up better under peak-season pressure simply because it was built for it.

Doing both: fly to Skiathos, ferry across

This is the pair where doing both is not a compromise but the classic play. Skiathos has the airport; Skopelos is reached via Skiathos on the same Sporades line, so the crossing is on your way regardless. From the mainland it is Volos or Agios Konstantinos, about three hours' drive from Athens, then two to three hours' ferry to Skiathos (€20–35) and 3.5 to 5 hours in total to Skopelos. There is no Piraeus ferry to either. Leaving Skopelos, boats run to Skiathos, Alonnisos, Volos and Thessaloniki, so the loop closes either way.

Fly to Skiathos, spend three nights — the town, Koukounaries, the boat day to Lalaria — then cross the forty minutes to Skopelos for four: the town and Stafylos, Agios Ioannis and Panormos, the monastery and Limnonari before the ferry back. Skiathos first, because arriving on Skopelos from Skiathos feels like exhaling, while the reverse feels like being woken up. Both guides run to three days, but Skopelos is the one that rewards a fourth, and its car-need score (4.0 against 3.0) says rent there rather than on Skiathos, where the boat and the south-coast road cover most of what you want.

Choose Skiathos for beach variety, nightlife, and the convenience of flying straight in. Choose Skopelos for atmosphere, walking, and a week that moves at half speed. The classic play is both: fly to Skiathos, spend three nights, then cross to Skopelos for four.

Common questions

How do I get from Skiathos to Skopelos, and can I do both in one trip?

Yes, and it is the classic Sporades plan. Skopelos is reached via Skiathos on the same ferry line, and the crossing takes about forty minutes. Fly into Skiathos (international airport with summer charters), spend three nights, then cross to Skopelos for four; from Skopelos, ferries also run to Alonnisos, Volos and Thessaloniki. From Athens by land it is about three hours' drive to Volos or Agios Konstantinos, then 2–3 hours to Skiathos and 3.5–5 hours in total to Skopelos.

Which is cheaper, Skiathos or Skopelos?

Skopelos, clearly: affordability 3.8 against Skiathos's 3.0. Skiathos prices for a charter market and hits maximum in August, when the guide says to book everything weeks ahead. Skopelos has no airport and no strip, and its pleasures are cheap ones — Panormos with a beach taverna, Stafylos four kilometres from town, the forest path to Limnonari. Ferry fares from the mainland are the same €20–35 band for both, so the difference is what you spend once you land.

Which is better for families?

Skiathos for young children. Koukounaries is 1.2 kilometres of fine golden sand with a very gradual, safe-for-children slope, natural pine shade and full facilities; Big Banana next door is organised and shallow. Skopelos's beaches are mostly pebble — Panormos, Stafylos — and its sandy ones, Limnonari and Milia, need a forest path or a boat. Families who want quiet evenings and a pretty town rather than beach clubs will be happier on Skopelos, but pack the water shoes.

Which has the better nightlife?

Skiathos, by the widest margin in the Sporades: 4.7 against 2.5. Skiathos Town runs from Rock 'n' Roll Bar on the Old Port steps to Kentavros for blues and cocktails to Bar Street (Polytechniou), which goes late — 'loud till dawn' in July. Skopelos's evenings are dinner at Kyratso's Kitchen or To Rodi, tavernas screening Mamma Mia, and a walk through the lanes. If nightlife is on your list at all, that settles it.

Which is better in September?

Both are rated 'perfect', but pick Skopelos. September is the plum month — the island's signature crop — the bus tours to Agios Ioannis have thinned, the sea is still warm, and the town is at its best without the August boatloads. Skiathos in September is also excellent: charters winding down, Koukounaries breathing again, the Old Port lively but no longer suffocating. Go there instead only if the bars matter more than the forest.
