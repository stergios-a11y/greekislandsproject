Our verdict

The short answer: Kefalonia for substantive Ionian travel — dramatic landscapes, the Myrtos coastline, mountains, and a road-trip mindset. Zakynthos for Navagio (Shipwreck Beach), high-energy southern resorts, and easier package holidays. The overall scores are identical (4.1 vs 4.1), but the islands solve very different problems: Kefalonia rewards travelers who want to discover an island, Zakynthos rewards travelers who want a defined, social, sun-and-beach week. Both score top-tier on beaches (4.7 vs 4.8 — essentially a tie), and the right choice has more to do with vibe than with quality.

Choose Kefalonia if…

  • You want one of the most dramatic coastlines in Greece. Myrtos Beach with its white cliffs is the iconic image, but the entire west coast follows this pattern.
  • You want a real road trip. Kefalonia is huge (773 km², the largest Ionian island) with pine-forested mountains, Mt. Ainos national park, and serious driving culture.
  • You care about wine. The indigenous Robola grape produces serious whites that don't exist elsewhere in Greece.
  • You're traveling as a couple or with active friends. Hiking Mt. Ainos, the Drogarati and Melissani caves, and exploring tiny villages reward this kind of trip.
  • You want quieter evenings. Kefalonia is more rural and less developed than Zakynthos — outside Argostoli, the rhythm is tavernas and early nights.

Choose Zakynthos if…

  • Navagio (Shipwreck Beach) is on your must-see list. It's one of the most photographed beaches in the world — accessible only by boat from Porto Vromi or Agios Nikolaos.
  • You want a developed package-holiday experience. Laganas and Tsilivi on the south and east have the most complete tourist infrastructure of any Ionian island.
  • You're traveling in your 20s with friends and the nightlife scene is part of the appeal. Laganas has Greece's most concentrated young-British-and-Italian club strip outside Mykonos.
  • You'd consider snorkeling with sea turtles. Zakynthos has the Laganas Bay marine park, the most important loggerhead turtle (Caretta caretta) breeding site in the Mediterranean.
  • You want the cheapest possible Greek-island package — flights+hotels from British, German, and Italian operators are aggressively priced.

Beaches: a true tie, but the character is very different

Both islands score 4.7-4.8 on beaches — among the best in Greece. The numbers are essentially identical; the experiences are not.

Kefalonia's beaches are tall-cliffs-falling-to-turquoise-water dramatic. Myrtos is the iconic example — a long pebble crescent backed by 200-meter cliffs, reached by a winding road with a stunning overlook at the top. Petani is a quieter alternative with similar drama. Antisamos has clear water and the Captain Corelli's Mandolin film association. Xi Beach on the south has red volcanic sand. Fteri Beach is reached only by boat or a 25-minute hike. The variety and visual quality competes with the best of the Cyclades.

Zakynthos's beaches divide into two categories: the southern resort beaches (Laganas, Kalamaki, Tsilivi) which are wide sand strips backed by developed tourist infrastructure, and the famous west-coast beauty spots reached only by boat. Navagio (Shipwreck Beach) is the calling card — a 200-meter cove of white pebbles and turquoise water surrounded by sheer cliffs, with the wreck of a 1980 ship resting on the sand. It's genuinely spectacular but currently has access restrictions due to cliff erosion (check before visiting). The Blue Caves at the northern tip are another must-see by boat. The Marathonisi turtle-protection island offshore from Laganas is famous for its loggerhead nesting beach. For traditional swim-from-the-beach experiences: the south coast. For iconic photography spots: the west coast (boat required).

The reputation question is real

Zakynthos has acquired a reputation in some travel circles as "the Greek island most damaged by mass tourism" — and there's truth in it for specific areas. Laganas is the main offender: a 4 km strip of bars, clubs, English-pub food, foam parties, and young British package travelers. In peak August, the volume and noise can be overwhelming. Kalamaki is somewhat better but still package-tourism-dominated. Tsilivi is family-oriented and less rowdy but still very developed.

The catch is that this represents maybe 15% of the island's actual area. The north (Volimes, Anafonitria), the west coast, the inland villages, and Zakynthos town itself remain genuinely charming and feel like a normal Greek island. Travelers who avoid Laganas and Kalamaki — staying in Zakynthos town, the north, or the inland villages — get a substantially different experience than the British-package-tourism stereotype suggests. The reputation problem is real, but it's manageable by base choice.

Kefalonia has no equivalent zone. Lassi (near Argostoli) is the main resort area but it's gentler — more couples and families than youth-package travelers, more apartments than club strips. There's nothing on Kefalonia equivalent to Laganas in scale or character.

The feel of each island

Kefalonia feels like a country compressed into an island. Argostoli, the capital, was destroyed in the catastrophic 1953 earthquake and rebuilt — pleasant but not historic. Fiskardo (north) is the exception: it survived the earthquake, retains its Venetian architecture, and feels more like a polished harbor village. The interior is dominated by Mount Ainos (Greece's only national park on an island), with pine-fir forests and wild horses. Most visitors stay in Lassi or Argostoli (south), Skala (southeast), or Fiskardo (north) and accept that distances mean driving 30-60 minutes between sights. Evenings are tavernas and early nights; Skala has the closest thing to a nightlife scene.

Zakynthos has more contrast between developed and authentic. Zakynthos town is rebuilt from the same 1953 earthquake but has good restaurants and a working harbor. The southern resort strip (Laganas-Kalamaki-Argassi) is what's famous; the northern half of the island (Volimes, the Blue Caves area, Skinari) is much quieter. Inland villages like Maries and Anafonitria are traditional and almost untouched by mass tourism. Travelers who base in the north or in Zakynthos town itself rather than the southern strip get a meaningfully different island.

Logistics and cost

Both islands have international airports with direct European flights — Zakynthos has substantially more flight volume in summer (British, German, Italian charters dominate; ferries from Italy also possible). Kefalonia has more refined and slightly fewer flights. Both have ferry connections from Kyllini on the mainland — Kefalonia 1.5 hours, Zakynthos 1 hour.

Zakynthos is meaningfully cheaper, partly because of package-holiday pricing. Mid-range hotels in high season run €90-180 a night on Kefalonia vs €70-160 on Zakynthos, with package deals on Zakynthos often dropping below €50/night including flights from northern Europe. Restaurants run €25-45 per person on Kefalonia, €20-40 on Zakynthos (less in Laganas, more in Zakynthos town). For a non-package week, Kefalonia runs €200-400 more than Zakynthos for comparable accommodation. The affordability scores reflect this: Kefalonia 3.8, Zakynthos 3.5 — both reasonable but Zakynthos is genuinely the cheaper choice.

Both islands genuinely require a car (Kefalonia 5/5, Zakynthos 4/5). Kefalonia is huge and the highlights are spread out; Zakynthos is smaller but the north-south distance is still 30-45 minutes by car.

How long should you stay?

Kefalonia rewards 6-7 days because the island is large and the highlights are spread out. A serious Kefalonia trip: Myrtos and the west coast, Fiskardo, Argostoli and its turtles, the Melissani-Drogarati cave pair, Skala and the southeast beaches, and at least one drive into Ainos. Going for less than 5 days means making hard cuts.

Zakynthos works at 4-7 days depending on the trip's intent. A package-style week with most days at the resort base and one or two boat trips (Navagio, Blue Caves, turtle spotting) absorbs 7 days easily. A more exploratory trip can be done in 4-5 days: the boat trips (one day), the western cliffs (one day), the northern villages (one day), and Zakynthos town plus a south beach (one day).

The honest verdict

The original short-form framing remains right: Zakynthos if Navagio is non-negotiable, if you're traveling as a young group, or if a package-holiday cost structure matters. Kefalonia for almost any other reason — families, couples, active travelers, anyone wanting Ionian beauty without the southern-Zakynthos crowds. But two refinements after spending more time with both islands: first, Zakynthos genuinely has more sides than its reputation suggests — basing in the north or in Zakynthos town rather than Laganas/Kalamaki gives a much closer-to-Kefalonia experience. Second, Kefalonia's "no Navagio" position is sometimes overstated — Myrtos genuinely competes for the same iconic-beach status, and the cliff overlook above Myrtos is arguably the better photo spot in the Ionian. The combination of Kefalonia + Zakynthos in one trip works well (they're connected by direct ferry in summer — about 1.5 hours), with a typical 10-day Ionian arrangement being 5 days Kefalonia + 4 days Zakynthos. If you have to pick one, the right question isn't "which is better" — both are excellent — but "which kind of trip am I after." The two islands offer genuinely different trips at the same overall quality level.

Common questions

Which has Myrtos Beach?

Kefalonia. Myrtos is on Kefalonia's northwest coast — a 1km crescent of white pebbles backed by 200-meter limestone cliffs. It's one of the most photographed beaches in Greece and the cliff overlook above it provides arguably the best photo spot in the Ionian. Zakynthos has Navagio (Shipwreck Beach) as its iconic counterpart — a different kind of beauty, accessed only by boat. Both are world-class; both are reasons to pick their respective islands.

Is Zakynthos really all about Laganas and partying?

No — that's a stereotype that captures maybe 15% of the island. Laganas is genuinely overdeveloped and dominated by young British and Italian package travelers, particularly in peak August. But Zakynthos's north (Volimes, the Blue Caves area), west coast, and inland villages remain genuinely traditional and quiet. Travelers who base in Zakynthos town, in the north, or in the inland villages — rather than Laganas/Kalamaki — get a meaningfully different island experience. The reputation problem is real but manageable by base choice.

Can I visit Navagio (Shipwreck Beach)?

Yes, but currently with restrictions. Navagio became famous worldwide and over-visited, leading to cliff erosion concerns and access changes. As of recent years, the beach itself has had periods of closure to swimmers due to falling-rock risk; the cliff-top viewing platform above it has also had restrictions. The reliable approach is the boat tours from Porto Vromi or Agios Nikolaos that view it from the water (you may or may not be allowed to land depending on current rules — check directly with operators). The viewing platform on the cliff is sometimes accessible by car/walk. Don't book your entire Zakynthos trip around being able to swim at Navagio specifically; build it as a hoped-for highlight, not a guaranteed one.

Which is better for families, Kefalonia or Zakynthos?

Both work, but for different reasons. Kefalonia is better for families with older children (8+) who can handle longer drives between sights and appreciate the natural drama (caves, mountains, beaches with cliffs). Zakynthos is easier for families with younger children — the resort areas (Tsilivi especially, but also family-oriented sections of Laganas) have shallow beaches, kid-friendly restaurants, water parks, and dense infrastructure within walking distance. For an under-5s holiday: Zakynthos resort. For an 8-12 active-family holiday: Kefalonia. For teenagers: Kefalonia for the active families, Zakynthos for families who specifically want resort comfort.

Can I do Kefalonia and Zakynthos in one trip?

Yes — they're connected by direct ferry in summer (about 1.5 hours between Pessada on Kefalonia and Agios Nikolaos on Zakynthos). A typical 10-day Ionian arrangement: 5 days Kefalonia + 4 days Zakynthos, in either order. Both islands also have international airports, so you can fly into one and out of the other to skip the return ferry. The combination works particularly well because the islands offer such different experiences — Kefalonia's road-trip drama plus Zakynthos's Navagio boat trip + resort relaxation.
