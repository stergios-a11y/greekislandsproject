When to visit — overlap

Our verdict

The short answer: Neighbours with a daily ferry, so this is also a "both?" question. Santorini (4.8) you know: the caldera, the sunsets, the prices (1.0). Heraklion (4.2) is the anti-Santorini — a working Cretan capital with Knossos 5km from the centre, the world's finest Minoan collection in the Archaeological Museum, wine villages at Peza and Archanes, and food that locals actually eat, at double the affordability (3.5). Santorini is scored for three days; Heraklion for five, and it needs a car (5.0) to use them. Both have international airports, and there is a two-hour fast ferry between them — which is why for most people the honest answer isn't either/or.

Choose Heraklion if…

  • Archaeology is the reason you're going. The Palace of Knossos sits 5km from the city centre, and the Archaeological Museum holds the Snake Goddess figurines, the Phaistos Disc and the Bull-leaping fresco — allow two hours minimum.
  • You have five days and a car. The 5.0 car-need score is not a warning here, it's an invitation: Phaistos, Gortyna, the Lasithi Plateau at 840m and Matala all work from one base.
  • Money matters. Heraklion's 3.5 affordability against Santorini's 1.0 is the widest gap on this page, and the overnight ferry from Piraeus runs €40–70 deck, €70–120 for a cabin.
  • You eat as a reason to travel. The covered market on 1866 Street does the best street food in Crete — kalitsounia, dakos, local honey — and Peza's wineries pour Kotsifali and Mandilari.
  • You're travelling in July or August. Santorini's calendar marks both months avoid; Heraklion's is merely hot — do Knossos at 8am and the air-conditioned museum at midday.

Choose Santorini if…

  • You have three nights, not seven. Three days is the island's scored stay, and the Fira-to-Imerovigli caldera path (1.5km, 30 minutes, flat and paved) gives you its best view inside the first hour.
  • The caldera itself is the trip. Skaros Rock below Imerovigli — 15 minutes down the stone steps, 20 back up — delivers 360° views that beat anything else on the island.
  • You want a volcano and a winery in the same 48 hours. The half-day boat from Fira Old Port covers Nea Kameni's crater and the Palea Kameni hot springs; Santo Wines outside Pyrgos pours flights from €25.
  • Bronze Age archaeology moves you but driving doesn't. Akrotiri is €12 and an hour, its frescoes are in the Museum of Prehistoric Thera in Fira, and car-need here is only 3.0.
  • You're going in late September. That's the sweet spot — sea still warm, cruise crowds thinning, light golden, prices halving overnight. Heraklion is good then too; Santorini is transformed.

The two-hour ferry makes this a route, not a choice

Santorini's own getting-there note is blunt about it: combining with Crete, the daily fast ferry to Heraklion takes about two hours. That single line changes the question. Fly into Santorini, fly out of Heraklion — both are major international airports served by Aegean, Sky Express, Ryanair and EasyJet — and you never backtrack. Three nights on the caldera is exactly what Santorini is scored for; four on Crete buys you Knossos and the museum on one day, Archanes, the Fourni cemetery and Peza on another, and the long southern run to Phaistos, Matala and Gortyna on a third. The order matters: spectacle first, substance second.

Run it the other way and two things go wrong. You arrive on the caldera already sunburnt and tired of driving, and you pay Santorini prices at the end of the trip, when the budget is thinnest. There is a practical asymmetry too. Santorini's ferries dock at Athinios, the commercial port 10km south of Fira, so arriving by sea means a transfer up the cliff before you see anything. Heraklion's port is in the centre of the city — five minutes from the Koules fortress and the market street, with the airport out east for the flight home. As an arrival, Crete is the softer landing; as a departure, Santorini's airport sits ten minutes from Akrotiri, which makes the last morning easy.

Price, pace, and what each island is scored for

The affordability numbers — 3.5 against 1.0 — behave exactly as you'd expect. Santorini is the rare island where the recommended stay (three days) is shorter than most people would like, because the cost curve bends up fast on a 76km² island where the caldera view is the product. Heraklion is scored at five days and rewards them: Lasithi and the Dikteon Cave on one, Kritsa and the north coast on another, the Messara plain on a third. Transport is the other half of the story. Heraklion's 5.0 car-need means you will rent one and use it daily; Santorini's 3.0 means buses and taxis cover Fira, Oia, Pyrgos and Akrotiri well enough, with a taxi from Imerovigli down to Fira Old Port costing about €15.

Timing splits them further. Santorini's calendar marks July and August as avoid outright — three or four cruise ships a day in Fira and Oia, every sunset spot heaving, prices at maximum. Heraklion's July and August are only "ok": hot, often 35°C or more, with Knossos punishing at midday, but the museum is air-conditioned and the city lives year-round rather than closing down. In June and September both are at their best — Heraklion "perfect" for the Summer Festival at the Koules fortress and for Knossos breathing again, Santorini "perfect" as the cruise season tapers. If your dates are locked to high summer, that alone is close to an answer.

Santorini for the show, Heraklion for the meal — ideally in that order, connected by ferry. As a straight choice: two or three days and a camera, Santorini; a week and an appetite, Heraklion, with a car. But the smart play is the combination — fly into one, out of the other, with the two-hour ferry in between. Three nights of caldera spectacle, four of Cretan substance: it fixes both islands' weaknesses, and our Budget page prices the whole route.

Common questions

Can I visit both Heraklion and Santorini in one trip?

Yes — this is the pairing the ferry timetable was made for. The daily fast ferry between them takes about two hours. Fly into Santorini, spend three nights on the caldera (its scored stay), take the ferry to Heraklion and give Crete four days with a rental car, then fly home from Heraklion. Doing it in that order means you never backtrack and you don't pay Santorini prices at the end.

Which is cheaper, Heraklion or Santorini?

Heraklion, by the widest margin on our board: 3.5 affordability against Santorini's 1.0. Getting there is cheaper too — the overnight ferry from Piraeus to Heraklion is €40–70 on deck and €70–120 with a cabin, against €60–90 for the Santorini fast ferry. And on the ground, a lunch of kalitsounia and dakos from the 1866 market street costs a fraction of a caldera-view table in Fira.

Which is better with children?

Heraklion. You'll have a car anyway (car-need 5.0), and that opens up Agia Pelagia, a sheltered horseshoe cove 20km west with calm water and tavernas above the sand, plus long shallow Malia beach on the north coast. Knossos works for kids if you go at opening. Santorini's beaches are volcanic pebble and black sand, its cliff paths involve steps, and the prices punish a family of four.

Which is better in September?

Both are marked perfect in September, so this comes down to what changes. Heraklion's September is Knossos breathing again after the August crush — good, but not different from June. Santorini's late September is transformed: cruise season tapering, sunset spots breathable, prices halving overnight. If you can only take one and the month is September, take Santorini — it is the only time the island is worth what it charges.

How do I get from Heraklion to Santorini?

By the daily fast ferry, roughly two hours. It docks at Athinios, Santorini's commercial port 10km south of Fira, so budget for a transfer up to the caldera villages on arrival. Heraklion's own port is in the middle of the city, five minutes from the Koules fortress. Both islands also have international airports if you'd rather fly one leg.
