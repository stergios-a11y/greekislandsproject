Our verdict

The short answer: Chios, 3.6 to 3.3, and it wins for one reason that outweighs everything else: the mastic villages and Nea Moni are unlike anywhere else in Greece, and they are the reason Chios scores 4.7 for culture — the highest in the North Aegean. Samos is greener, has slightly better beaches (3.5 to 3.2), the Eupalinos Tunnel, and a one-hour boat to Ephesus. Both are large, working islands where tourism is a side business rather than the economy, both are cheap, and both are badly under-visited. This is a good problem to have.

Choose Chios if…

  • You want villages that are genuinely unique. Pyrgi's houses are covered in xysta — scraped geometric black-and-white facades — and Mesta is an intact fortified medieval village you walk into through a gate.
  • You want Byzantine art. Nea Moni is a UNESCO World Heritage site with 11th-century mosaics of the first rank, and there is rarely anyone else there.
  • You want mastic. Chios is the only place on earth where these trees yield gum mastic, and the cultivation is on the UNESCO intangible heritage list.
  • You want the cheapest of the two — 4.5 for affordability against 4.2, on an island where tourist pricing barely exists outside Karfas.
  • You want the strange ones: Anavatos, an abandoned village on a cliff edge, and Mavra Volia, a beach of black volcanic pebbles.

Choose Samos if…

  • You want to swim more. 3.5 against 3.2 — Tsamadou and Lemonakia on the north coast are properly good, and Megalo Seitani is reachable only on foot or by boat.
  • You want green. Samos is pine, vine and cypress, with Mt Kerkis at 1,433 m, walkable gorges and the Potami waterfalls. Chios is drier and barer.
  • You want ancient engineering. The Eupalinos Tunnel is a 1,036 m 6th-century BC aqueduct driven through a mountain from both ends at once, and walking it is the best thing either island offers.
  • You want Ephesus. Boats from Pythagoreio and Vathy reach Kuşadası in about an hour, making one of the great classical sites a day trip.
  • You want wine. Samos Muscat is a PDO sweet wine and the co-operative is worth an afternoon.

Both are better than their scores suggest

Worth saying plainly: Chios at 3.6 and Samos at 3.3 look modest, and the reason is that our scoring rewards beaches and access heavily, and neither island is exceptional at either. On what they actually contain, both punch far above those numbers. Samos has one of the greatest surviving works of ancient engineering and a sanctuary of Hera that was among the largest in the Greek world. Chios has a UNESCO monastery, a UNESCO-listed craft, and two medieval villages that no amount of restoration elsewhere can imitate. If you are the kind of traveller who reads the site before booking the beach, adjust both upwards.

Culture: a near tie, decided on strangeness

4.7 against 4.6 is inside the noise, so the choice comes down to what kind of past you want. Samos's is monumental and classical: the Heraion with its single re-erected column, the Pythagoreio harbour, and the Eupalinos Tunnel, which you can walk a long way into. Two ancient teams dug from opposite sides of a mountain and met in the middle; standing at the junction is one of the few places where you feel the intelligence of the ancient world rather than just its scale.

Chios's past is medieval and commercial. The Genoese ran the island for two centuries on the mastic trade, and the mastihohoria in the south — Pyrgi, Mesta, Olympi — were built as fortified villages to protect it. Pyrgi's scraped facades are a decorative technique found nowhere else at that density; Mesta is a complete defensive plan you can still read in the streets. Add Nea Moni's mosaics and the ruined ghost village at Anavatos, and Chios is the more unusual of the two — which is why it edges the total.

Beaches: Samos, but neither is why you come

Samos scores 3.5 and earns it on the north coast, where Tsamadou, Lemonakia and Kokkari sit under wooded slopes, and in the far northwest at Megalo and Mikro Seitani — no road, a walk in, and a monk seal population. Psili Ammos in the southeast is shallow, sandy and startlingly close to the Turkish coast.

Chios scores 3.2, which is fair. Mavra Volia at Emporios is memorable for the black pebbles rather than the swimming, Karfas is the one organised sandy resort beach, and the west coast around Elinta and Trachili is beautiful but requires effort. Vroulidia in the far south is the small spectacular one. Swimming here is a pleasant part of the day, not the plan.

Practicalities

Both are big — Chios 842 km², Samos 477 km² — and both need a car; 4.0 apiece. Both have airports with domestic flights and some seasonal European charters, and overnight ferries from Piraeus. Samos scores marginally better on access at 3.5 against 3.2, largely on flight frequency.

Chios is the cheaper, at 4.5 against 4.2. On both islands you are eating in tavernas that serve locals in winter, which is the practical meaning of those numbers.

The honest verdict

Chios if you want to be surprised — the mastic villages, the mosaics at Nea Moni and the sheer oddity of Pyrgi are the strongest single argument in the North Aegean, and almost nobody outside Greece has heard it. Samos if you want a greener island, better swimming, a serious walk and the option of Ephesus for a day. If you have ten days, do both: they are neighbours, the connection is straightforward in season, and together they make the case that the North Aegean is the most under-rated group in the country.
