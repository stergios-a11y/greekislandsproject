Our verdict

The short answer: Chania, 4.8 to 4.0, and 4.8 is the highest total on this site. It takes beaches 5.0 to 4.0, culture 4.7 to 3.5, and access 4.5 to 3.2. But the number that should shape your plan is the distance: these are the opposite ends of Crete, roughly four hours apart by road. This is not a choice between two bases for one trip — it is a choice between two different holidays. Chania is the better one for most people. Lasithi is the answer if you want the east's specific set-pieces or fewer other visitors.

Choose Chania if…

  • Beaches are anywhere near the point. A 5.0, the joint-best on this site: Balos lagoon, Elafonisi's pink sand, Falassarna and the fjord-like inlet at Seitan Limania.
  • You want a town to fall for. The Venetian harbour with its Egyptian-built lighthouse, the Ottoman Küçük Hasan mosque, the arsenali and the covered market are one walk.
  • You want mountains. The White Mountains rise to 2,453 m and the Samaria Gorge — 16 km, one of Europe's longest — starts up there.
  • You want the roadless south: Loutro, Agia Roumeli and Sfakia, reached on foot or by boat.
  • You care about Cretan food specifically. Western Crete is where the reputation comes from.

Choose Lasithi if…

  • You want Vai — the largest natural palm forest in Europe, and nothing in western Crete resembles it.
  • You want Spinalonga, the Venetian sea-fort that became a leper colony until 1957, reached by boat from Elounda or Plaka.
  • You want Chrissi, a protected islet of cedar forest and pale sand an hour's boat from Ierapetra.
  • You want the Zakros gorge — the Valley of the Dead — walking down to a Minoan palace at the water's edge on the island's eastern tip.
  • You want fewer people. Lasithi has 76,000 residents across 1,823 km² and the far east empties out completely.

One thing that applies to every corner of Crete: car reliance scores 5.0 here, the maximum we give. The island is 260 km long, the bus network serves the north coast and little else, and every recommendation on this page assumes you are driving.

Four hours apart — plan for one, not both

Chania town to Sitia is around 350 km and most of a day's driving. People routinely underestimate this, book a week, and spend two days of it on the national road. If you have seven nights, pick an end. If you have twelve or more, splitting Crete into a western half and an eastern half genuinely works, and Rethymno or Heraklion make sensible middle stops.

Chania wins on the scoring, and it is not marginal

4.8 is the highest total we give any destination. The beach score of 5.0 is earned in the far west — Balos on the northwest tip, reached by rough track or boat from Kissamos; Elafonisi in the southwest where the sand genuinely runs pink; Falassarna's long exposed strand. Culture at 4.7 comes from layers rather than a single site: Venetian, Ottoman and Jewish, including the restored Etz Hayyim synagogue, all inside a harbour town you can walk in an afternoon.

Lasithi's case is specific, not general

Its 4.0 is respectable rather than remarkable, and the reason to come is a handful of things that exist nowhere else on the island. Vai's palm forest is genuinely singular. Spinalonga carries a hard, recent history and has become one of Crete's most-visited sites since Victoria Hislop's novel — go early. Chrissi, off Ierapetra, is a protected island of Lebanon cedar and shell sand that feels tropical. And Kato Zakros pairs a Minoan palace with a gorge walk in a way no other site manages.

Inland, the Lasithi plateau with its windmills and the Dikteon Cave — where Zeus was supposedly born and hidden — is a straightforward half-day, and Kritsa's Panagia Kera holds some of the best Byzantine frescoes in Crete.

The honest weaknesses

Lasithi is the most expensive corner of Crete at 3.2, because Elounda is where the island's luxury hotels are concentrated, and it has the weakest access at 3.2 — Sitia's airport is small, so most people fly into Heraklion and drive one to two hours east. Chania is busy: its old town in August is intensely crowded and priced accordingly, and Balos and Elafonisi both suffer from their own fame.

The honest verdict

Chania for a first trip to Crete, without much hesitation — the best beaches on the island, the best town, and the mountains behind. Lasithi if you have been to Crete before, or if Vai, Spinalonga and Chrissi are the images that made you want to go, or if you would rather trade a little scenery for considerably fewer people. What does not work is trying to have both in a week.
