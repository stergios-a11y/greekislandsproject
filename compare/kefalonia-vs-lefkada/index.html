Kefalonia vs Lefkada

Side-by-side comparison — beaches, culture, atmosphere, and the practical question of which one suits your trip.

Our verdict

The short answer: Kefalonia by the narrowest margin on this site — 4.1 to 4.0 — and the two are neighbours across a channel, both beach-first, both demanding a car. Lefkada actually has the better beaches, 4.9 against 4.7, the highest beach score in the Ionian, and you drive onto it across a bridge with no ferry or flight to book. Kefalonia is four times its size at 773 km², Greece's sixth-largest island, with more culture (3.2 against 2.5), better villages and a grander landscape. Lefkada for a shorter, cheaper, beach-dense trip. Kefalonia for a full week of road-tripping.

Choose Kefalonia if…

  • You have five days or more. Kefalonia's sights are spread across 773 km² and the driving is the trip — car reliance scores 5.0, the maximum we give.
  • You want villages worth staying in. Fiskardo is the only settlement that survived the 1953 earthquake intact, so its Venetian houses are the real thing; Assos below its fortress is the other one.
  • You want geology you can walk into. Melissani, its roof collapsed so a shaft of midday sun lands on the water, and Drogarati's stalactite chamber are both genuinely remarkable.
  • You want a mountain and a wine. Mt Ainos rises to 1,628 m with a national park of endemic Kefalonian fir, and the high-altitude Robola vineyards are on the way up.
  • You want the single most photographed bay in Greece. Myrtos seen from the road above is why people come, and it earns it.

Choose Lefkada if…

  • Beaches are the entire point — 4.9 against 4.7, the best in the Ionian. Porto Katsiki, Kathisma and Egremni are a wall of white cliff and turquoise that nothing on Kefalonia quite matches for consistency.
  • You do not want to fly or book a ferry. Lefkada is joined to the mainland by a bridge across a narrow canal: access 4.5 against 3.5, and you simply drive on.
  • You want the cheaper island — 4.0 for affordability against 3.8, and four days rather than five.
  • You windsurf. Vassiliki in the south is one of the best and most reliable windsurfing bays in Europe.
  • You sail, or want the small islands. Meganisi, Kalamos, Kastos and Skorpios sit just offshore, and Lefkada is the natural base for all of them.

Scale against concentration

This is the whole comparison. Kefalonia is enormous and its highlights are deliberately far apart: Argostoli in the south, Myrtos on the west coast, Melissani and Sami on the east, Fiskardo at the northern tip, Ainos in the middle. An hour's drive between two things is normal. That is a feature if you like a road trip and a problem if you have three days — and it is why we budget five.

Lefkada packs almost everything into the western third of a much smaller island. Base yourself at Agios Nikitas or Vassiliki and the great beaches are a 20-minute drive apart. Four days genuinely covers it.

The beaches: Lefkada, but read the small print

4.9 is one of the highest beach scores we give. The west coast is a single limestone wall dropping into water that reads almost synthetic — Porto Katsiki with its stack and staircase, Kathisma the long organised one, Egremni the biggest. One honest caveat: the 2015 earthquake destroyed Egremni's long stairway and access has been unreliable since, so treat the boat trip from Nydri or Vassiliki as the dependable way to see it rather than assuming you can drive and walk down.

Kefalonia's 4.7 is spread wider and more varied: Myrtos for the view more than the swim, Antisamos in its green amphitheatre, Xi with red sand, Petani, Skala's long strand. Fewer showstoppers, more range.

Kefalonia wins on everything that is not sand

Culture 3.2 against 2.5 undersells it slightly. The Katavothres at Argostoli are a genuine curiosity — seawater pours into sinkholes on the west coast and re-emerges across the island near Karavomylos, a mechanism that took decades to prove. The loggerhead turtles in Argostoli harbour are wild and reliably present. Mt Ainos has a forest of Abies cephalonica that exists nowhere else. And the 1953 earthquake, which flattened almost every building on the island, is the reason Argostoli looks modern and Fiskardo does not — knowing that changes how you read both.

Lefkada's cultural case is thinner and it does not pretend otherwise: Karya for lace and embroidery, Eglouvi for its lentils, the Dimosari waterfalls behind Nydri, and the canal itself, cut in antiquity and re-cut by the Venetians.

Getting there, and doing both

Kefalonia has an airport with summer charters and ferries from Kyllini, Patras and Astakos; 3.5 for access. Lefkada has no airport of its own — you fly to Preveza, 20 minutes away, or just drive — and scores 4.5 because the bridge removes the variable that ruins Ionian itineraries.

The pairing works. The Vasiliki–Fiskardo ferry runs in summer, so a loop that drives onto Lefkada, works down the west coast, then crosses to northern Kefalonia is the best week either island supports.

The honest verdict

If it is one week and you like driving, Kefalonia — it is the more complete island, and 4.1 against 4.0 reflects a genuine edge in variety rather than a tie. If it is a long weekend, or the beach photograph is the reason you are going, or you are already road-tripping western Greece, Lefkada is the better answer and cheaper besides. And if you cannot decide, do not: they are a summer ferry apart and the contrast between the beach wall and the big green island is the argument for going at all.
