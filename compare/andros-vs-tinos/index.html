When to visit — overlap

Our verdict

The short answer: The two big northern Cyclades, both two hours from Rafina, both blissfully underbuilt — and both scoring 3.7 for completely different reasons. Andros is the walkers' island; Tinos is the island of faith and food. Andros is green in a way no other Cycladic island is: springs, waterfalls, and a legendary network of restored footpaths linking the mansions the shipowners built. Its beaches (3.8) — Achla, Chrissi Ammos, Vitali — reward the effort of reaching them. Tinos answers with the Panagia Evangelistria pilgrimage, forty-plus marble-laced villages (Pyrgos above all, with its Halepas museum), 740 dovecotes, and quietly the best food scene in the Cyclades right now — from a village taverna like Sta Fys Aera to San to Alati on the sand at Agios Fokas. Together they make the best one-week culture-and-calm loop in the Cyclades.

Choose Andros if…

  • Your ideal day is a three-hour trail ending at an empty beach. The Andros Route is 100 km of stone-paved paths across the island, and the organised Andros Marathon walks run in May and October.
  • You want water in a Cycladic landscape. Drink from the Sariza fountain in Apikia, walk fifteen minutes through oak forest to the Pythara waterfalls, and finish at Stenies with its running streams.
  • You'd like an art museum with your island. The Goulandris Museum of Contemporary Art in Chora has Modigliani, Picasso and Giacometti, and the Archaeological Museum holds the Hermes of Andros.
  • You want a beach that costs you a walk. Gialia, below Stenies, is a twenty-minute path away with no canteen; Achla in the northeast is boat or dirt track only, with a stream feeding emerald water.
  • You're going in May, June or September — all "perfect" months on our calendar — and you'll avoid August, when the Athens summer-house crowd makes it the one month we say to skip.

Choose Tinos if…

  • It's a marble village, a long taverna lunch, and a glass of local wine. Pyrgos has three museums including Giannoulis Halepas' old house; Panormos, five minutes below it, has seafood tavernas on the water.
  • You eat first and plan second. Marathia and San to Alati at Agios Fokas and Thalassaki on the Ysternia jetty are all in the Greek Gastronomy Guide; louza and volaki cheese come from any village shop.
  • You want the shortest, easiest logistics. The fast ferry from Rafina takes about two hours (€30–45), runs multiple times daily, and docks in Tinos Town itself — no 35 km transfer from the port.
  • You'd like something strange. Volax is a village sitting in a plain of giant granite boulders; the Tarabados trail passes fifty-plus dovecotes in one kilometre of terraced valley.
  • You surf, kitesurf, or don't mind wind. Kolymbithra on the north coast has a surf school and is the best place in the Cyclades to learn; Livada draws kitesurfers when the meltemi blows.

Ferry logistics from Rafina — and doing both

Neither island has an airport, and neither is really a Piraeus island: Andros sails only from Rafina (about two hours, €18–35), and Tinos is best reached the same way — the fast ferry takes about two hours (€30–45) and runs multiple times daily, against four to five hours from Piraeus. Rafina is an hour east of Athens, and the KTEL bus from the Mavromateon terminal goes straight to the port, so you do not need a car in Athens. The difference is what happens when you land. Tinos ferries dock in Tinos Town, and the closest real beach, Agios Fokas, is ten minutes on foot. Andros ferries dock at Gavrio on the northwest coast, 35 km from Chora and the lively southeast. On Andros the car starts working the moment you disembark.

Doing both is not just possible, it is the obvious plan. Tinos' ferries run south to Andros as well as to Mykonos (30–40 minutes) and Syros, so the loop is Rafina–Andros–Tinos–Rafina in either direction, with no backtracking. Give each island three nights — that is the length of both our itineraries — and you have the week. Which comes first depends on what you want at the end: if you would rather finish with a long dinner at To Perivoli tis Kardianis than with a hike, take Andros first and Tinos second; if you want to leave on a high, reverse it and let Andros' Chora, with the Tourlitis lighthouse at golden hour, be the last thing you see.

The August question

Both islands have an August problem, and they are different problems. On Andros, August is the one month we mark "avoid": the Athens summer-house crowd arrives, the road from Gavrio fills, and the island that is quiet for eleven months is briefly not. It is still walkable — the Pythara trail stays shaded and cool even in August — but it is not the island you came for. On Tinos, August means 15 August: the Dormition at Panagia Evangelistria is one of the largest religious gatherings in Greece, pilgrims crawl the kilometre from the port to the church, and accommodation is booked months ahead. If that is why you came, this is the month. If not, the chaos is overwhelming.

Outside August the calendars line up almost perfectly. Late May, June and September are the months both islands are at their best: sea warm enough to swim, the dovecote villages and marble workshops open without tour groups on Tinos, sea plus walks on Andros — September is the single best month on Andros, and we say so. Tinos is the safer bet at the edges of the season: April brings Holy Week to the Panagia, while Andros in March and April is "hiking heaven" but the sea is still cold. Note the wind: the meltemi hammers Tinos from mid-July through August, which is why its best beaches — Pachia Ammos, Agios Romanos — face south and west, while Kolymbithra becomes a surf beach.

Choose Andros if your ideal day is a three-hour trail ending at an empty beach. Choose Tinos if it's a marble village, a long taverna lunch, and a winery. Both are weekend-perfect from Athens; together, with the ferry between them, they make the best one-week culture-and-calm loop in the Cyclades.

Common questions

Can I do Andros and Tinos in one trip?

Yes, and you should. Both are served from Rafina, an hour east of Athens, and Tinos' ferries run south to Andros as well as on to Mykonos and Syros. The loop is Rafina–Andros–Tinos–Rafina, three nights each — the length of both our itineraries. Remember that Andros ferries dock at Gavrio, 35 km from Chora, while Tinos ferries dock in Tinos Town itself, so plan the car around the Andros leg.

Which is cheaper, Andros or Tinos?

They score the same for affordability (3.2). The ferry from Rafina costs €18–35 to Andros against €30–45 on the fast ferry to Tinos. On the ground both are honest: Sta Fys Aera in Aetofolia and Mpeee in Arnados on Tinos are village prices, and Nonna's in Andros Chora is the reliable local favourite. The exception is 15 August on Tinos, when accommodation is booked months ahead. Outside that week, it is a draw — book by ferry price.

Which is better for a car-free trip?

Neither is ideal — both score 4.0 for needing a car — but Tinos is clearly easier. The ferry docks in Tinos Town, Agios Fokas beach is ten minutes on foot, and the Panagia Evangelistria is an uphill walk from the port. Andros is harder: Gavrio port is 35 km from Chora, the beaches are spread around the coast, and the trailheads at Apikia and Stenies are inland. If you refuse to drive, Tinos.

Which is better for hiking?

Andros, without question. The Andros Route is 100 km of stone-paved trails through oak forest and across streams, the Andros Marathon walks run in May and October, and short walks like the 15-minute forest path to the Pythara waterfalls or the 20-minute descent to Gialia beach are all within reach of Chora. Tinos has one great short walk, the 1 km Tarabados dovecote trail, but it is a driving island.

Which is better in September?

Both are "perfect" in September, so this comes down to what you want. On Andros it is the single best month — sea and walks together, the August summer-house crowd gone. On Tinos the pilgrims have left, Volax and the dovecote villages are quiet again, the sea is at peak warmth, and Kolymbithra calms down enough to be a swimming beach. If forced: Andros, because September is when its beaches and its trails both work.
