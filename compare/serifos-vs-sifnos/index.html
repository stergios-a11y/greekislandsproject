Our verdict

The short answer: Two western Cyclades an hour apart, the same 3.9 overall, and a genuine fork in the road: Serifos is raw, Sifnos is refined. Serifos is granite and light — a spectacular chora spilling down its rock, mining-era relics at Megalo Livadi, and the better beaches of the two (4.5 against 3.8), many reached only by dirt track and mostly empty. Sifnos is the polished one: Kastro's medieval lanes, Apollonia's evening buzz, pottery workshops, the Chrysopigi monastery on its wave-cut rock, and the best village tavernas in the western Cyclades (4.0 culture, food to match). Same ferry line from Piraeus, same three-day rhythm, roughly the same prices (3.8 against 3.5 affordability). What separates them is what you want your evenings to be: a plate of revithada in Artemonas, or the climb to the castle ruins above Livadi for the sunset.

Choose Serifos if…

  • Beaches are the point. Psili Ammos — fine sand, shallow, east-facing and protected, tavernas behind it — is the postcard; Agios Sostis is ten minutes further on foot by dirt track, with a chapel on the headland and nobody there.
  • You want a chora that is an event in itself. Serifos's Chora climbs a pyramid-shaped hill above Livadi; the Venetian castle ruins on top take fifteen minutes more and show you Sifnos and Kythnos at sunset.
  • You like landscapes with a past. Megalo Livadi's abandoned mining harbour, the 1916 strike monument, the semi-abandoned stone village of Mega Chorio — iron mining kept the island going until 1963 and the ruins are still there.
  • You're travelling in late June or September and want an island that feels emptied out. Serifos in August is a Meltemi problem — the north-facing beaches become unusable — so time it right and you'll have Koutalas to yourself.
  • You'd rather spend less and get by with less. Affordability 3.8 against Sifnos's 3.5, ferries from €32, and a car-need score of 3.0: Livadi's rentals, bus stop and rooms are all five minutes from the ferry ramp.

Choose Sifnos if…

  • Food is the reason you travel. Revithada baked overnight in the village oven, mastelo lamb at To Steki in Apollonia, the fish tavernas of Faros, and two FNL award winners, Pelicanos and Alyelo, on the same bay.
  • You want to walk between villages rather than drive. The old mule path from Apollonia to Kastro through olive groves takes forty-five minutes, flat — the finest walk on the island, a daily ritual if you stay a week.
  • Villages matter more than sand. Apollonia's white lanes and its folk museum, Artemonas's Venetian mansions and pottery workshops, and Kastro, a fortress-village whose houses form the outer wall, all within fifteen kilometres.
  • You want a beach with a monastery above it. Apokofto sits directly below the 17th-century Chrysopigi complex on its causeway-linked rock; Platis Gialos, 900 metres of sheltered south-facing sand, is the reliable family option.
  • You like an evening that ends in a bar, not a kafenio. Sifnos packs its nightlife into one Apollonia alley, Steno — Argo since 1983, rooftop jazz at Botzi 93, Doloma for the late crowd. Civilised, not raucous.

Food is the real difference

Both islands claim revithada — Serifos says it has the original, Sifnos serves it best in Artemonas and Kastro — and both age manoura cheese in wine lees until the rind goes purple. That is where the parity ends. Sifnos has a restaurant culture that runs on bookings: in June the famous tavernas (To Steki, Omega, Lempesis) are bookable same-day, in July they need two or three days' notice, and in August the island turns into a queue, with Omega impossible without a week's warning. The reward is depth — Cantina at Seralia below Kastro, Pelicanos and Alyelo on Faros bay, the Theodorou bakery's almond cookies in Artemonas, pottery workshops in Vathy and Kamares on an island that has been throwing clay for thousands of years.

Serifos eats well but narrowly. Stou Stratou on Chora's square does mezes with the sunset over the port; Seriani does modern cooking with island produce; Takis at Agios Sostis grills fish before the boat. Wild-fennel fritters (marathotiganites) show up in spring and fade by August. That is most of the list, and it's deliberate — facilities are thin because the island is thin. If you'll happily eat at the same three places for three nights, Serifos is fine. If a new taverna every evening is part of the holiday, that alone decides it for Sifnos.

Doing both: the Western Cyclades line

The correct answer for a week is both, and the ferries make it easy. Serifos and Sifnos sit on the same Western Cyclades line out of Piraeus, with Milos and Kimolos further down it: Seajets fast boat to Serifos in around two hours, to Sifnos in two and a half, conventional ferries in four and five. Fares run €32–50 to Serifos and €38–55 to Sifnos. Serifos's boats are daily in summer only and the island shuts down by mid-October; Sifnos runs daily year-round, so put Sifnos at the tail of any shoulder-season trip. Serifos docks at Livadi, the only real village on the coast; Sifnos at Kamares, five kilometres below Apollonia.

Order matters. Do Serifos first — three nights at most, based in Chora or Livadi, with the beaches on day three (Psili Ammos, then the walk to Agios Sostis) — then hop the hour to Sifnos for three or four nights of villages and tavernas. Going the other way, Serifos's emptiness reads as absence rather than relief. Both guides run to three days; both islands score 3.5 for access and 3.9 overall, so neither is the compromise. June and September are perfect months on both; August is "avoid" on Serifos and "ok" on Sifnos, which tells you which one copes with crowds.

Choose Serifos for wildness, swimming, and a cheaper, quieter trip that feels twenty years back in time. Choose Sifnos for food, walking between pretty villages, and comfort with character. They're one ferry stop apart on the same line — and for a week, the correct answer is both, Serifos first.

Common questions

Can I do Serifos and Sifnos in one trip?

Yes — this is the pairing the ferries are built for. Both sit on the same Western Cyclades line from Piraeus (Seajets fast boat about 2 hours to Serifos, 2.5 to Sifnos; conventional 4 and 5), and they are one stop, roughly an hour, apart. Do Serifos first for three nights, then Sifnos for three or four. Sifnos has daily ferries year-round while Serifos runs daily only in summer and winds down by mid-October, so end on Sifnos in the shoulder season.

Which is cheaper, Serifos or Sifnos?

Serifos, by a small but real margin: affordability 3.8 against 3.5, and ferry fares of €32–50 from Piraeus against €38–55. It also gives you less to spend on — three or four reliable tavernas, thin facilities, beaches with a taverna or nothing at all. Sifnos costs more because it offers more: award-winning restaurants at Faros, the Steno bar alley in Apollonia, pottery to carry home.

Which has the better beaches?

Serifos, and the scores agree: 4.5 against 3.8. Psili Ammos is fine, shallow, east-facing sand with tavernas behind it; Agios Sostis is a ten-minute dirt-track walk beyond with a chapel and no shade; Koutalas is a long empty bay with no infrastructure. Sifnos answers with Platis Gialos, 900 metres of organised south-facing sand, and Apokofto below the Chrysopigi monastery — better set-up, less wild. One warning for Serifos: the Meltemi closes its north-facing beaches in July and peaks in August.

Can I visit either island without a car?

Serifos more easily — its car-need score is 3.0 against Sifnos's 4.0. Livadi, the port, has the rooms, the rentals and the bus stop for Chora within five minutes of the ferry, and Livadakia beach is a five-minute walk south. On Sifnos you can walk between Apollonia, Artemonas and Kastro on the old mule paths, but Vathy and Chrysopigi are a long winding road away, so a car for at least a day is the sensible plan.

Which is better in September?

September is rated 'perfect' on both, but pick Sifnos if you can only have one. Late September is when Greeks themselves go: warm sea, tavernas working without the August queues, the Vathy pottery workshops reopening for visitors, and revithada on cooler evenings. Serifos in September is emptier and still warm — a great month for swimmers — but almost everything closes by mid-October, so it is the earlier of the two to visit.
