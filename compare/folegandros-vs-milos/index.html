Folegandros vs Milos

Side-by-side comparison — beaches, culture, atmosphere, and the practical question of which one suits your trip.

Our verdict

The short answer: Milos, 4.7 to 4.0, and the gap is real rather than a rounding difference. Milos holds a perfect 5.0 for beaches — the only island on this site that does — because its volcanic coast produced Sarakiniko, Kleftiko and around seventy beaches that genuinely do not repeat. Folegandros scores 3.9. What Folegandros has instead is one of the two or three finest choras in the Cyclades, perched on a 200-metre cliff, and 765 residents on 32 km². One is a landscape you explore. The other is a village you sit in.

Choose Folegandros if…

  • The Chora is the reason. It sits on the edge of a 200 m cliff with the Kastro quarter at its heart, founded in 1212, and it is as good as anything in the Cyclades.
  • You want stillness. 765 residents on 32 km², and the honest appeal is that there is very little to do beyond walking, swimming at Agali and eating simply.
  • You want to walk up to Panagia. The church above the Chora is a short steep climb and the standard evening ritual.
  • You want somewhere still relatively unphotographed. Milos has grown substantially in the last five years; Folegandros has not, to the same degree.
  • You have three days and no ambition to fill them. Katergo by boat or on foot, Livadaki, and a long dinner is the whole itinerary.

Choose Milos if…

  • Beaches and landscape are the point. A perfect 5.0: Sarakiniko's white volcanic moonscape, the ochre and rust cliffs of Paliochori, striped Firiplaka, and Tsigrado down a rope and ladder.
  • You want Kleftiko — a collapsed white massif of sea caves and arches on the southwest coast, reachable only by boat, and the best day on the water in the Cyclades.
  • You want variety. Around seventy beaches on 151 km², and because the rock changes colour every few kilometres they are genuinely different from each other.
  • You want the painted syrmata at Klima — fishermen's boathouses with primary-coloured doors built into the rock at the waterline.
  • You want easier access — 3.2 against 2.8, and Milos has an airport with Athens flights while Folegandros has none.

Milos is a geology lesson you can swim in

The 5.0 comes from the rock, not the sand. Milos is a volcano that has been quarried since antiquity — obsidian first, then bentonite and perlite, still mined commercially today — and the coastline changes colour and texture every few kilometres as a result. Sarakiniko is white pumice scoured smooth enough to read as lunar. Paliochori is red and ochre with hot springs seeping up through the sand. Firiplaka is banded. Tsigrado is a slot in the cliff you descend by rope and wooden ladder. Kleftiko, on the southwest coast and boat-only, is a collapsed white formation full of arches and swimmable caves.

Its culture score of 3.5 is modest but specific: the early Christian catacombs are among the most important outside Rome, there is a Hellenistic theatre above Klima, and the Venus de Milo was found here in 1820 — though she is in the Louvre, a fact locals will raise before you do.

Folegandros is one village doing all the work

And it does it very well. The Chora is built along the lip of a 200-metre cliff, so the drop is right there beside the square, and the medieval Kastro at its centre dates from 1212 and Marco Sanudo's Duchy of Naxos — houses built as a continuous defensive wall, with wooden balconies inside. Three linked plateias, no cars, and one of the genuinely great Cycladic evenings.

Above it, the church of Panagia is a fifteen-minute zigzag climb and the thing everyone does before dinner. Ano Meria, the scattered farming settlement along the spine of the island, is the other half of Folegandros and where the traditional Cycladic themonies — self-contained farmsteads — still stand.

The beaches, at 3.9, are the honest weak point. Agali is the good one, reached by road then footpath or by the little boat from Karavostasis; Katergo in the south is the striking one and easier by boat; Livadaki and Ambeli are quiet. They are pleasant coves rather than destinations, and after Milos they will feel ordinary.

Cost is where Folegandros gets awkward

It scores 2.2 for affordability — among the lowest on this site, level with Paros and lower than Milos's 2.8. That is worth sitting with: a 32 km² island with 765 people, no airport and an access score of 2.8 is charging Paros prices in August. It is a beautiful place and the scarcity of rooms explains the economics, but nobody should go expecting a bargain for the effort of getting there.

Neither is quiet in August any more

The old framing — that these are the islands you graduate to after the famous ones — is only half true now. Milos in particular has grown fast, and Sarakiniko at midday in August is a queue. Folegandros is smaller and harder to reach so it holds up better, but its Chora is not a secret either. If quiet is the actual requirement, go to either in June or late September and the comparison changes entirely.

The honest verdict

Milos is the better island and it is not marginal — 4.7 is among the highest totals here, earned on a coastline nothing in the Cyclades matches. Choose Folegandros deliberately, for the Chora and the stillness, not as a cheaper or easier alternative, because it is neither. And if you have a week, they connect: an itinerary that gives four days to Milos's coast and three to a cliff-edge village is better than either island alone.

Common questions

Can I visit Folegandros and Milos in one trip?

Yes — the western Cyclades line connects Folegandros with Milos, Sikinos, Ios and Santorini, so this is a normal pairing rather than a detour. Four days on Milos's coast and three in a cliff-edge village is better than either island alone. Confirm the day's sailing when you book: Folegandros has fewer boats, and the access scores, 2.8 against Milos's 3.2, are exactly that difference.

Which is cheaper, Folegandros or Milos?

Milos, though neither is cheap — 2.8 for affordability against Folegandros's 2.2, which is among the lowest on this site and level with Paros. That is worth sitting with: a 32 km² island of 765 people with no airport charges Paros prices in August. The scarcity of rooms explains the economics, but nobody should go to Folegandros expecting a bargain for the effort of getting there.

Which has better beaches?

Milos, and it is not marginal — a perfect 5.0, the only one on this site, against 3.9. Around seventy beaches on 151 km²: white Sarakiniko, ochre Paliochori with hot springs seeping through the sand, striped Firiplaka, Tsigrado down a rope and ladder, and boat-only Kleftiko. Folegandros has pleasant coves — Agali, Katergo, Livadaki — reached on foot or by boat, and after Milos they feel ordinary.

Which is easier to get to?

Milos, 3.2 for access against 2.8. It has a domestic airport with two or three Athens flights a day in summer, 40 minutes, plus fast catamarans from Piraeus in 3–4 hours and conventional car ferries in 6–7. Folegandros has no airport: the fast ferry to Karavostasi takes about 4 hours, the conventional one about 9, and then a 15-minute shuttle bus up to Chora.

Which is better in August?

Folegandros. August is the one month we tell people to skip on Milos — Sarakiniko is packed by 9am, the Tsigrado ladder queues, Kleftiko boats are fully booked and prices double. Folegandros is smaller and harder to reach, so even its busiest weeks stay calm, though its Chora is no secret either. Late May, June or September changes the comparison entirely on both islands.
