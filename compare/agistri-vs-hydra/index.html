Agistri vs Hydra

Side-by-side comparison — beaches, culture, atmosphere, and the practical question of which one suits your trip.

Our verdict

The short answer: Hydra scores higher — 4.0 to 3.4 — and the two share one unusual thing: both score 1.0 for car reliance, the lowest we give, because neither has traffic to speak of. Beyond that they are opposites. Hydra bans wheeled vehicles outright, has a harbour of 18th-century shipowners' mansions, and is the most expensive island on this site at 1.8 for affordability. Agistri has better beaches — 3.5 against Hydra's 2.2, the worst in the Saronic — and costs less than half as much at 4.0. Hydra for the harbour. Agistri to actually swim.

Choose Agistri if…

  • You came to swim. 3.5 against 2.2 is the widest gap here: Dragonera with pines to the waterline, Aponisos's turquoise channel, Chalikiada under the cliff.
  • Money matters. 4.0 for affordability against Hydra's 1.8 — the lowest score on this entire site. This is not a small difference; it is the difference.
  • You want somewhere unpretentious. 1,142 residents, no arts scene, no cruise tenders, nothing to live up to.
  • You have one day. Agistri is 13 km² and an hour from Piraeus on the fast boat; you can swim, eat and be home.
  • You want pine shade rather than bare rock — Agistri is wooded almost to the sea.

Choose Hydra if…

  • You want the most striking harbour in the Saronic. Hydra town is an amphitheatre of grey-stone captains' mansions built on 18th and 19th-century shipping money, and nothing in Greece looks quite like it.
  • You want no vehicles at all — not just few. No cars, no scooters; goods move by mule and people by water taxi. Agistri is walkable, Hydra is genuinely vehicle-free.
  • Culture and atmosphere are the point — 4.2 against 2.5. Hydra supplied admirals and money to the 1821 revolution, then became an artists' colony; the fine-arts school is still there.
  • You want an evening with some style. Nightlife 3.8 against 3.5, but the character is completely different — harbour-front bars rather than a village strip.
  • You are happy to swim off rocks. Hydronetta's ledges, Kaminia and Vlychos are lovely; they are just not sand.

The one thing they share

Both score 1.0 for car reliance, which only a handful of Greek islands do. On Agistri that means the island is small enough to walk or cycle — 13 km², with Skala, Megalochori and Limenaria strung along one coast. On Hydra it means something stricter: wheeled vehicles are prohibited, so freight moves on mules and you get around the coast by water taxi or on foot. Arriving without a car is not a compromise on either island; it is the point.

Where the 4.0-to-3.4 gap comes from — and what it hides

Hydra's lead is built on culture (4.2 against 2.5) and on being a genuinely remarkable-looking place. The town rises in tiers of stone houses around a horseshoe harbour, built by shipping families who then funded the war of independence — Miaoulis and Koundouriotis came from here. In the 1950s and 60s it became an artists' town; Leonard Cohen bought a house, Ghika painted there, and the Athens School of Fine Arts still keeps a station on the island. The Deste Foundation runs a summer show in the old slaughterhouse.

What the total hides is that Hydra is a poor swimming island. 2.2 is the lowest beach score in the Saronic and it is deserved — the coast is rock and pebble, the good spots are boat rides, and there is essentially no sand. If your day is built around getting in the sea and staying there, the higher-scoring island is the wrong one.

The price gap is the real decision

Hydra scores 1.8 for affordability — the single lowest figure on this site, below Mykonos and Santorini. It has very limited rooms, no capacity to build more, day-trip and yacht traffic, and a reputation that lets the harbour-front price accordingly. Agistri's 4.0 is one of the better value scores in the Saronic.

In practice: two nights on Hydra can cost what four or five on Agistri would. If budget is a live constraint, that decides it, and no amount of admiring the architecture changes the arithmetic.

Getting to each

Both are easy. Hydra scores 4.2 for access, Agistri 4.5 — hydrofoils from Piraeus in around 1½ hours for Hydra, about an hour for Agistri direct or via Aegina. Neither needs planning; both fill at summer weekends and empty on Monday.

The honest verdict

They answer different questions, and the totals mislead. Hydra if you want to look at something extraordinary, eat well on a harbour, and do not mind swimming off rocks or paying Saronic-premium prices — it is the more memorable place, and 4.0 is fair. Agistri if you want to spend the day in clear water under pine trees for a fraction of the cost. And a note for anyone weighing a short break: Hydra rewards two nights and gets no better on the fourth, while Agistri is a day trip that can stretch to three easy ones.
