Our verdict

The short answer: Karpathos and Rhodes are the two largest islands of the southern Dodecanese, and they could not be more different in development and character. Rhodes is the regional capital — a UNESCO-listed medieval city, the cliff-top acropolis at Lindos, an international airport with direct flights from across Europe, and a fully developed tourist infrastructure along its eastern coast. Karpathos sits just to the south, similarly long and mountainous, but has stayed remote — Olympos remains a working window into pre-industrial island life, and the beaches are some of the wildest in the Aegean. The scores say it plainly: Rhodes 4.4 overall against Karpathos 3.8, with the gap almost entirely in access (4.8 vs 2.0) and nightlife (4.1 vs 2.2). Rhodes is the easier trip; Karpathos is the more singular one.

Choose Rhodes if…

  • You want to fly in directly. Rhodes has the largest airport in the Dodecanese — Aegean, Sky Express, Ryanair and charters — while Karpathos runs a domestic airport with summer charters, and most people connect through Athens or Rhodes.
  • History is the point. The Palace of the Grand Master, the Street of the Knights and the Lindos acropolis on its 116-metre cliff earn the 5.0 culture score; Karpathos scores 4.0 almost on the strength of Olympos alone.
  • You're travelling with children who need a beach that works every day. Tsambika is 1.2 km of shallow golden sand, fully organised and sheltered from the west wind; Agathi and Glystra are the quieter versions.
  • You want evenings with options. Rhodes scores 4.1 for nightlife against 2.2 — Old Town wine bars, Bar Street on Orfanidou, rooftop drinks in Lindos under the floodlit acropolis. A Karpathos evening is a taverna in Pigadia.
  • You're visiting outside summer. The medieval Old Town is at its best from October to April, when you can walk it without cruise groups; Karpathos runs a skeleton service from December to February.

Choose Karpathos if…

  • The beach is what you came for. Apella — white pebbles, turquoise water, one small taverna, arrive before 11am — and Kyra Panagia and Achata beside it are quieter than any comparable beach in the Cyclades, let alone Rhodes.
  • You want to see Olympos before it changes. Women wear traditional dress daily, bread comes out of communal wood ovens, and the 15 August panigiri — lyra and tsambouna through the night — is among the Aegean's great festivals.
  • You windsurf or kitesurf. Arkassa hosts the PWA World Cup, and July is peak kitesurfing season on the southern bays at Afiartis and Devil's Bay. Rhodes has Prasonisi, but it's an 80 km detour from the itinerary.
  • You hike. September and October are the island's perfect months — the wind drops and the trails from Olympos to Avlona and Aperi to Volada are walkable in cool afternoon light. Rhodes has no equivalent trail culture.
  • You've done the famous islands already. Pigadia is a working port, not a Cycladic postcard; Othos, the highest village, makes a sweet dark red wine; the makarounes at Milos in Olympos are hand-rolled. This is the island before tourism.

The car question is settled; the getting-there question isn't

Both islands score 5.0 for car need, so that's not the deciding line — you rent at Pigadia port or at Rhodes airport and drive either way. What separates them is how you arrive. Rhodes: a Blue Star overnight from Piraeus daily, 13–18 hours, €55–95 on deck or €100–180 in a cabin, or a direct flight that is usually faster and costs about the same as a cabin. Karpathos: the Piraeus ferry runs 17–20 hours overnight, two or three times a week, which is why most travellers fly — via Athens, or on a summer charter straight from Europe. The access scores (4.8 vs 2.0) are the whole story.

Doing both in one trip is realistic because Karpathos is linked to Rhodes both ways: its airport has flights from Rhodes as well as Athens, and its port has ferries to Rhodes, Piraeus and Crete. The sensible shape is Rhodes first — five days for the Old Town, the west-coast castles and Lindos — then Karpathos for four days built around Apella, Olympos and the south. Book the Karpathos leg early; the island is small (6,200 people) and August rooms around the Olympos panigiri go months ahead.

Which one in September — and which one in August

September is perfect on both, for the same reasons: sea at peak warmth, wind dropping, crowds thinning. On Rhodes, Lindos begins to breathe in late September and the Old Town becomes walkable in daylight. On Karpathos, September is the reward for waiting — the hiking trails open up in cooler light and the beaches empty. If you have to pick one September, Karpathos edges it: that's the month it's built for, whereas Rhodes has another perfect month in June and stays good through November.

August splits them. Rhodes calls August a great month despite peak heat of 35°C-plus and a resort coast that's packed — the island is large enough (1,400 km², 115,000 people) that the interior villages and the west coast stay manageable. Karpathos rates August only "ok": the point of going then is the 15 August panigiri in Olympos and Othos, and you should book months ahead. Outside that festival, August on Karpathos means kitesurf wind and a full Pigadia. Families in August: Rhodes. Anyone who wants the panigiri: Karpathos, booked in spring.

Choose Rhodes for a first Dodecanese visit, history and archaeology, or any trip where you want serious infrastructure and easy access. Choose Karpathos for travellers who have already done the famous islands and want something authentically traditional — a place where you have to drive to find the best beaches, where the women in Olympos still bake bread in communal ovens, and where the wind makes the southern coast one of the world's premier windsurfing destinations. If you can manage both, fly into Rhodes and cross to Karpathos second — the contrast lands harder in that order.

Common questions

Can I do Karpathos and Rhodes in one trip?

Yes. Karpathos airport has flights from Rhodes as well as Athens, and Karpathos port has ferries to Rhodes, Piraeus and Crete. Do Rhodes first for five days, then Karpathos for four. Rhodes is the easy arrival — daily Blue Star from Piraeus and a big international airport — while the Piraeus ferry to Karpathos takes 17–20 hours and runs only two or three times a week, so use the Rhodes link rather than a separate Athens leg.

Which is cheaper, Karpathos or Rhodes?

Karpathos, slightly: affordability 3.8 against 3.5. Getting there costs about the same — €55–95 for either Piraeus ferry on deck, with Rhodes cabins at €100–180. Where Karpathos saves you money is on the ground: basic facilities at Apella and Kyra Panagia (one small taverna each), family tavernas like Ellinikon in Pigadia, and no resort strip. Rhodes runs every price band from Faliraki packages to Mavrikos in Lindos, so it's easy to spend far more.

Which is better for families?

Rhodes. Tsambika is 1.2 km of shallow golden sand with a very gradual entry, fully organised with showers and parking, and sheltered from the west wind; Agathi and Glystra are the quieter, equally shallow alternatives. Karpathos's best beaches, Apella and Kyra Panagia, are pebble, deep and served by a single taverna; Amopi near Pigadia is the family exception. Add the 1.5–2 hour mountain drive to Olympos and Rhodes wins for anyone with small children.

Which is better in September?

Both call September perfect, but Karpathos more so. The wind drops, the sea is at peak warmth, the trails from Olympos to Avlona and Aperi to Volada are at their best, and the beaches empty. On Rhodes, Lindos begins to breathe in late September and the Old Town becomes walkable in daylight. If you only get one September in the Dodecanese, take Karpathos; Rhodes gives you June and November as well.

How do I get from Rhodes to Karpathos?

Fly — Karpathos airport has flights from Rhodes — or take the ferry, since Karpathos port connects to Rhodes and on to Crete and Piraeus. Check the current timetable before you build the trip around it: the Karpathos line runs far less often than the daily Rhodes–Piraeus service. Rent a car at Pigadia port the moment you land; Karpathos scores 5.0 for car need and the best beaches and Olympos are only reachable by driving.
