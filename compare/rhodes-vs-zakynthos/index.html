Our verdict

The short answer: Two package heavyweights on opposite sides of Greece. Zakynthos (4.1) is the beach specialist: Navagio's shipwreck cove, turtle bays, and the Ionian's soft green light — beaches 4.8, history 2.5, and that ratio tells you the whole island. Rhodes (4.4) is the generalist: real medieval history (5.0), a longer season, ferry connections onward to Symi and Halki, and more to do when the wind blows. For a one-week beach holiday, especially a young group or a couple chasing swimming spots, Zakynthos delivers more per day. For anything longer, anything off-peak, or any traveller who gets restless on a sunbed by day three, Rhodes has the deeper reserves. Both need a car.

Choose Zakynthos if…

  • The shipwreck photo is non-negotiable. Navagio is reachable only by boat from Porto Vromi or Agios Nikolaos (20 minutes), and the viewpoint above it is a 40-minute drive north of town — in July and August, go at sunrise.
  • You're a young group who wants the night to start at midnight. The Laganas Strip is a kilometre of clubs — Rescue alone holds 2,000 people — and it earns its 4.5 nightlife score honestly.
  • You want turtles as part of the trip. Laganas Bay is a national marine park and the Mediterranean's main loggerhead nesting ground; boat trips from Laganas and Keri keep their distance, and Gerakas closes at sunset from June.
  • You're travelling in June or September. Both are "perfect" months on our calendar: sea warm, Navagio photographable without queueing boats, and Laganas either not yet started or already calming down.
  • You'd rather fly direct than change in Athens. Ryanair runs cheap summer flights from London and Manchester; the alternative is a three-hour drive to Kyllini and a one-hour ferry (€10–14).

Choose Rhodes if…

  • You want serious history, not a museum morning. The UNESCO Old Town, the Palace of the Grand Master and the Street of the Knights fill a full day; the Lindos Acropolis and ancient Kamiros fill two more.
  • You're travelling with small children. Tsambika is 1.2 km of golden sand with a very gradual entry, fully organised; Agathi and Glystra are the quieter versions further down the east coast.
  • You're going outside July–August. Rhodes' Old Town is at its best from October to April, the Medieval Rose Festival runs in October, and the airport keeps flying when Zakynthos winds down.
  • You'd get restless after three beach days. The west side has the Kritinia and Monolithos castles, the Valley of the Butterflies, and the wine village of Embonas under Mount Attavyros.
  • You want island-hopping options. Rhodes has a daily summer hydrofoil to Marmaris in about an hour and ferry connections onward to Symi and Halki; Zakynthos' only ferry is the shuttle from Kyllini.

Beaches: the one thing Zakynthos does better, and how much better

Zakynthos scores 4.8 for beaches against Rhodes' 4.2, and the gap is real. Laganas is 9 km long — the longest beach in the Ionian — and shallow enough for toddlers; Gerakas and Porto Zoro on the Vassilikos side are calmer and clearer; Banana Beach is the organised family option; and the north has Makris Gialos, Alykes, and the sulphur-warmed pebbles at Xigia. Then there is the west coast, which you see from a boat: Navagio, the Blue Caves at Cape Skinari, the sea caves under Keri. A four-day Zakynthos trip is basically a beach-and-boat schedule with one town morning for the Byzantine Museum and Bochali castle. That is the whole island, and for a beach week it is enough.

Rhodes' beaches are very good rather than exceptional, and they are concentrated on the sheltered east coast, which is calm almost every day. Tsambika is the best family beach on the island; Anthony Quinn Bay is the snorkelling cove; St Paul's Bay sits directly beneath the Lindos Acropolis, and it is hard to name a prettier swim in Greece. What Rhodes lacks is Zakynthos' drama — no shipwreck, no cliff-enclosed cove — and what it has instead is a beach for every day plus a castle above most of them. If your ideal day is towel, book, swim, repeat, Zakynthos wins on points. If it is swim, then something else, Rhodes wins outright.

Season, access, and the windy day

These islands cannot be combined. Rhodes is the far southeast of the Dodecanese, reached by direct international flights or the 13–18 hour overnight Blue Star from Piraeus (€55–95 deck, €100–180 cabin). Zakynthos is the far west, with no Piraeus ferry at all: you fly in on a summer charter or drive about three hours to Kyllini for the one-hour crossing (€10–14). There is no ferry between them and no reason to try — pick one and use the flight. What the map does decide is the season. Rhodes claims more than 300 days of sunshine a year, its Old Town is best from October to April, and even January is a "great" month with us. Zakynthos in January, February and December is merely "ok": mild, quiet, no swimming.

Then there is the wind question, and it favours Rhodes. Rhodes needs a car full stop (5.0), but that car takes you to Kamiros, to Kritinia, to Monolithos at sunset, to souma and thyme honey at Siana, to a long lunch at Mavrikos in Lindos, and to the Kallithea Springs on the way back. Zakynthos needs a car too (4.0), but the drive mostly delivers you to another beach or another boat. In August, when Lindos is rammed by 11am and Navagio's boats queue for the photo spot, Rhodes' interior villages and west-coast beaches stay manageable; Zakynthos' escape route is the east-coast villages and the mountain road through Volimes. Both survive August. Only one of them still has a full programme when the sunbed loses its appeal.

Choose Zakynthos for the perfect beach week — a young group, a couple chasing swimming spots, boat days and that shipwreck photo. Choose Rhodes for everything beyond it: longer trips, off-peak months, real medieval history, and a car that always has somewhere to go. Zakynthos' famous spots crowd brutally by 11am in August — go at opening time or by boat. Zakynthos for the perfect beach week, Rhodes for everything beyond it.

Common questions

Can I do Rhodes and Zakynthos in one trip?

No, not sensibly. Rhodes is in the far southeast Dodecanese, Zakynthos in the far west Ionian, and there is no ferry between them. Rhodes is reached by direct flights or the 13–18 hour overnight ferry from Piraeus; Zakynthos by summer charter flights or the one-hour ferry from Kyllini, about three hours' drive from Athens. Both have airports, so with two separate flights it is technically possible, but you would lose two days to transit. Pick one.

Which is cheaper, Rhodes or Zakynthos?

They score identically for affordability (3.5), so choose on other grounds. Getting to Zakynthos is cheaper if you drive: the Kyllini ferry is €10–14 against €55–95 deck on the overnight Piraeus–Rhodes ferry. Flights are the equaliser — both have Ryanair and charter traffic. On the ground, a farm taverna like Ampelostrates on Zakynthos is €25–30 a head, and Rhodes' Embonas tavernas do great-value spreads with the village wine. Call it a draw.

Which is better for families?

Rhodes. Tsambika is 1.2 km of golden sand with a very gradual entry, fully organised, and the east coast is sheltered almost every day; Agathi and Glystra are the quieter equivalents. Zakynthos has Banana Beach and Tsilivi for children, but its signature spots — Navagio, the Blue Caves — are boat trips, and Laganas is a party strip with marine-park rules that close the beach after dark from June to August. Rhodes also has the knights' castles for the windy day.

Which is better for nightlife?

Zakynthos, by a clear margin (4.5 vs 4.1). The Laganas Strip is a kilometre of full-on clubs — Rescue holds 2,000, Cherry Bay has run thirty years, Zeros does foam parties — and nothing starts before midnight. Rhodes' Bar Street on Orfanidou in the new town, plus Faliraki 14 km south, is loud enough, and the Old Town's candle-lit wine bars are the grown-up alternative. If the night is the point of the trip, Zakynthos.

Which is better in October?

Rhodes, easily. October is a "great" month on Rhodes: last swims, the Old Town emptying of cruise groups, and the Medieval Rose Festival inside the walls, typically on the second weekend. Zakynthos in October is also "great" for last swims, but the island is built around beaches and boats, and once Laganas closes down there is less to do. Rhodes' 300-plus sunshine days and its inverted second season give it the deeper October.
