Our verdict

The short answer: Poros edges it overall, 3.5 to 3.4, but they are answering different questions. Agistri is the better swim — 3.5 for beaches against 3.0 — and the better value at 4.0 against 3.2, and it is the only island in the Saronic where you genuinely need no vehicle at all. Poros is the better place to spend an evening: a proper town wrapped around a clock tower, a strait two hundred metres wide with the Peloponnese on the other side, and a culture score of 4.2. Swim on Agistri. Stay on Poros.

Choose Agistri if…

  • Swimming is the priority. Dragonera, Aponisos and Chalikiada are clearly better water than anything on Poros.
  • You want no car and no scooter. Agistri scores 1.0 for car reliance — 13 km², all of it walkable or cyclable.
  • You want the cheaper trip. 4.0 against Poros's 3.2 on affordability is the biggest single gap between them.
  • You want fewer people and less town. Agistri has 1,142 residents against Poros's 3,993, and it feels quieter than that ratio suggests.
  • You are coming for two or three days of doing very little in pine shade.

Choose Poros if…

  • You want a town with an evening in it. Poros town climbing the rock under its clock tower is one of the prettiest small harbours in Greece, and the waterfront is alive after dark.
  • You like the geography: the strait to Galatas is barely 200 m wide, so you sit at dinner watching cars on the mainland. Nowhere else feels like this.
  • You want the lemon forest — Lemonodasos, thousands of trees on the Peloponnese slope opposite, reached by a five-minute boat.
  • You want pine to the water at Love Bay and Russian Bay, which are lovely even if the sand is not Agistri's.
  • You are sailing, or want to be near boats — Poros is a charter and flotilla hub.

Water: Agistri wins clearly

3.5 against 3.0, and the difference is visible from the ferry. Agistri's coast is pine down to pebble down to genuinely transparent water — Dragonera on the north shore is the one people photograph, Aponisos in the southwest has turquoise shallows and a wadeable islet, and Chalikiada under the cliff is the dramatic option. Skala's beach is sandy and organised for families.

Poros swims perfectly well but not memorably. Askeli is the main organised beach, Love Bay and Russian Bay are small pine-fringed coves that are prettier than they are practical, and Monastiri sits below the monastery. It is a green, sheltered, comfortable coastline rather than a spectacular one.

Town: Poros wins clearly

Poros town is the reason to choose it. The old part climbs a volcanic hump above the strait, capped by the 1927 clock tower, and the whole thing reflects in water two hundred metres from mainland Greece. You can eat on the quay watching the little ferries shuttle across to Galatas every few minutes. It is a working town of nearly four thousand people with a naval training tradition, and it has a night-time life that Agistri's Skala — pleasant, low-key, a few bars — does not attempt.

Culture scores 4.2 against Agistri's 2.5, which is the largest gap in the comparison. Poros has the sanctuary of Poseidon (ruined, sparse, but historically serious — Demosthenes died here), the Archaeological Museum, and the lemon forest across the water. Agistri, honestly, has pine trees and swimming. That is not a criticism; it is the entire proposition.

Money and logistics

Agistri is the cheaper island by a clear margin — 4.0 against 3.2 — which shows up in rooms more than in food. Poros carries yacht-charter prices in the harbour restaurants.

Both are easy from Athens. Poros is about an hour on the hydrofoil from Piraeus, slightly less than Agistri's direct fast boat, and it also has the odd advantage of being reachable by car via Galatas if you drive down through the Peloponnese. Agistri usually means Piraeus or a hop via Aegina. Access scores 4.8 and 4.5 respectively — both fine.

The honest verdict

For a day trip in high summer where the point is to get in the sea, Agistri, without hesitation — better water, cheaper, and no transport to arrange once you land. For a weekend, Poros, because two days needs somewhere to spend the evenings and Poros town genuinely delivers that while Skala does not. For families with small children, Agistri: shallow organised sand at Skala and no roads to worry about. For couples wanting dinner and a view, Poros. And if you have four days, the two combine well — they are ninety minutes apart on the same Saronic network, and the contrast between the pine-and-swim island and the harbour town is the trip.
