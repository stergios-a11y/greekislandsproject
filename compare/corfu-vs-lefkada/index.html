Our verdict

The short answer: Corfu, 4.2 to 4.0, and the two anchor opposite ends of the Ionian and opposite ideas of a holiday. Corfu wins on culture 4.8 to 2.5 — the largest gap in this comparison and one of the largest on the site — with a UNESCO Old Town that was never taken by the Ottomans and therefore looks Italian. Lefkada wins on beaches 4.9 to 3.9, which is nearly as large a gap the other way, and you drive onto it across a bridge. Corfu is the rounded trip. Lefkada is the beach trip.

Choose Corfu if…

  • You want a real town. Corfu's Old Town is UNESCO-listed: two Venetian fortresses, the arcaded Liston built under the French, St Spyridon, and a cricket ground on the Spianada left by the British.
  • Culture is the point — 4.8 against 2.5. Corfu was never under Ottoman rule, and four centuries of Venice followed by France and Britain is why nowhere else in Greece looks or eats like this.
  • You want an evening. Nightlife 4.2 against 3.2, and it ranges from Old Town wine bars to Kavos, which is its own thing entirely.
  • You want the easiest arrival in the Ionian — access 4.7, with direct flights from most of Europe all summer.
  • You want green. Corfu is the wettest, greenest Ionian island, with millions of Venetian-planted olives, Mt Pantokrator, the 220 km Corfu Trail and the restored ghost village at Old Perithia.

Choose Lefkada if…

  • Beaches decide it — 4.9 against 3.9, a full point. Porto Katsiki, Kathisma and Egremni are the most dramatic stretch of coast in Greece.
  • You do not want a ferry or a flight. Lefkada is bridged to the mainland; you drive straight on.
  • You want the cheaper trip — 4.0 for affordability against Corfu's 3.2, a real gap on rooms and dinner.
  • You windsurf, or you sail. Vassiliki is a world-class wind bay, and Meganisi, Kalamos, Kastos and Skorpios are all just offshore.
  • You are road-tripping western Greece and want the coast without the logistics.

Corfu's case is depth

The 4.8 for culture is the second-highest we give any island, and it is not padding. The Old Town is a genuine Venetian city — two fortresses, tall shuttered lanes called kantounia, and the Liston arcade modelled on the Rue de Rivoli. The reason it survives is that Corfu held out against the Ottomans when the rest of Greece did not, so the layers are Venetian, then French, then British, and each left something specific: the olive groves, the arcades, and cricket on the Spianada.

Outside town there is the Achilleion, built for Empress Elisabeth of Austria; Mon Repos, where Prince Philip was born; Vlacherna and Pontikonisi in the lagoon; Paleokastritsa's monastery above its bays; Angelokastro on its crag; and Old Perithia, a mountain village that emptied and is being brought back one taverna at a time.

Then the food, which is the underrated part. Venetian rule left a cuisine no other island has — sofrito, pastitsada, bourdeto — plus kumquat, which arrived with the British. And if you can travel at Easter, Corfu's is the most extraordinary in Greece: the philharmonic bands, and the clay pots thrown from balconies on Holy Saturday morning.

Lefkada's case is the coastline, and it is overwhelming

4.9 against 3.9 is the widest beach gap in any Ionian pair here. The west coast is one continuous wall of white limestone with water beneath it that photographs as though it has been edited. Porto Katsiki is the famous one; Kathisma is the long organised alternative; Egremni is the largest, though the 2015 earthquake destroyed its stairway and access has been unreliable since — the boat from Nydri or Vassiliki is the dependable route. Agios Nikitas is the one village on that coast worth basing in, and Milos beach below it is a short walk or a five-minute water taxi.

Corfu's beaches are good rather than great and the score is fair. Paleokastritsa's coves, Porto Timoni's double bay, Glyfada, Agios Georgios and the sculpted channel at Sidari are all worth a day; none is Porto Katsiki.

Cost and crowds

Corfu is markedly more expensive — 3.2 against 4.0 — and it is also much busier, with 102,000 residents and Europe's charter fleet arriving all summer. Lefkada's 22,600 people and drive-in access mean the crowd is more Greek and more seasonal, concentrated in August.

The honest verdict

Corfu for a first Ionian trip, or any trip where you want a town, history, food and gardens with beaches as a bonus rather than the point. Lefkada if the beach is the reason you are going, if you are driving down the west of the mainland anyway, or if you sail — and it will cost you less. The useful test: if you would happily spend a whole day without swimming, Corfu is the better island. If you would not, Lefkada is, and the 4.2-to-4.0 total is beside the point.
