Our verdict

The short answer: The Athenian weekend dilemma: both under three hours from Piraeus, both cheap by Cycladic standards (3.8 affordability), both blissfully undeveloped, neither with an airport. Serifos edges it overall (3.9 vs 3.5) — but Kythnos has its own case. Kythnos's signature is Kolona, the double-sided sandbar you've seen in every drone reel, plus the thermal springs at Loutra, the Katafyki cave and Dryopida's tiled roofs. It's the closest real Cycladic island to Athens, which makes it busy with Greek boats in August and pleasantly local otherwise. Serifos brings the more dramatic chora — one of the great Cycladic sights — better and more numerous beaches (4.5 vs 4.2), and a slightly more "discovered" but still unspoiled feel. On everything else — nightlife 3.0, access 3.5, car need 3.0 — they tie exactly.

Choose Kythnos if…

  • You want the shortest possible escape. Conventional ferries from Piraeus and Lavrio reach Merichas in about 2.5–3 hours, daily, and Lavrio is closer to the airport than Piraeus — a 30-minute saving if you're flying in.
  • Hot springs are the point. Loutra's water comes out at 52°C and has been bathed in for two thousand years; the 19th-century spa hotel sells day passes, and the springs make March and October real months here.
  • You want Kolona at sunrise. The 150-metre white sandbar joins Kythnos to the Agios Loukas islet, so you swim on two sides at once — one canteen in summer, no umbrellas, and the day boats arrive later.
  • You're travelling with small children. Episkopi on the south coast is 300 m of shallow, protected sand with a taverna and rooms behind it — a local favourite that stays calm.
  • You like an island with an inland life. The Chora (Messaria) has lanes too narrow for cars, Dryopida has red-tiled roofs and a folk museum, and the Katafyki mine still holds the workers' rusted tools.

Choose Serifos if…

  • The chora is your reason to travel. Serifos's climbs a volcanic cone like a staircase of white cubes; the Venetian castle ruins at the top are a 15-minute climb and the place to be at sunset.
  • Beaches decide it. Psili Ammos — 250 m of fine sand in a perfectly arced, east-facing bay — has been voted among Greece's best; Agios Sostis, ten minutes on by dirt track, is nearly empty.
  • You want the post-industrial edge: Megalo Livadi's abandoned mining harbour and the 1916 strikers' monument, the semi-abandoned Mega Chorio, and the 10th-century basilica at Panagia built on an older temple.
  • You can spare the extra hour. The Seajets fast ferry from Piraeus does it in about 2 hours, the conventional in about 4 — and it's the same Western Cyclades line as Sifnos and Milos.
  • You'll eat where the island cooks: revithada baked overnight in a sealed clay pot (Stou Stratou on the Chora square, Takis on Agios Sostis beach), and manoura cheese aged in wine lees.

Ferry logistics from Athens — the extra hour

This is a weekend decision, so start with the boat. Kythnos is the easier of the two: conventional ferries from both Piraeus and Lavrio dock at Merichas in roughly 2.5–3 hours, daily, for €20–35, and Lavrio is the port to use if you've flown into Athens. Serifos is €32–50 from Piraeus: about 2 hours on the Seajets fast ferry, about 4 on the conventional, daily in summer. That is the owner's "extra hour", and it's real — on a Friday-to-Sunday trip an hour each way is a quarter of your Saturday. Neither island has an airport, so the ferry is the whole story.

Doing both in one weekend isn't something either guide's timetable supports — Kythnos lists one main ferry link, and Serifos's natural pairings are Sifnos and Milos further down the Western Cyclades line — so treat them as two separate trips rather than a loop. The arrival is different too. At Merichas you rent a car or scooter on the spot and leave the same hour; there's almost nothing to keep you in the port. At Livadi, most rooms, the car hire and the bus stop for the Chora are within five minutes' walk of the ferry, and Livadakia beach is five minutes further. Serifos is the island you can do for a day without wheels; Kythnos isn't.

June or September — and never August

The two calendars agree on the big thing: go in June or September, and avoid August. On Kythnos, August is "peak Athenian boats" — the harbour fills with weekenders and Kolona is shared with the day boats. On Serifos, August is the meltemi peak, and July already closes the north coast to swimming; Livadakia and Psili Ammos are sheltered, Agios Ioannis on the northeast is not. September is "perfect" on both — warm sea, emptier beaches — and late June is Serifos's other best window, with the dramatic light the island is known for.

Where the calendars split is at the edges of the season. Kythnos has a year-round asset in the Loutra springs: March is "great" (mild, springs), October is "last swims plus springs", and even November and December are "ok" for a soak and a quiet walk. Serifos, by contrast, is wrapping up fast by November and almost everything is closed by mid-October. So the honest split is this: for a summer weekend, Serifos's chora and beaches justify the hour; for an off-season weekend — a cold March, a late October — Kythnos is the only one of the two that's properly open.

Choose Kythnos for the shortest escape, hot springs, and Kolona at sunrise before the day boats. Choose Serifos if you can spare the extra hour — the chora and the beaches justify it. Either way, go June or September; both islands are at their best without the August Athens exodus.

Common questions

Can I do Kythnos and Serifos in one trip?

Not as a loop — neither guide lists a direct crossing between them. Kythnos has one main ferry link (Piraeus and Lavrio to Merichas), and Serifos's natural pairings are Sifnos and Milos on the Western Cyclades line. Treat them as two separate weekends: Kythnos when you want the shortest escape or an off-season soak at Loutra, Serifos when you have a summer weekend and can spare the extra hour.

Which is cheaper, Kythnos or Serifos?

On the island they tie — both score 3.8 for affordability, both are deliberately thin on infrastructure, both have tavernas where the family cooks. The difference is the ferry: Kythnos is €20–35 from Piraeus or Lavrio, Serifos €32–50 from Piraeus. For a couple on a weekend that's a small but real saving in Kythnos's favour, before you've spent a cent on the island.

Which is better for families?

Kythnos. Episkopi on the south coast is 300 m of shallow, protected sand with a taverna and rooms behind it, Kolona is shallow on both sides, and Apokrousi next door has a beach bar and umbrellas. Serifos has a good family beach in Livadakia — five minutes from the port, tamarisk shade, two tavernas — but the Chora is a steep climb on a volcanic cone, which the island itself flags as a reason to skip it.

Which is better for a car-free trip?

Serifos. At Livadi the rooms, the car hire and the bus stop for the Chora are within five minutes' walk of the ferry, Livadakia beach is five minutes further, and the Chora is a bus ride and a climb. Kythnos's own guide says to skip it if you won't rent a car or scooter — the best beaches need wheels, and there's almost nothing to keep you in Merichas. Both score 3.0 for car need, but Serifos is the one that works on foot for a day.

Which is better in October?

Kythnos, clearly. October there is 'last swims plus springs' — the sea still holds and the 52°C water at Loutra makes a cool evening the point rather than a problem. On Serifos almost everything closes by mid-October and the island is wrapping up fast by November. If your weekend is in the first half of October, Serifos is still swimmable; from the second half onwards, Kythnos is the only one properly open.
