Our verdict

The short answer: A genuine tie on the numbers — both 3.9 — and the only real tie between any two Evia regions. They get there differently. Central wins culture 4.5 to 3.5 and access 5.0 to 4.5, because Chalkida is joined to the mainland by a bridge and Eretria is the best ancient site on the island. South wins beaches 4.6 to 3.5, which is the largest single gap between them, and it wins the hiking. Central Evia is the region you drive through and look at things. South Evia is the one you go to and swim.

Choose Central Evia if…

  • You do not want a ferry. The Chalkida bridge is the only road onto Evia — access 5.0 against 4.5, and about an hour from Athens by car.
  • You want ancient Greece. Eretria has the theatre, the House of Mosaics and a good museum; culture scores 4.5 against 3.5.
  • You want the Evripos strait — the tidal current that reverses several times daily and that Aristotle could not explain.
  • You want an evening out. Chalkida's 120,000 residents support real restaurants and bars; nightlife 3.5 against South's 2.5.
  • You want Dirfys at 1,743 m, beech forest, and the highest summit on the island.

Choose South Evia if…

  • You came to swim, full stop. 4.6 for beaches against 3.5 — the east coast under Ochi is a different class of water.
  • You want the best hike on Evia: the Dimosari Gorge from Ochi down to the sea at Kallianou.
  • You want the Dragon Houses, megalithic slab structures on Ochi that remain genuinely unexplained.
  • You want Karystos — a planned neoclassical town under a Venetian sea-fort, with the best fish tavernas on the island.
  • You want emptiness. 42,000 people across 780 km² of granite, gorse and wind.

Two regions, two completely different trips

This is not a case of one being a better version of the other. Central Evia is populous, cultivated, mountainous and connected — 120,000 people, 1,700 km², a bridge to the mainland, and a chain of things to visit: Chalkida's waterfront and the strange strait beside it, ancient Eretria half an hour west, the pilgrimage church at Prokopi inland, Kymi on its east-coast balcony, and Dirfys rising behind all of it with marked trails from Steni.

South Evia is 780 km² of hard granite tapering to the Kafireas strait, with 42,000 people and one real town. It is bare where Central is green, windy where Central is sheltered, and the coastline is the entire argument.

The beaches decide it for most people

4.6 against 3.5 is not a close call. South Evia's east coast — Kallianou, Potami, Agios Dimitrios, Giannitsi, Thapsa — faces open Aegean at the bottom of steep empty valleys, and the water is cold, deep and completely clear. Marmari and Bouros on the west are the sheltered alternatives for meltemi days. It is the best swimming on Evia and comfortably competitive with named Cycladic islands, at a fraction of the attention.

Central Evia's beaches are decent and convenient rather than special: the Eretria and Amarynthos strip is organised and family-friendly, Chiliadou on the north coast is the dramatic exception and worth the drive, and Kymi's shore below the town is pleasant. Fine for a swim between sights, which is exactly the right way to use them.

Culture and access decide it for the rest

If your trip is built around driving from Athens with limited days, Central is materially easier. No ferry booking, no queue, no schedule — you cross the bridge and you are on the island. Karystos requires the Rafina–Marmari ferry, which is only an hour and runs several times daily, but it is still a boat.

And Central genuinely has more to look at. Eretria was a significant Archaic and Classical city, with real excavated remains and a museum that does them justice. Add the Evripos current — worth watching once, if only because Aristotle wrote about it and got nowhere — plus the Prokopi pilgrimage and the Dirfys trails, and 4.5 for culture against South's 3.5 is honest. South's cultural case rests on the Venetian fort at Karystos, the drakospita and the small museum, which is interesting but thinner.

The honest verdict

Both score 3.9 and both deserve it. Go south if the holiday is about the sea, walking, and having a coastline largely to yourself — that is the more distinctive experience and the one most visitors miss. Go central if you have two or three days, no ferry patience, and want variety: a town, a mountain, an ancient city and a swim, all within an hour's drive. With a week, do both, in that order — Chalkida and Eretria on the way down, then the long drive south to Karystos, which is about three hours and one of the better road trips in Attica's hinterland.
