Our verdict

The short answer: Agistri, and it is not close — 3.4 against 2.8, the lowest overall score of any island this near Athens. The gap is almost entirely water: Agistri scores 3.5 for beaches, Salamina 2.0. Agistri is a pine-covered rock of 1,142 people where you swim in clear water all day. Salamina has 39,283 residents, a naval base, shipyards along the eastern shore, and the strait where the Greek fleet broke the Persians in 480 BC. One is a holiday. The other is a genuinely interesting half-day that happens to be an island.

Choose Agistri if…

  • You came to swim. Dragonera with pine trees to the waterline, Aponisos with its turquoise channel, Chalikiada under the cliff — this is the best swimming within an hour of Athens.
  • You want no car. Agistri scores 1.0 for car reliance, the lowest we give: 13 km², walk or rent a bike and you have seen it.
  • You want quiet with a pulse. Skala has a handful of bars, so evenings are not silent, but nothing here is loud.
  • You want a genuine overnight rather than a day out. Two nights on Agistri is a real short holiday.
  • You are happy paying a little more — 4.0 for affordability against Salamina's 4.5, which in practice is a small difference.

Choose Salamina if…

  • You have half a day and want the Battle of Salamis where it happened. Standing on the west coast looking across the strait is the point, and no other island near Athens offers that.
  • You want the cheapest possible escape — 15 minutes on the ferry from Perama, a couple of euros, no planning.
  • You want Faneromeni Monastery, which is genuinely worth the drive, with 17th-century frescoes and almost no visitors.
  • You are curious about a working Greek island that tourism never reached — 39,000 people who live and commute, not a resort.
  • You will drive to the west coast for Kanakia or Peristeria, which are the only beaches here worth the trip.

Why Salamina scores 2.8

Being honest about this matters, because Salamina gets searched more than Agistri and the numbers say the opposite of what that implies. It scores 2.0 on beaches — the eastern and northern coasts face the industrial Saronic Gulf, with shipyards, the naval base and the shipping lanes into Piraeus. Those shores are not for swimming and no amount of goodwill changes that. The west and southwest — Kanakia, Peristeria — are genuinely pleasant, sandy and pine-backed, but they are a drive away and they are all there is.

What Salamina does have is history that actually happened here. The 480 BC naval battle that stopped the Persian invasion of Greece was fought in the strait you can see from the shore. There is a decent archaeological collection, the Faneromeni monastery is a real building with real frescoes, and the island has never been prettified for visitors. If you find working places more interesting than pretty ones, that is a genuine argument.

Agistri is the swimming answer

Thirteen square kilometres, pine down to the sea, and water that is transparent in a way the inner Saronic mostly is not. Dragonera is the picture everyone takes — pebbles, pines, deep clear water. Aponisos in the southwest has the turquoise shallows and a tiny islet you can wade towards. Chalikiada, under the cliff near Skala, is the dramatic one and unofficially clothing-optional. Skala's own beach is sandy and organised, which is what you want with children.

None of it needs a car. That is unusual and it changes the trip: you arrive on foot, you walk or hire a bike, and the whole island is available. Athenians have known this for decades, which is why Agistri fills at summer weekends and empties on Monday.

Getting there

Salamina is the closer by a wide margin — the Perama ferry crosses in about 15 minutes and runs constantly, which is why it functions as a suburb. But Perama itself is a 40-minute drive or bus from central Athens, so door-to-door the advantage narrows.

Agistri is roughly an hour from Piraeus on the fast boat, or via Aegina on the slower route. Both islands score well on access (4.5) but for different reasons: Salamina because it is next door, Agistri because the Saronic ferry network is dense and reliable.

The honest verdict

If you have a day and want to swim, go to Agistri; the beach scores are 3.5 against 2.0 and that is the whole comparison. If you have half a day, an interest in the Persian Wars, and no intention of getting in the water, Salamina is a better use of the time than most people realise — and it is almost free to reach. What you should not do is go to Salamina expecting an island holiday. It has 39,000 residents, a shipyard and a naval base, and it is honest about being a place where people live rather than visit. Agistri is the holiday. Salamina is the field trip.
