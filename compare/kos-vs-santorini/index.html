Our verdict

The short answer: These attract the same charter flights and completely different travellers. Kos (3.7) is the easy Dodecanese package: flat, cyclable, with the longest continuous stretch of sandy beach in the Aegean (4.0), Hippocrates' Asklepion for a culture morning, and honest prices (3.8). Santorini (4.8) is the world-famous one — caldera, wine, Akrotiri, the architecture everyone has already seen — at world-famous prices (1.0), with beaches that are volcanic curiosities rather than swimming destinations (3.2). Kos is scored for four days, Santorini for three. Both have international airports with heavy summer charters. The decision is almost entirely financial, and almost entirely about what you want the fortnight to feel like.

Choose Kos if…

  • You're travelling with children. Tigaki is 10km of fine sand with a cycle path running its full length, Lambi is 2km north of Kos Town with every facility, and the island is flat enough to move around on a rented bicycle.
  • You want two easy weeks of sun rather than three nights of spectacle. Kos is scored at four days but absorbs far more: the south coast at Paradise and Camel, Zia at 350m for sunset, the Asfendiou villages for lunch.
  • Money is a real constraint. 3.8 affordability against Santorini's 1.0 — a week on Kos costs roughly what three nights on the caldera do, hotel for hotel.
  • You want a volcano without the price tag. Day cruises from Kardamena give you five hours on Nisyros, 20km south, walking down into an active crater of steam vents and yellow sulphur.
  • You want a culture morning, not a culture week. The Asklepion is 4km from town by bicycle or mini-train — three terraces where Hippocratic medicine was taught, with views across the strait to Turkey.

Choose Santorini if…

  • The caldera photograph is the dream and you've decided to pay for it. Nothing on Kos comes close to the Fira-to-Imerovigli cliff path or the 360° view from the chapel on Skaros Rock.
  • Three nights is all you have. Santorini's scored stay is three days and it's built for exactly that: caldera walk, volcano boat, a winery afternoon, Akrotiri on the way to the airport.
  • You care about wine. Santo Wines outside Pyrgos pours tasting flights from €25 — twelve wines with small plates — and the 10:00 slot is much quieter than the sunset scrum.
  • Bronze Age archaeology moves you. Akrotiri, buried by the Minoan eruption around 1600 BCE and preserved under ash, is €12 and an hour; its frescoes hang in the Museum of Prehistoric Thera in Fira.
  • It's a honeymoon or an anniversary and the point is the set piece. Kos doesn't do set pieces; it does long, good, unremarkable beach days.

The money question decides most of these

The affordability gap — 3.8 against 1.0 — is the widest kind of gap our board produces, and it isn't a rounding error. Kos's accommodation stock is charter and all-inclusive, which holds prices down even in August; on Santorini the caldera view is the product, and everything from a coffee in Fira to a sunbed is priced accordingly on 76km² of very finite cliff. The owner's rule of thumb holds: a week on Kos costs roughly what three nights on Santorini do, hotel for hotel. That isn't quite like-for-like, and it shouldn't be — it's a different holiday, and the honest question is which shape you actually want your time off to take.

Transport is the second cost, and it runs the other way from what you'd guess. Kos scores 4.0 for car-need but you can dodge much of it: the town is perfectly flat, bicycles are rented at the harbour, the Asklepion is 4km out, and Tigaki's cycle path runs the length of the beach. Only the south coast — Paradise, Camel, Agios Stefanos and Kefalos, about 50 minutes' drive from town — genuinely requires a car, and the Nisyros cruise leaves by boat from Kardamena. Santorini's 3.0 reflects a small island where buses and taxis reach Fira, Oia, Pyrgos and Akrotiri; a taxi from Imerovigli down to Fira Old Port is ten minutes and about €15.

Doing both, and which one in September

Both in one trip is a flight, not a ferry. Kos is Dodecanese, Santorini Cycladic, and neither island's getting-there notes list a direct connection: Kos is 11–14 hours from Piraeus on the Blue Star overnight (€50–160), Santorini about 5 hours by fast ferry or 7–8 conventional (€40–90). Both have international airports, so the only sane combination routes through Athens — and at that point you've spent a day of a two-week holiday in airports. If it's a volcano you want on a Kos trip, Nisyros does it in a day from Kardamena. If it's a long sandy beach you want on a Santorini trip, you're on the wrong island: Perissa is the best of the black-sand strands, Vlychada is the best for actually swimming, and Red Beach is a viewpoint with a rockfall problem.

Timing separates them more sharply than the scores do. Santorini's July and August are marked avoid outright — three or four cruise ships a day, the Oia castle unusable at sunset, prices at maximum. Kos in the same months is still "great" but package-heavy: Tigaki, Kardamena and Mastichari fill up with charter crowds. September is the answer for both, for opposite reasons. Santorini's cruise season tapers, the sunset spots become breathable by the second week, and prices halve. Kos gets a calmer second half with the sea at peak warmth and the Old Town and Asklepion at their best. If your dates are fixed to July or August and your budget is finite, that alone points to Kos.

Santorini for the bucket list, Kos for the actual holiday. If this is the year's one big trip and the caldera photo is the dream, take Santorini and don't look at the receipts — three days is what it's scored for and three days is what it needs. If you want two easy weeks of sun with kids or a bicycle, Kos delivers exactly that, and you can day-trip to Nisyros' volcano for your geological drama at a fraction of the cost.

Common questions

Can I visit both Kos and Santorini in one trip?

Not comfortably. Kos is in the Dodecanese and Santorini in the Cyclades, and neither island's ferry information lists a direct link — Kos is 11–14 hours from Piraeus, Santorini 5–8. Any combination routes through Athens by air, which costs you the better part of a day each way. With two weeks it's doable; with one it isn't worth it. Pick the one that matches your budget.

Which is cheaper, Kos or Santorini?

Kos, dramatically: 3.8 affordability against Santorini's 1.0, the lowest score on our board. The working rule is that a week on Kos costs about what three nights on Santorini do, hotel for hotel. Kos's charter and all-inclusive stock keeps prices down even in August, while Santorini prices the caldera view into every coffee, sunbed and dinner table.

Which is better for families with children?

Kos, easily. Tigaki gives you 10km of fine sand with a cycle path along its whole length and a flamingo wetland behind it; Lambi is 2km north of Kos Town with full facilities and calm shallow water. The island is flat enough for bicycles. Santorini's beaches are black and red volcanic, its villages are built on cliff steps, and its prices punish a family.

Which is better for a honeymoon?

Santorini, and it isn't a close call. The caldera path from Fira to Imerovigli, the 360° view from Skaros Rock, a Santo Wines tasting flight with the volcano below and dinner in Oia are set pieces Kos simply doesn't attempt. Go in late September, when prices halve and the sunset spots become breathable — that's the version worth paying for.

Does Santorini actually have good beaches?

Not by Kos standards — 3.2 against 4.0, and the island's own guide says it isn't primarily a beach island. Vlychada, under wind-carved ash cliffs, is the best for actual swimming; Perissa is the finest long black-sand strand; Red Beach is really a viewpoint, with rockfall risk and a partly restricted path. Kos answers with Paradise, Agios Stefanos and 10km of Tigaki.
