Our verdict

The short answer: The two most photographed islands in Greece, photographing completely different things. Santorini (4.8) is the caldera: a geological drama with wine and architecture on top, unbeatable for 2–3 days, brutally expensive (affordability 1.0) and not really a beach island (3.2). Corfu (4.2) is a green, lived-in island with an aristocratic old town, forested hills, and enough coastline variety for two weeks — at three-fifths the price (3.2). The honest framing: Santorini is an experience, Corfu is a holiday. Honeymoon or once-in-a-lifetime trip with money set aside — Santorini, and stay somewhere with the view because the view is the product. Family week, mixed group, or any trip where the budget matters — Corfu, no contest. Combining them doesn't work; they're on opposite sides of the country with no direct link.

Choose Santorini if…

  • The view is the trip. The caldera path from Fira to Imerovigli is 1.5 km and thirty minutes, and Skaros Rock below Imerovigli gives 360° views that beat anything else on the island.
  • You're coming for 2–3 nights, not a fortnight. Our itinerary is three days — caldera, volcano boat tour, wine and Akrotiri — and there is no fourth; the island doesn't refresh.
  • You care about Bronze Age archaeology. Akrotiri, the "Pompeii of the Aegean" (€12), and the Minoan frescoes at the Museum of Prehistoric Thera (€6) are a genuine 5.0 for culture.
  • You want wine with the scenery. Santo Wines outside Pyrgos has the best caldera views of any winery on the island; tasting flights start at €25 — book the 10:00 slot.
  • You can travel in June or late September — the only months we mark "perfect" — and you've accepted that July and August are "avoid", with three or four cruise ships a day.

Choose Corfu if…

  • The budget has to survive a week. Corfu's benchmark taverna, Klimataria tou Bellou at Benitses, feeds you bianco and bourdeto for under €25 a head; Santorini's Roza's runs €50–75.
  • You want a real beach rota. Glyfada's kilometre of golden sand, Paleokastritsa's six coves, Roda and Acharavi for shallow family water, Porto Timoni if you'll hike 45 minutes from Afionas.
  • You're travelling as a family or a mixed group. Corfu is big enough that Kavos parties, Sidari does karaoke, and the olive-grove interior stays quiet — everyone gets their own version.
  • You like a town you can live in. The Liston, the Spianada (the largest square in the Balkans), two Venetian fortresses, and the Museum of Asian Art — a UNESCO old town with a working population.
  • You're coming overland from Europe. Ferries from Bari, Ancona and Venice land on Corfu; from the mainland, Igoumenitsa is a frequent 1.5-hour shuttle (€11–18).

What the money actually buys

Affordability 1.0 against 3.2 is the widest gap on this site, and it is worth being literal about it. On Santorini the view is the product and everything near it is priced accordingly: the caldera-side hotels, the €25 Aperol in a sunset queue in August, a benchmark dinner at Metaxi Mas that needs booking days ahead, Roza's in Vourvoulos at €50–75 a head. Even the cheap things are not free — €5 to walk Nea Kameni, €30–45 for the five-hour volcano boat, €12 for Akrotiri, €25 to taste at Santo Wines. Our own advice is to stay on the caldera in Imerovigli, because a Santorini trip without the view is the worst of both worlds. Budget for three nights done properly, not seven done badly.

Corfu prices like a holiday island rather than a destination. Rex, off the Liston since 1932, and the Venetian Well are the grand tables; Klimataria tou Bellou at Benitses does the island's Corfiot classics for under €25 and is open all year. The Old Fortress, the Achilleion and Angelokastro cost entry money but not caldera money, and the beaches are free apart from a sunbed. The crossing from Igoumenitsa is €11–18. At three-fifths the price, a Corfu week costs roughly what a Santorini long weekend does, and you get a full five-day itinerary plus two days you haven't planned. That is the trade: fewer set-piece moments, many more ordinary good ones.

Beaches, and the two-week question

Santorini's beaches are volcanic curiosities rather than swimming destinations, and our score (3.2) says so. Vlychada, with its wind-carved ash cliffs, is the best beach on the island for actually swimming; Perissa is 3 km of hot black sand under Ancient Thera; Red Beach is spectacular from the overlook, but rockfalls are real and the access path has been partly restricted since 2023. None of this is a reason to come, and after day three the island does not refresh — which is why we give it three days and the honeymoon. Corfu scores 3.9 for beaches, and more importantly it has variety: west-coast sand at Glyfada and Agios Gordios, Mirtiotissa if you'll walk 30 minutes through the olive groves, the sandstone channels of the Canal d'Amour, the six coves of Paleokastritsa with a monastery above.

That variety is what fills a fortnight. Our Corfu itinerary is five days and moves base three times — Corfu Town, Paleokastritsa, Sidari — and still leaves out most of the south. Doing both islands in one trip does not work: Santorini sits in the southern Cyclades with an airport and a five-hour fast ferry from Piraeus; Corfu sits off the northwest mainland with an airport and the Igoumenitsa shuttle. There is no ferry between them and no route that isn't a day lost. If you genuinely want caldera plus a green island, Santorini's own guide points to Crete — a daily two-hour fast ferry to Heraklion. Corfu is the answer to a different question: where do I go for the holiday itself?

Choose Santorini for the spectacle — a honeymoon or once-in-a-lifetime trip with money set aside, and stay somewhere with the view. Choose Corfu for the stay — a family week, a mixed group, or any trip where the budget matters. Santorini for the spectacle, Corfu for the stay.

Common questions

Can I do Corfu and Santorini in one trip?

No — not as one journey. Corfu is off the northwest mainland, reached by air or via Igoumenitsa; Santorini is in the southern Cyclades, reached by air or a 5–8 hour ferry from Piraeus. There is no ferry between them and no direct link. Both have international airports, so with separate flights it can be done, but you spend a travel day each way. If you want the caldera plus a second island, Santorini pairs with Crete: a daily two-hour fast ferry to Heraklion.

Which is cheaper, Corfu or Santorini?

Corfu, by a wide margin: affordability 3.2 against Santorini's 1.0, the lowest on the site. Corfu's benchmark taverna, Klimataria tou Bellou, is under €25 a head; on Santorini, Roza's is €50–75 and Santorinia in Fira €30–50. The Igoumenitsa ferry is €11–18; Piraeus–Santorini fast ferries are €60–90. Santorini's prices halve after late September, but even then Corfu is the cheaper week — and the cheaper fortnight.

Which is better for a honeymoon?

Santorini, if the money is set aside. The caldera, Oia at sunset, a terrace in Imerovigli with both villages in view, dinner by the water at Ammoudi Bay — these are the set-piece moments, and nothing on Corfu matches them. Corfu is the better honeymoon only if you want two weeks rather than three days: swim the Canal d'Amour together (local legend says marriage follows), hike to Porto Timoni, and eat at the Venetian Well.

Which is better for families?

Corfu, no contest. Roda and Acharavi are long, shallow, calm north-coast beaches; Glyfada has water sports and full facilities; Sidari has a resort promenade for the evenings. The island is large enough that families can avoid Kavos entirely. Santorini's beaches are black or red volcanic sand, Red Beach has rockfall warnings and a partly restricted path, and the caldera villages are steps and cliff edges. Three days on Santorini works with older children; a week does not.

Which is better in September?

Both are "perfect" in September, and for the same reason: the August crowds ease and the sea is at its warmest. On Santorini the change is dramatic — cruise season tapers, sunset spots are breathable by week three, prices halve. On Corfu the charters thin, prices soften, and the green interior recovers from the heat. If you can only manage one September trip, Santorini gains the most from the month; Corfu is simply good all summer.
