Our verdict

The short answer: Hydra for an atmospheric short break — the harbour, the silence, the absence of cars. Spetses for a longer stay, families, and beach time. The numbers favor Hydra slightly overall (4.0 vs 3.7), but the islands solve different problems and Spetses is the easier "real holiday" option. Both are reachable from Athens in under two hours, both have a refined Athenian summer-house tradition, but where Hydra pursues a single concept (no cars, ever), Spetses offers more conventional comforts.

Choose Hydra if…

  • You want a singular, atmospheric experience — the harbour, the bell tower, the cobbled streets, the donkeys instead of cars. There is nothing else like it in Greece.
  • You're going for 2-3 nights, not a full week. Hydra rewards short, immersive stays.
  • You're traveling as a couple or with friends who appreciate the walking-everywhere ethos.
  • The artistic and intellectual layer matters — Leonard Cohen lived here, the school of Greek fine arts has a summer base here, and the island has been a refuge for painters and writers for sixty years.
  • You're combining it with Athens (a 1.5 hour fast ferry) for a city-and-island week.

Choose Spetses if…

  • You want better beaches without sacrificing the Saronic-from-Athens convenience. Spetses scores 2.8 vs Hydra's 2.2 — modest gap, but in practice the difference is real.
  • You're traveling with kids, older parents, or anyone for whom walking-everywhere is not appealing.
  • You're staying 4-7 nights. Spetses absorbs a longer stay better — more places to eat, more beaches to rotate through.
  • You want refined comforts: the old harbor, Anargyrios school grounds, well-regarded hotels like the Poseidonion.
  • You'd consider mainland day trips. Spetses has water-taxi access to Costa Navarino-tier Peloponnese beaches across the strait.

The car-free question

This is the single most important question for choosing between them, more than beaches or food. Hydra has banned all cars, motorbikes, and even bicycles (the last by community choice, not law). Transport is by donkey, by foot, by sea-taxi around the coast. The result is one of the quietest harbour towns in the Mediterranean — no engine noise, no scooter horns, no parking arguments. For most visitors this is magical for three days. For some, the lack of mobility becomes constraining: you cannot quickly drive to a quieter beach, you cannot easily explore the interior, and luggage management at arrival and departure involves donkey porters.

Spetses takes a middle path. Cars are restricted in the main town but allowed for residents and on the rest of the island. Most visitors get around by bicycle, scooter, or horse-drawn carriage. The town has the same beautiful, walkable quality as Hydra without the absolute prohibition. You can rent a quad bike for €30/day and visit the southern beaches that you'd need a sea taxi (€40-80) to reach on Hydra.

Beaches: a draw, with caveats

Neither island is a "beach destination" in the way Milos, Paros, or Naxos are. Both score in the lower-middle range (Hydra 2.2, Spetses 2.8). What they have are small pebble coves rather than long sandy stretches.

Hydra's best beaches — Bisti, Agios Nikolaos, Vlychos, Mandraki — are reached either by walking (Vlychos, 25 minutes from the port; Mandraki, 20 minutes) or by sea taxi (Bisti, Agios Nikolaos). The water is clear, the swimming is good, but you'll never have a long lazy beach day; the beaches are too small for that. Most visitors plan one beach session per day rather than spending the whole day on sand.

Spetses's beaches are more accessible. Agia Marina is a 15-minute walk from the town and works as the easy default. Agii Anargyri (the island's most beautiful beach, with two organized tavernas) and Zogeria (quieter, partly nudist) are reachable by bike, scooter, or the island's circular bus. The southern coast has a string of small coves with reasonable swimming and pine-shade. For a family that actually wants beach days, Spetses delivers; Hydra requires you to want the harbour more than the swimming.

The feel of each town

Hydra's harbour is one of the great urban harbours of Greece. A natural amphitheatre of stone mansions rising up the hillside, painted in restrained whites and ochres, with a hidden cannon-emplacement above. Cohen's house is on a small alley above the port. The Lazaros Koundouriotis mansion (now a museum) and the Hydra School of Fine Arts give the town an intellectual layer that other Saronic islands lack. Dining options are fewer than Spetses but generally higher-quality — Pirate, Sunset, and the upscale Techne stand out. The crowd skews Athenian-cultured, art-world, and well-traveled European. Many visitors return year after year.

Spetses's town is broader, more comfortable, less concentrated. Dapia (the main square and old harbor) is the social center; the Old Harbor on the other side is where the better restaurants and the marina sit. Architecture is whitewashed-with-blue, more conventionally Aegean than Hydra's stone-mansion style. The Poseidonion Grand Hotel is an old-school institution; smaller boutique hotels like Orloff Resort and Klimis offer good mid-range options. The crowd is wealthier-Athenian rather than internationally cultured — second-home owners on summer rotation, mixed with European visitors. The overall mood is gentler, more familial.

Logistics and cost

Both islands are reached by fast ferry (Flying Dolphin or Cat) from Piraeus's Gate E8. Hydra is about 1.5 hours; Spetses about 2 hours, sometimes via Hydra. Tickets €30-45 each way. No airport on either island — so the trip starts in Athens. This makes both ideal for the start or end of a Greek vacation rather than a destination in themselves.

Hydra is the more expensive island. Hotels in high season run €180-450 a night for mid-range and €600-1200 for the top boutique places. Restaurants run €40-70 per person. Spetses is meaningfully cheaper — mid-range hotels €120-280, restaurants €30-50. For a four-night stay for two, expect Spetses to cost €500-1000 less than Hydra all-in.

One Spetses-specific cost worth knowing: scooter/quad rental is €25-40/day and almost mandatory if you want to use the island's beaches. Budget for it.

How long should you stay?

Hydra works best as a 2-3 night trip. The town can be walked through in an hour, the museum visited in a morning, one or two beach trips done across two days, and dinner sampled across two or three evenings. After 4 nights the lack of variety begins to show; the island doesn't have enough alternative destinations for a week.

Spetses absorbs 4-7 nights comfortably. The town deserves two evenings, the southern beaches need a couple of days, a day for the Old Harbor and museum, and possibly a day trip by water taxi to Porto Heli or the Peloponnese mainland. A week is genuinely full without feeling stretched.

The honest verdict

The compass test: do you want the trip to be defined by the place itself, or by what you do there? Hydra is the former — the experience is being in Hydra, walking its streets, sitting at its harbor, hearing the donkey bells in the morning. Spetses is the latter — the island is the setting for what you do, which is beach days, dinners, and gentle activities. Both are valid choices and both will reward the right visitor. The mistake is picking based on Instagram ("Hydra looks more dramatic") rather than honest self-knowledge ("I will be bored after two days without a beach"). The data says most travelers who can only choose one should choose Spetses — easier, cheaper, longer-stay friendly, more family-suitable. But for visitors with the right temperament, Hydra is the unforgettable choice. If you have time in Greece, the cleanest answer is to do both: two nights on Hydra, four on Spetses, with the contrast as part of the trip.

Common questions

Is Hydra or Spetses better for a weekend trip?

Hydra, by a clear margin. The car-free harbor, the architectural intensity, and the walking culture pay off most in a short, immersive stay. 2-3 nights on Hydra is the sweet spot — you absorb the place fully without exhausting its variety. Spetses is better for longer stays of 4+ nights where the additional infrastructure and beach options come into play.

Can you visit Hydra and Spetses in the same trip?

Yes — and many travelers do. Both are on the same Flying Dolphin / Cat fast ferry route from Piraeus, and ferries between them run multiple times daily in summer (about 45 minutes apart). A common 5-7 night Saronic itinerary is 2 nights Hydra, 3-4 nights Spetses, ending with the return to Athens. Going in that order gives you the dramatic-but-tiring Hydra experience first and the more comfortable Spetses second.

Which has better beaches, Hydra or Spetses?

Spetses, but neither is a beach destination. Spetses scores 2.8 vs Hydra's 2.2 — the gap is real, primarily because Spetses's beaches are accessible by bike/scooter while Hydra's require walking or expensive sea taxis. If beaches matter to you as a primary trip element, neither island is the right choice — go to Milos, Paros, or Naxos instead. If you want a Saronic trip and beaches are secondary, Spetses delivers more beach time.

Are there cars on Spetses?

Limited cars, yes. Spetses bans cars in the main town but allows them on the rest of the island (for residents and rentals). Most visitors use bikes, scooters, quad bikes, or horse-drawn carriages within town. Hydra, by contrast, bans all cars, motorbikes, and bicycles — transport is by donkey or on foot. This is the single biggest practical difference between the two islands.

How long is the ferry from Athens to Hydra and Spetses?

From Piraeus (Gate E8), Hydra is about 1.5 hours by Flying Dolphin or Cat fast ferry. Spetses is about 2 hours, often via Hydra. Fares are €30-45 each way. Service runs 4-6 times daily in summer, less frequent off-season. Book tickets ahead in July and August as ferries sell out, especially Friday and Sunday.
