Our verdict

The short answer: An hour apart in the Saronic, and a clean trade: Hydra (4.0) is the icon with the price tag; Poros (3.5) is the easy weekend nobody brags about. Hydra is unique in Greece — no cars at all, a preserved stone amphitheatre of a harbour town, donkeys doing the freight, art history from Leonard Cohen to the DESTE Foundation. It's also expensive (1.8 affordability), beach-poor (2.2; rock platforms mostly), and heaving with day-trippers at noon. Poros is softer: a green pine channel opposite the Peloponnese, a clock-tower town, Love Bay for a decent swim (3.0), family tavernas — and prices a working Athenian actually pays (3.2). Both are quick fast-ferry runs from Piraeus: Poros barely an hour (4.8 access), Hydra about an hour and a half (4.2).

Choose Hydra if…

  • You're staying at least one night. The harbour at twilight, after the day boats have gone, is the Hydra you came for — dinner at Gitoniko, then a drink at Hydronetta out on the cannons above the sea.
  • You swim off rocks happily. Spilia's platforms are five minutes from the harbour, with cliff-jumping; Vlychos is a 30-minute coastal walk past a 17th-century stone bridge, with Taverna Marina right behind the pebbles.
  • Art and history are the reason. The Historical Archive Museum tells the War of Independence story, Cohen's house is at No. 10 Merkouraki lane, and the DESTE Foundation shows each summer.
  • You can travel with one light bag. Nothing rolls on Hydra — donkeys carry luggage from the port to your hotel, you tip for it, and a heavy suitcase becomes someone else's problem, expensively.
  • You're going in June or September. June has the galleries open and the donkey paths walkable; September has the yachts thinning. August is the month to avoid — taverna prices doubled, Vlychos and Bisti packed.

Choose Poros if…

  • You want sand and shade. Love Bay is a 150 m sandy cove ten minutes from town, ringed by pine, with sunbeds, a beach bar and a taverna, and calm shallow water that works for small children.
  • You're doing a day trip. Poros is about an hour from Piraeus by fast ferry (€15–20), and the one-day route — clock tower, Temple of Poseidon, Love Bay — fits between a morning and an evening boat.
  • Budget matters. Poros scores 3.2 for affordability against Hydra's 1.8, and there's a conventional ferry at about 2.5 hours if you'd rather save more than time.
  • You want the Peloponnese too. Galatas sits about 200 metres across the strait — visible from the harbour, minutes away by boat — with lemon groves behind it that are the reason to come in March.
  • You're a family that wants zero logistics. Poseidon on the seafront has fish from its own boats, Joy Cocktail Bar covers the sunset from 6pm, and nothing on Poros requires a mule.

The car-free question decides it

Hydra scores 1.0 for car need because the number is meaningless: there are no cars, no scooters, ever. You move on foot, by donkey or by water taxi, and every plan bends around that. Vlychos is the last beach reachable on foot, 30 minutes along the coast path; Bisti, the pine-fringed pebble bay on the west coast where the diving club operates, is a 20-minute water taxi or beach boat from the harbour and nothing else. Mandraki, the island's only sandy beach, is a 20-minute walk east and belongs to a resort with resort prices. The Two-Day Itinerary covers 5 km on day one — all of it walked.

Poros scores 3.0 and the difference is felt immediately. The one-day route covers 15 km with about 25 minutes of driving: up through the lanes to the 1920s blue-domed clock tower, out to the 6th-century BC Temple of Poseidon in its pine forest — where Demosthenes took poison rather than surrender to Macedon — then down to Love Bay for the afternoon. Nothing on Poros is remote and nothing needs a boat. That is the trade in one line: Hydra's constraint is the product, and it costs you convenience and money; Poros's convenience is the product, and it costs you the singularity. For 2–3 nights the constraint is a pleasure. For a Sunday with a pushchair it is not.

Doing both, and when to go

They combine easily, and in the right order. Poros departs for Piraeus, Hydra or the other Saronic islands with frequent connections; Hydra's ferry back to Piraeus takes about 90 minutes and also serves the rest of the Saronic. So the loop is Piraeus to Poros (about an hour), a morning on Poros for the clock tower and a swim at Love Bay, the afternoon boat across to Hydra, one or two nights there, and the 1.5-hour catamaran home. Do it that way round rather than the reverse: Poros is the warm-up and Hydra is the finish, and Hydra after the day-trippers leave is worth the overnight in a way Poros is not.

On timing they agree. May and June are perfect on both, September too. On Hydra June is the realistic best month — day-trippers manageable, the donkey paths to Vlychos and Kamini walkable in daylight, the artists' galleries open — and the last weekend of June brings Miaoulia, the fireboat burned in the harbour, the most spectacular event in the Saronic. September is the reward month, yachts thinning and the Spilia platforms back to locals. August is different: Hydra is avoid, its small harbour overflowing with megayachts, while Poros is merely ok, peak weekends with Athens on the boat. Both are busy on weekends year-round because both are Athenian favourites; Poros is never overwhelming, Hydra can be. Easter is Hydra's — the Epitaphios carried into the sea on Saturday is unforgettable.

The honest verdict

Choose Hydra for romance, art, and the single most beautiful harbour arrival in Greece — and stay overnight, to have it after the boats leave. Choose Poros for an unpretentious family weekend with swimming, shade and zero logistics, at prices that don't need a special occasion. If you have two nights and a spare morning, do both in that order — Poros for the swim, Hydra for the night. Cohen pilgrims: there's only one answer.

Common questions

Can I do Hydra and Poros in one trip?

Yes, and it's the best way to see them. Poros has frequent connections to Piraeus, Hydra and the other Saronic islands; Hydra's boat back to Piraeus takes about 90 minutes. Do Piraeus to Poros (about an hour) for a morning at the clock tower and a swim at Love Bay, cross to Hydra in the afternoon, stay one or two nights, and take the 1.5-hour catamaran home. Poros first, Hydra second — Hydra is the one worth the overnight.

Which is cheaper, Hydra or Poros?

Poros, clearly. It scores 3.2 for affordability against Hydra's 1.8. The ferry alone tells the story: Poros is €15–20 from Piraeus on the fast boat, with a cheaper conventional ferry at about 2.5 hours, while Hydra is €25–35 on the catamaran with no cheaper option. On Hydra you also tip the donkey that carries your bag, and in August taverna prices double. Poros is the island a working Athenian actually pays for.

Which is better for families, Hydra or Poros?

Poros, without hesitation. Love Bay is a 150 m sandy cove ten minutes from town, sheltered by pine-covered hills, with calm shallow water, sunbeds, a beach bar and a taverna. Hydra has one sandy beach, Mandraki, a 20-minute walk east and part of an expensive resort; everything else is rock platforms or a water taxi. Add luggage carried by donkey and stairs everywhere, and Hydra with small children is work. Save Hydra for a couple's night.

Which is better for a car-free trip?

Both are car-free in practice, but differently. Hydra bans cars and scooters outright — you walk, ride a donkey or take a water taxi, and Bisti is only reachable by a 20-minute boat. Poros scores 3.0 for car need because its one-day route covers 15 km, but the town, the clock tower and Love Bay (ten minutes from town) all work on foot. If car-free is the point, Hydra is the purer version; if it's simply a preference, Poros is easier.

Which is better in September, Hydra or Poros?

Hydra. September is its reward month — the August megayachts thinning, weekenders winding down, the donkey paths walkable in daylight again, the cliff-jumping platforms at Spilia back to locals. Poros in September is also perfect and calmer than summer, but Poros is good in any shoulder month; Hydra in September is the island at its best and cheapest relative to August. If you have one September weekend for the Saronic, spend it on Hydra and stay the night.
