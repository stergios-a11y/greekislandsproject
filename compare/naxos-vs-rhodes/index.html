Our verdict

The short answer: These are both big, self-sufficient islands where a week fills itself — the choice is about what fills it. Rhodes (4.4) is history first: the medieval Old Town is the best walled city in Greece, Lindos is spectacular, and the resort machine along the east coast means flights, rooms and rental cars at every price. Naxos (4.5) is the better Greek holiday: the west-coast beach strip (Plaka, Mikri Vigla) beats anything on Rhodes, the mountain villages are living places rather than excursions, and food comes from the island itself — potatoes, graviera, citron. Nightlife and access favour Rhodes (4.1 vs 3.5; direct international flights year-round). Beaches and affordability favour Naxos (4.8 vs 4.2, and noticeably cheaper).

Choose Naxos if…

  • You're travelling with young children. Plaka, Agios Prokopios and Kastraki are flat white sand with water that stays shallow a long way out, and Agios Prokopios is ten minutes from town with sunbeds, tavernas and summer lifeguards.
  • You want beaches without a long drive. The whole west-coast strip sits within 25 minutes of Naxos Town; on Rhodes, Glystra and the Prasonisi sandbar in the south are an hour or more from the capital.
  • Food is part of the plan. PDO graviera, Kitron liqueur from the Vallindras distillery in Halki, slow-cooked patatato in Apeiranthos, meat by the kilo at Stou Vasilarakiou in Kinidaros — the island feeds itself.
  • You hike. Mount Zas, at 1,001 m the highest peak in the Cyclades, is a three-hour return walk through fir forest past the Cave of Zeus; Halki and Apeiranthos are the marble villages on the way.
  • You're island-hopping. Paros is 30 minutes away, Piraeus 3.5 hours by fast ferry, and the domestic airport takes Athens flights — Naxos is a hub, Rhodes is an end point at 13–18 hours from Piraeus.

Choose Rhodes if…

  • You want to fly straight in from home. Rhodes has the largest airport in the Dodecanese — Aegean, Sky Express, Ryanair and charters — while Naxos has a small domestic airport and the Piraeus ferry.
  • History is the point. The Palace of the Grand Master, the Street of the Knights, the Acropolis of Lindos above its 116-metre cliff, Ancient Kamiros — Rhodes scores 5.0 for culture and earns it.
  • You're travelling outside summer. The Old Town is best from October to April; the guide rates January "magical" and November "empty". Naxos in those months is hiking weather and closed tavernas.
  • You want a proper night out. Bar Street (Orfanidou) in the new town, Macao and Sissitio inside the walls, Faliraki 14 km south — Rhodes's 4.1 for nightlife beats Naxos's Paralia bar-hop at 3.5.
  • You want sheltered swimming in a meltemi summer. Tsambika, Anthony Quinn Bay, Agathi and St Paul's Bay all face east, protected from the west winds, with calm water all day.

The car question decides the shape of the week

Both islands need a car, but not equally: Naxos scores 4.0, Rhodes 5.0. On Naxos the town does a lot of the work. Agios Georgios beach is a walk from the Kastro, the bus runs to Agios Prokopios, the Portara is at the end of a causeway, and the four-day itinerary never covers more than 60 km in a day — the mountain loop to Halki, Panagia Drosiani, Apeiranthos and Mount Zas is 80 minutes of driving in total. A family could rent a car for two days and skip it for the other two.

Rhodes is 1,400 square kilometres with 115,000 people, and the distances are real. The southern day — Glystra, Siana, Monolithos Castle — is 140 km and two and a half hours behind the wheel, with a 90-minute drive back to Rhodes Town in the dark. Kritinia Castle is an hour out; even Lindos is a 90-minute driving day down the east coast. Without a car Rhodes shrinks to the Old Town and Elli beach, which is a fine long weekend but not the island the 4.4 score describes.

Doing both, and which one in September

Nothing in either guide's ferry notes links Naxos and Rhodes directly. Naxos sits on the Piraeus and Rafina lines, 3.5 hours by fast ferry and 5–6 by Blue Star, and its neighbours are Paros and the Cyclades. Rhodes is 13–18 hours from Piraeus on the overnight boat — €55–95 on deck, €100–180 in a cabin — and the guide's own advice is that a direct flight is faster and costs about the same as the cabin. If you want both in one trip, treat them as two separate legs through Athens: fly into Rhodes, fly Rhodes–Athens–Naxos, and ferry out of Naxos. It works for two weeks; it is a waste of a single week.

On timing the two agree more than you'd expect. June and September are "perfect" on both — Naxos warm with the beaches emptying, Rhodes with the sea at peak warmth and Lindos beginning to breathe in late September. They split in August: Naxos drops to "ok" as the meltemi howls across Plaka and the villages bake, while Rhodes stays "great" despite 35°C-plus heat because the interior and west coast absorb the crowds. And they split again in winter, where Rhodes has an Old Town season Naxos cannot answer — the Medieval Rose festival on the second weekend of October, November with the lanes empty. Naxos's equivalent is 15 August, when the Filoti panigiri runs three days into the small hours.

Choose Naxos for beaches and authenticity; choose Rhodes for history and a longer season. Families torn between them: Naxos, unless the kids are old enough to be bribed with a knights' castle.

Common questions

Can I do Naxos and Rhodes in one trip?

Only with two weeks and two flights. Neither guide lists a direct ferry: Naxos is 3.5 hours from Piraeus by fast ferry, Rhodes is 13–18 hours on the overnight boat, and the two sit on different lines. The practical route is Rhodes first by direct flight, then Rhodes–Athens–Naxos by air, and the ferry out of Naxos at the end. For a single week, pick one.

Which is cheaper, Naxos or Rhodes?

Naxos. It scores 4.0 for affordability against Rhodes's 3.5, and the owner's verdict calls it "noticeably cheaper". Getting there is cheaper too: €38–55 from Piraeus by ferry versus €55–95 on deck or €100–180 in a cabin to Rhodes — though a direct flight to Rhodes from abroad can undercut the boat. On the island, Naxos's food comes from its own farms, which keeps taverna prices honest.

Which is better for families?

Naxos, unless the kids are old enough to be bribed with a knights' castle. Plaka, Agios Prokopios and Kastraki are long flat white-sand beaches with water that stays shallow far out, ten to twenty-five minutes from town. Rhodes's answer is Tsambika — 1.2 km of golden sand, east-facing and calm all day, fully organised — plus the Palace of the Grand Master and the Street of the Knights for older children.

Which is better in September?

Both are rated "perfect" in September, so decide on what you want to do. Naxos in September is warm with the beaches emptying and the mountain villages comfortable again. Rhodes has the sea at peak warmth and Lindos breathing again by late September. If you might slip into October, Rhodes wins: the Medieval Rose festival is on the second weekend and the Old Town is at its best off-season.

Do I need a car on Naxos or Rhodes?

On Rhodes, yes — it scores 5.0 for car need, the southern loop is 140 km and even Lindos is a 90-minute driving day. Without one you are confined to the Old Town and Elli beach. On Naxos (4.0) you can manage two days without: Agios Georgios is walkable, the bus reaches Agios Prokopios, and the Portara and Kastro are in town. Rent for the mountain villages and Mount Zas.
