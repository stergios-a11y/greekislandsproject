Our verdict

The short answer: Central Evia scores higher, 3.9 to 3.6, and it wins on the two things that decide most trips: you can drive there without a ferry at all, and it has the culture. Chalkida is joined to the mainland by a bridge over the Evripos strait — the current that reverses four to six times a day and defeated Aristotle — with ancient Eretria half an hour away and Mount Dirfys, the island's highest peak, behind it. North Evia is the cheaper, softer, more thermal option: Loutra Edipsou's hot springs have been in use since antiquity, and affordability scores 4.8, the highest of the three regions. Central for interest, North for rest.

Choose Central Evia if…

  • You do not want a ferry at all. The Chalkida bridge means you drive straight on — access scores 5.0, the only region of Evia that does.
  • You want ancient sites. Eretria was a major Archaic city with a museum and the House of Mosaics; culture scores 4.5 against North's 3.5.
  • You want mountains to walk. Dirfys reaches 1,743 m, the highest point on Evia, with Steni as the trailhead village.
  • You want an evening. Chalkida has 120,000 people, a real waterfront, and the only nightlife on the island worth the word — 3.5 against 2.5.
  • You are coming for a weekend from Athens and want the shortest possible drive: Chalkida is about an hour.

Choose North Evia if…

  • You want thermal water. Loutra Edipsou is one of the oldest continuously used spas in Europe — Aristotle wrote about these springs, Sulla came for them, and they still run hot into the sea.
  • You want the cheapest base. 4.8 for affordability, the best value on Evia and among the best in Greece for a place this green.
  • You want the better beaches of the two: 3.8 against 3.0. Rovies, Pefki, Agia Anna and the long stretch at Agios Georgios are properly swimmable.
  • You want Lichadonisia — the cluster of tiny islets off the northwest tip, shallow and startlingly turquoise, reached by boat from Kavos.
  • You want quiet. North Evia scores 2.5 for nightlife and that is exactly the appeal.

The bridge is the real difference

Evia is the second-largest Greek island and the only major one you can reach without a boat. That happens at Chalkida, where two bridges cross the Evripos — the old sliding bridge at the narrowest point and the newer suspension bridge to the south. The current in the strait reverses direction several times a day and has never been fully explained by any simple model; Aristotle studied it and, according to legend, drowned in frustration. He did not, but the water genuinely does behave strangely and it is worth ten minutes of watching.

North Evia needs a ferry: Arkitsa to Edipsos takes about 45 minutes and runs frequently, or Glyfa to Agiokampos further north. Neither is difficult, but it is a booking, a queue and a schedule. If you are travelling with a car and want zero friction, Central wins before you have compared anything else.

What Central actually offers

Ancient Eretria is the headline: one of the significant cities of Archaic Greece, with a theatre, a palace, the House of Mosaics and a good small museum, sitting quietly in a modern seaside town that almost nobody visits for the ruins. Prokopi, inland, holds the relics of Saint John the Russian and is one of the most-visited Orthodox pilgrimage sites in Greece — worth seeing for the phenomenon even if you are not religious. Kymi on the east coast is called the balcony of the Aegean for obvious reasons once you get there.

And there is Dirfys. At 1,743 m it is a serious mountain with a refuge, marked trails from Steni, and beech and chestnut forest that looks nothing like anyone's mental image of a Greek island. If you like walking, this is the reason to choose Central over anywhere else on Evia.

What North offers, and one honest caveat

North Evia is the softer, greener, cheaper half — thermal springs, pine forest running down to pebble beaches, and prices that have not moved much. Loutra Edipsou is a genuine spa town with faded grand hotels and hot water arriving at around 40°C; you can sit in it where it meets the sea for nothing at all. Rovies, Limni, Agia Anna and Pefki are all pleasant low-key bases.

The caveat: the 2021 wildfires burned a very large area of northern Evia's forest, and while the coast and the villages were largely spared and regrowth is well under way, some inland stretches still look scarred. It is honest to expect that rather than be surprised by it. The affected areas also make North the region most in need of visitors, which is a reason of a different kind.

The honest verdict

For a first trip, or any trip built around driving from Athens and seeing things, Central Evia — no ferry, the best ancient site on the island, the best mountain, and the only real town. For a slow week of swimming, thermal water and low bills, North Evia, which is cheaper (4.8 to 4.6) and has better beaches (3.8 to 3.5). Note that neither is the beach answer on Evia: that is the south, at 4.6. Central and North are competing over what you do between swims, and on that Central is more interesting while North is more restful.
