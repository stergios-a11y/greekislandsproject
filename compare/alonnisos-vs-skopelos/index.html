Our verdict

The short answer: The quiet end of the Sporades, one short ferry hop apart, and a choice between two shades of calm. Skopelos (3.6) is the postcard; Alonnisos (3.8) is the retreat. Skopelos has the architecture — its chora is among the loveliest towns in the Aegean, 123 churches climbing above a Venetian harbour — plus the Mamma Mia chapel, pine forests, and a fuller taverna scene. Alonnisos has the water: it sits inside Europe's largest marine park, home of the monk seal, and its bays have a clarity even the Cyclades struggle to match (4.4 beaches against 4.2). Both are ferry-only (2.5 access) via Skiathos or Volos, both are cheap by island standards (4.0 vs 3.8 affordability), and both want three days. The difference is what you do with the days.

Choose Skopelos if…

  • The town matters to you. Skopelos Town climbs from a Venetian harbour to a kastro with one surviving church inside; Alonnisos's Patitiri is a relaxed port, and its old Chora, abandoned after the 1965 earthquake, is lovely but small.
  • You want beaches with a taverna at the back. Panormos is a pebble bay enclosed by pine forest with a good taverna; Stafylos is 4 km from town with sunbeds. Alonnisos's best beach, Agios Dimitrios, has no facilities.
  • You're a Mamma Mia pilgrim. The Agios Ioannis chapel — 112 steps cut into the rock on the northwest coast — is the wedding scene. Go early, before the tour buses, then lunch at Agnanti in Glossa, open since 1953.
  • You want more to see in three days. Evangelistria monastery in the pines above town, the Folklore Museum with its plum products, Glossa's hilltop square, Limnonari down a 20-minute forest path — Skopelos is 96 km² to Alonnisos's 64.
  • You're coming in September for the plums — Skopelos calls it its best month. Alonnisos's September is also perfect, but for the trails and the seal boats, not for a harvest.

Choose Alonnisos if…

  • Snorkelling and water clarity are the trip. The marine park is Europe's largest protected sea area; the full-day boat from Patitiri stops at Peristera's deserted sandy beaches and Gioura, with about 40 monk seals in the surrounding waters.
  • You want the empty beach. Agios Dimitrios — white sand, turquoise water, rough road or boat only — is almost always empty. Leftos Gialos is the accessible version with a small taverna; Chrisi Milia the organised one.
  • You walk. The paths from the Old Chora to Megalos Mourtias are at their best in June and September, and the island is small enough (64 km²) for a scooter from Patitiri — car need 3.0 against Skopelos's 4.0.
  • You want the deeper quiet. Alonnisos scores 2.5 for nightlife, the same as Skopelos, but there's no Mamma Mia bus-tour stream and Patitiri fills only at dinner. Even in July the coves at Megalos Mourtias and Tzortzi Gialos stay quiet.
  • You'd rather skip Skiathos entirely. Alonnisos is the end of the line; ferries are fewer, and that's what keeps it as it is. Come in June for the first seal-spotting boats, or September when they return to small groups.

Ferry logistics: the same line, one stop further

Neither island has an airport, and both score 2.5 for access. You come from Volos or Agios Konstantinos on the Sporades line, which calls at Skiathos, then Skopelos, then Alonnisos — about 3.5 to 5 hours in total to Skopelos, with Alonnisos one hop further. Fares on both sit in the €20–35 band. The practical consequence: Skopelos is the easier island to reach and to leave, because it sits in the middle of the chain; Alonnisos is the terminus, ferries are less frequent, and you check the timetable before you commit. In the shoulder months that matters — Skopelos marks December through March as limited, and Alonnisos's December, January and February are effectively closed.

Combining them is trivially easy — they're 30 minutes apart on the same boats, and both guides say so: Skopelos tells you to combine with Skiathos and Alonnisos, Alonnisos tells you to combine with Skopelos and skip Skiathos. The right order is Skopelos first and Alonnisos second, so you end the trip at the quiet end and don't backtrack past it. Three days each: Skopelos Town, Agios Ioannis and Panormos, then Evangelistria and Limnonari; cross; Patitiri and the Old Chora, the marine park boat (book at the harbour the day before), then Agios Dimitrios and Leftos Gialos on the way out.

August is the real difference

In June and September the two islands are near-identical in mood — both list both months as perfect — and you choose on the beach-versus-town question above. August is where they split. Skopelos marks August as a month to avoid: peak crowds, heat, and the Mamma Mia tours at full flow since July. Alonnisos rates August "ok" rather than "avoid", but it's honest about why: the island fills, marine-park boats are fully booked, prices hit their maximum, and a 64 km² island in August feels more like Skiathos than the retreat it is for the rest of the year. The marine park itself stays uncrowded — that's why it exists.

So the rule: if August is your only window, Alonnisos, and book the boat before you arrive. If you can move, go to either in June — Skopelos warm and before the buses, Alonnisos with the seal boats just starting and the Chora paths walkable — or in September, when Skopelos has its plums and Alonnisos has golden afternoon light on the trail down to Megalos Mourtias. October still works on both for last swims, and it's the month the seal boats and the plum stalls have both gone quiet.

Choose Skopelos for the prettier town, more variety, and easier logistics. Choose Alonnisos for snorkelling, marine-park boat trips, hiking mule paths, and the deepest quiet in the Sporades. Combining them is trivially easy — they're 30 minutes apart — and for a week, Skopelos then Alonnisos is the answer that satisfies both halves of this question.

Common questions

Can I do Alonnisos and Skopelos in one trip?

Yes, and you should — they're 30 minutes apart on the same Sporades ferry line from Volos or Agios Konstantinos, and both guides recommend the pairing. Do Skopelos first and Alonnisos second, since Alonnisos is the end of the line and you don't want to backtrack past it. Three nights each is right: both islands are rated for three days. The Alonnisos guide adds that with this combination you can skip Skiathos entirely.

Which is cheaper, Alonnisos or Skopelos?

Alonnisos, marginally: affordability 4.0 against 3.8, and ferry fares are the same €20–35 band for both. Alonnisos also needs less vehicle — car need 3.0, a scooter from Patitiri covers the 64 km² island — whereas Skopelos scores 4.0 and you'll want a car for Agios Ioannis, Glossa and Panormos. The one caveat is August, when Alonnisos prices hit their maximum and the marine-park boats sell out. Outside August, both are cheap by Aegean standards.

Which is better for families?

Skopelos. Stafylos is an organised pebble beach with a taverna 4 km from town, Panormos has sunbeds and a taverna behind it, and Skopelos Town itself is walkable and lively. Alonnisos's beaches are wilder — Agios Dimitrios has no facilities and a rough road — with Chrisi Milia, shallow and warm with a hotel and taverna behind it, the family exception. For older children who snorkel, though, the Alonnisos marine-park boat day is the best single day in the Sporades.

Which is better for a car-free trip?

Alonnisos. Car need is 3.0 against Skopelos's 4.0; you rent a scooter at Patitiri, Roussoum and Chrisi Milia are close to the port, and the headline day — the marine park, Peristera, Gioura and seal-spotting — is by boat anyway. On Skopelos the Agios Ioannis chapel, Glossa and Panormos are on the far side of a 96 km² island, and the guide assumes a car. If you won't drive, Alonnisos is the cleaner choice.

Which is better in September?

Both call September perfect, so decide on what kind of trip it is. Skopelos names September its best month — the plum harvest, warm sea, the buses gone. Alonnisos's September is the charter season winding down, seal-spotting boats back to small groups, and golden afternoon light on the path from the Old Chora to Megalos Mourtias. Town and food: Skopelos. Water and walking: Alonnisos. If you're doing both, September is the ideal month for the loop.
