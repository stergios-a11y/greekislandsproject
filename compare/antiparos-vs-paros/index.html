Our verdict

The short answer: Paros, 4.1 to 4.0, and they are seven minutes apart by ferry — but the gap in the numbers hides how differently they behave. Paros holds a 5.0 for beaches and a 5.0 for nightlife, one of very few islands with both, and it is also one of the most expensive places on this site at 2.2 for affordability. Antiparos is a single Chora around a 15th-century Venetian castro, one pedestrian street, and two days is enough. Base on Paros. Cross for a night, or for the day, and understand which of the two you actually want.

Choose Antiparos if…

  • You want a deliberate slowdown. 1,211 residents, one main street, and the cafés putting chairs out at sunset is genuinely the whole evening.
  • You want walking distances. Antiparos is 35 km² and you can reach Psaraliki, Sifneikos and the Chora on foot from most rooms.
  • You want Despotiko. The uninhabited islet opposite holds an active excavation of a major Apollo sanctuary, and it is the most interesting thing on either island that almost nobody mentions.
  • You want the cave. The Cave of Antiparos is a large stalactite chamber carrying inscriptions left by visitors from antiquity onward — a French ambassador famously held Christmas Mass inside it in 1673.
  • You have two days, not three, and want quiet beaches like Soros and Apantima rather than an organised strand.

Choose Paros if…

  • Beaches are the priority — 5.0, a perfect score. Kolymbithres' wind-sculpted granite, Santa Maria, Golden Beach, Faragas and Monastiri are a genuinely varied set.
  • You want an evening out. Nightlife 5.0, level with Mykonos and Ios: Naoussa's harbour bars and Parikia's old town both deliver, in different registers.
  • You want ferries. Paros is one of the great Cyclades hubs — access 4.5, its own airport, and connections almost everywhere, which matters if you are island-hopping.
  • You want restaurants and choice. Paros has developed into the sophisticated Cycladic base, and the food scene is a real reason to go.
  • You want history without effort: the Panagia Ekatontapyliani in Parikia is one of the oldest churches in Greece, and the Marathi quarries produced the Parian marble the ancient world preferred.

Seven minutes, two economies

The Pounta–Antiparos ferry crosses in about seven minutes and runs constantly, so the practical question is not which island to visit but where to sleep. Paros is 196 km² with 13,700 people, two working ports, an airport and a season that starts in May. Antiparos is 35 km² with 1,211 people and one settlement. That is the entire difference, and both scores — 4.1 and 4.0 — are honest about the fact that neither is better, they are differently sized.

Paros earns two perfect scores

The 5.0 for beaches is spread around the whole coast rather than concentrated: Kolymbithres on the Naoussa side, where the granite has been weathered into smooth shapes you swim between; Santa Maria and Chrissi Akti (Golden Beach) on the exposed east, which has hosted windsurfing world-cup events; Faragas and Monastiri for calmer water.

The 5.0 for nightlife is the other. Naoussa has gone from fishing village to one of the most fashionable harbours in the Cyclades, and Parikia's old town has a denser, less polished bar scene behind the waterfront. Level with Mykonos on our scoring, at less money — though not at little money.

Which brings up the honest problem. Paros scores 2.2 for affordability, among the lowest on this site. It is no longer the value alternative to Mykonos it was a decade ago, and August prices in Naoussa reflect that.

Antiparos is small, and no longer secret

The Chora is genuinely lovely: a Venetian kastro from the 1440s at its centre, built as a single defensive block with houses forming the outer wall, and one pedestrianised main street running up from the port. Beaches are modest but good — Psaraliki right by town, Sifneikos, and Soros and Apantima on the south coast.

Two things deserve more attention than they get. Despotiko, the uninhabited islet off the southwest coast, is one of the most significant active archaeological digs in the Cyclades — a large Archaic sanctuary of Apollo, reachable by small boat in season, and genuinely worth arranging. And the Cave of Antiparos is a serious cavern whose walls carry graffiti from ancient visitors through to 18th- and 19th-century travellers.

Being straight about the rest: Antiparos has become a discreet high-end hideaway over the past decade, with villa money above the south coast and a well-known Hollywood homeowner, and its affordability score of 2.8 is only slightly better than Paros's. It is quieter, not cheaper in any meaningful sense.

How to actually do it

For most people: base on Paros, ideally Naoussa or Parikia, and treat Antiparos as a day or an overnight. That gives you the beaches, the ferries and the restaurants, plus the slow evening. Reverse it only if quiet is the specific goal — two or three nights on Antiparos with the boat to Despotiko is a lovely, narrow trip, and you will run out of island before you run out of time.

The honest verdict

Paros, for almost everyone, and the 4.1 is a fair reflection of an island that is good at more things. Antiparos is not a lesser Paros; it is a deliberately smaller one, and the mistake is going there expecting variety rather than stillness. Do not choose Antiparos to save money — that is not what it offers any more. Choose it for two days of doing very little, seven minutes from everything else.
