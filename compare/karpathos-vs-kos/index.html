Our verdict

The short answer: Karpathos is the better island — 3.8 to 3.7, with beaches at 4.4 against 4.0 — but Kos is the easier holiday by a wide margin. The gap that actually decides this is access: Kos scores 4.6, Karpathos 2.0, the widest single-category gap in any Dodecanese comparison on this site. Kos has a large airport with direct flights from most of Europe, flat terrain you can cycle, and the Asklepion. Karpathos has Apella, Olympos and a coastline most people have never seen, and it will cost you a connection, a hire car and some wind. Both are worth four days.

Choose Karpathos if…

  • Beaches are the whole reason you are going. Apella is regularly called the best beach in the Dodecanese and Kyra Panagia, Achata and Diakoftis are not far behind — 4.4 against 4.0.
  • You want somewhere that still feels remote. 6,200 people on 300 km², against 33,300 on Kos.
  • You want Olympos: a mountain village in the far north where older women still wear traditional dress daily and the dialect preserves Doric forms. There is nothing else quite like it in Greece.
  • You windsurf or kitesurf. Afiartis in the south is a world-tour venue, and the wind that makes it great is reliable all summer.
  • You will hire a car and drive rough roads. Car reliance scores 5.0 — the maximum. Without a vehicle you will see almost nothing.

Choose Kos if…

  • You want to book a flight and arrive. Access 4.6 against 2.0: Kos has direct summer flights from across Europe, plus ferries to Rhodes, Bodrum and the rest of the Dodecanese.
  • You want serious antiquity. The Asklepion — the healing sanctuary where the Hippocratic tradition was taught — plus the Roman Odeon, Casa Romana, the ancient agora and the Knights' castle at the harbour. Culture 4.2 against 4.0.
  • You want an evening. Nightlife 4.0 against Karpathos's 2.2; Kos Town's bar street is the real thing, and Kardamena is the package-holiday version.
  • You would rather cycle than drive. Kos is flat, has proper bike lanes, and is one of the few Greek islands where a bicycle is a genuine option.
  • You want thermal water. Therma, east of Kos Town, is a hot spring that runs straight into the sea.

Access is the whole argument

Karpathos scores 2.0 and that number is doing real work. There are direct charter flights in season, mostly from Scandinavia and a handful of other markets, but most people arrive via Rhodes or Athens, and the ferry is a long haul on an exposed stretch of sea. Once you land, the island is 50 km long with a mountain spine and one difficult road north; the drive to Diafani and Olympos is slow and in places rough, and many visitors do that leg by boat instead.

Kos scores 4.6. The airport takes wide-body charter traffic all summer, the island is 40 km end to end on good flat roads, and the port has fast links to Rhodes, Kalymnos, Patmos and Bodrum. If your holiday is two weeks with children and a fixed budget, this difference is not a detail — it is the trip.

Beaches: Karpathos, clearly

4.4 against 4.0 understates it slightly, because the character is so different. Karpathos's east coast is a sequence of deep bays under bare mountain — Apella, Achata, Kyra Panagia with its red-domed church — reached by hairpin descents or by the day boat from Pigadia. Diakoftis in the south is white sand over turquoise shallows and looks tropical. Lefkos on the west is the low-key alternative. Almost none of it is developed.

Kos's beaches are good and easy rather than dramatic: the long shallow sand at Tigaki and Marmari, the Kefalos bay strip in the southwest with Paradise Beach and its seabed bubbles, Agios Stefanos with the Early Christian basilica ruins and Kastri islet just offshore. Sun beds, tavernas, watersports. Genuinely fine — and if you have small children, easier than anything on Karpathos.

The wind, and what it means

Karpathos is one of the windiest inhabited islands in Greece. In July and August the northerly can blow hard for days, which is exactly why Afiartis hosts world-tour windsurfing and why the beach umbrellas are anchored the way they are. It also means some east-coast bays are perfect while the south is unswimmable, or the reverse, and you plan each day around it. Some people find this exhilarating; some find it exhausting. Be honest with yourself about which you are.

Kos gets the same meltemi but is lower, flatter and better sheltered on the south coast, so it interferes less.

Culture: closer than you would expect

Kos wins narrowly on paper — 4.2 to 4.0 — and it deserves to. The Asklepion is a substantial site with a real intellectual history attached, and Kos Town has Roman remains, a Knights of St John castle and a good archaeological museum, all walkable.

But Karpathos's cultural case is not a site, it is a living one. Olympos was effectively cut off by road until recent decades, and the result is a village where dress, dialect, bread ovens and music survived rather than being revived for visitors. Going there is a different kind of experience from reading labels at an excavation, and for some travellers it is the more valuable one.

The honest verdict

If you are willing to connect through Rhodes or Athens, hire a car, and accept wind, go to Karpathos — better beaches, far fewer people, and Olympos. If you want a direct flight, a flat island you can cycle, a proper ancient site and somewhere that works for a family or a package, Kos is the sensible answer and there is no shame in it. The one combination that does not work is Karpathos without a car: the island is too big and too vertical, and you will spend four days in Pigadia wondering what the fuss was about.
