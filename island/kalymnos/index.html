The sponge divers' island — world-class climbing, vertical limestone cliffs and a deep sea tradition

📍 110 km²👥 16,179🗓 3 days✈ Airport🚗 Recommended

Pothia's sponge heritage, the climbing villages of the west coast, and the beaches and caves of Telendos.

Overall rating: 3.5/5 · 110 km² · 16179 residents

Last updated: July 5, 2026

Kalymnos has two identities. For millennia it was the sponge-diving capital of the Aegean — men dove to 100m without equipment, many dying of the bends, to fuel a global industry. Since 1997, it has become one of the world's top five sport climbing destinations, with 3000 bolted routes on extraordinary limestone cliffs. Today the island draws climbers from around the world, alongside a quieter tourism drawn by the dramatic mountains, clear water, and small traditional villages.

Getting there

✈ Airport (small)⛵ via Kos shuttle€10–20

Small domestic airport (Sky Express). Direct Piraeus ferry exists (10–13h overnight), but the most common entry is flying to Kos and taking the 30-minute shuttle from Mastichari. Frequent daily.

Read full route

Tip: For the climbing season (Oct–Apr) the Kos shuttle is your friend — direct ferries thin out.

Best time to visit Kalymnos

Kalymnos is Greece's climbing capital — over 3000 sport routes carved into limestone cliffs. Climbers come November to April when it's cool. Swimmers come June to September. The two communities barely overlap. Sponge diving heritage everywhere. Easter is intense; Anastenaria fire-walking on the Tuesday after Easter is the wildest event of the year.

3-day itinerary for Kalymnos

Day 1: Pothia & the Sponge Heritage

Overnight: Masouri · Drive: 10 km, ~15 min

  1. 09:00 · Pothia
    The capital — a working Greek port built on the slopes above a natural harbour. The Italian-era waterfront buildings are the finest in the Dodecanese outside Rhodes and Kos. Not pretty in a Cycladic sense but genuinely interesting.
  2. 10:00 · Sponge Factory & Museum
    Working sponge factories line the harbour — buy a genuine Kalymnos sponge. The small Nautical Museum tells the extraordinary and tragic story of the island's sponge-diving industry.
  3. 11:30 · Archaeological Museum
    Small but excellent museum including the Lady of Kalymnos, a magnificent 1st-century BC bronze statue recovered from the sea in 1994. A rare survival.
  4. 14:00 · Chora (Old Capital)
    The old Byzantine capital above Pothia — a medieval village with a Knights' castle and views over the island. Quieter and more atmospheric than the modern port.
  5. 17:00 · Myrties Beach
    After Chora, the natural detour west drops you at Myrties — the prettiest beach on the island, looking directly across the narrow channel to Telendos islet. Fine sand and pebbles, taverns set just back from the water, and the famous Telendos sunset as the cliffs opposite catch the last light. A perfect transition before returning to Pothia for dinner.
  6. 🍴 Stop for dinner · Pothia — see Eat & Drink below
Eat & Drink
🍴
Dinner · Pothia
The harbour tavernas do excellent fresh fish. In the backstreets, Xefteris is the island's old-timer (since 1933) for classic Kalymnian home cooking.

Day 2: Masouri & the Climbing Coast

Overnight: Masouri · Drive: 20 km, ~30 min

  1. 09:30 · Masouri
    The climbing capital of Kalymnos — a lively village on the west coast entirely built around the sport. Shops selling gear, cafes full of climbers from every country. Even if you don't climb, it's a fascinating scene.
  2. 11:00 · Grande Grotta
    The largest and most famous climbing cave on Kalymnos — a huge overhanging limestone cavern above the coast. Free to visit. Watch the climbers above while swimming below.
  3. 13:00 · Myrties Beach
    Pebble beach next to Masouri, with the islet of Telendos directly opposite — one of the most dramatic views in the Dodecanese. Good tavernas on the waterfront.
  4. 16:00 · Emborios Village
    Traditional fishing village at the northern end of the island — small pebble beach, a handful of tavernas, and a refreshingly unhurried pace compared to Masouri.
  5. 🍴 Stop for dinner · Masouri — see Eat & Drink below
Eat & Drink
🍴
Dinner · Masouri
Aegean Tavern at the end of Masouri beach (since 1989) is the island's best — a modern take on Greek cooking; Thrimpi just beyond has a white terrace for the sunset.

Day 3: Telendos Island & Departure

Drive: 5 km, ~15 min

  1. 10:00 · Telendos Boat
    Small boat from Myrties — the crossing takes 10 minutes. Telendos broke off from Kalymnos in an earthquake in 554 AD. No cars on the island.
  2. 10:15 · Telendos Village
    A tiny car-free village with a few tavernas, a small hotel and the best pebble beach on Kalymnos. Excellent lunch at one of the waterfront tavernas.
  3. 11:30 · Hochlakas Beach
    Pebble beach at Telendos — crystal clear water, no crowds, and views back to Kalymnos. One of the best swimming spots in the Dodecanese.
  4. 16:30 · Pothia — Departure
    Ferry to Kos (frequent), Leros, Patmos, Piraeus or Rhodes. Kalymnos has its own small airport with Athens flights.

Top beaches of Kalymnos

Myrties Beach

The best-known beach on Kalymnos — pebble shoreline with extraordinary views across to the islet of Telendos. The sunset here is one of the best in the Dodecanese. The water is exceptionally clear and the tavernas on the waterfront are excellent.

Hochlakas Beach (Telendos)

The finest beach on Telendos and arguably in the whole of Kalymnos — white pebble with extraordinary water clarity and views back to Kalymnos. The car-free island adds to the peaceful atmosphere.

🎉Festivals & Events

Easter dynamite throwingHoly Saturday night — 1 May 2027
Kalymnos does Easter louder than anywhere in Greece. Rival groups, each 'owning' one of the two mountains above Pothia, hurl homemade dynamite sticks across the harbour all through the Resurrection liturgy — a deafening barrage that rattles windows in the town below. A sponge-diver's tradition rooted in the island's resistance streak. Watch from a distance: this is genuinely loud and genuinely explosive.
