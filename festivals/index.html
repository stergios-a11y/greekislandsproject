Panigiri of Panagia Flevariotissa

1–2 February · Flevariotissa monastery

The winter panigiri of Astypalaia, at the monastery of Flevariotissa in the island's interior on 1-2 February: after vespers the courtyard fills with food cooked by the islanders, violins and dancing through the night. Only for those who are on the island in winter, which is exactly its charm.

SpetsesReligiousconfirm locally

Feast of the Three Spetsiot Neomartyrs, Spetses Town

2–3 February

Spetses has three local saints, the neomartyrs Nikolaos, Ioannis and Stamatios, martyred in Chios in 1822, and their church in town keeps 2-3 February with vespers and an episcopal liturgy; the 2022 bicentenary brought several bishops. A winter feast for the island's own, not a tourist event, but the tavernas around the Dapia are open.

source
PaxosReligious

Feast of Agios Charalambos, patron of Paxos, Gaios

10 February

Agios Charalambos is the protector of Paxos and his 10 February feast is the island's winter high point: hierarchical liturgy at his church in Gaios with the Metropolitan of Corfu, the mayor and the schools, then a procession of the icon along the waterfront and a reception. Boats from Corfu run all winter; almost nothing else is open, which is the point.

source
SkopelosReligious

Feast of Agios Riginos, Skopelos Town

24–25 February

The patron of Skopelos, a 4th-century bishop beheaded on the island, is honoured on 24-25 February: on the eve his relics are carried from the church of Christos in the Chora to the monastery of Agios Riginos 4 km south-west, with a stop at the old Ai-Riginaki bridge where he is said to have been martyred, and after the liturgy the faithful hand out local sweets (the Skopelos loukoumi and amygdalota). It is the one day the whole island is on its feet in winter; ferries from Volos still run but hotels are mostly shut, so book a room in the Chora in advance.

Skyrian Carnival (Apokries)

13–14 March

Among the most distinctive carnivals in Greece — the 'Yeros' figure dressed in goat skins and bells dances through the village. Pre-Christian roots, deeply Skyrian.

ChiosCarnival

Mostra of Thymiana (carnival)

14–16 March

Chios's famous carnival custom, listed in the national intangible-heritage register: on Carnival Sunday (the last Sunday before Clean Monday) Thymiana, just south of Chios town, re-enacts the villagers' victory over pirates with the 'talimi' sword dance and a procession of masked 'Mostres', now combined with a float parade. Run by the MEOTH association over the last carnival weekend, with thousands attending including visitors from Turkey. Go early, the village roads are closed.

Crete (Rethymno)Carnival

Rethymno Carnival (grand parade)

14 March

Crete's biggest carnival, running the whole Triodion with a treasure hunt, a night parade and a children's parade, and peaking on Cheese Sunday (the day before Clean Monday) with the grand parade along the waterfront: in 2026, 28 floats and about 11,000 participants, ending in fireworks. Hotels in the old town book out for that weekend; the parade starts mid-afternoon and the streets around the harbour close from midday.

14 March · Hydra Town / Kamini

LesvosCarnival

Agiasos Carnival (Agiasotiki Apokria)

15–17 March

The most literary carnival in the Aegean: the 'Satyros' carnival society of Agiasos writes satirical verse in the local dialect, performs it in the streets and runs masked parties, a children's masquerade and a 'patinada' with traditional musicians on Carnival Sunday, all culminating on Clean Monday with kites, free bean soup and rice pudding and the big satirical procession. Runs the last two weekends before Lent; the Monday is the day to be there.

Clean Monday customs, Gerani and Armenoi

15 March

The Rethymno carnival closes on Clean Monday in two villages just outside town: Gerani (west, on the coast road) and Armenoi (south, on the Spili road) stage the old Cretan Lenten customs, mock weddings and satirical skits, with lagana, olives, taramas and wine for everyone and lyra players in the square. Go around midday; both are 10–15 minutes from Rethymno.

SkyrosReligiousconfirm locally

Feast of Agios Georgios (patron), Agios Georgios Monastery, Chora

22–23 April · Skyros Chora

St George is the patron of Skyros (the Venetians called it Isola San Giorgio) and his 10th-century monastery sits on the rock under the castle above the Chora. The feast on 23 April, moved to Easter Monday when it falls in Lent, is a liturgy and procession rather than a tavla panigiri, but it is the day the Chora dresses up. Steep climb; go up early and have coffee in the square afterwards.

Crete (Chania)Religious

Blessing and milking of the flocks for Agios Georgios, Asi Gonia

23 April

On St George's morning shepherds drive thousands of sheep and goats down through the village lanes to the church of Agios Georgios Galatas, where the priest blesses the flocks; the animals are then milked on the spot and the milk (several tonnes of it) is handed out to everyone present. It is a real working custom, not a show, and it ends with raki and Cretan music in the kafeneia. Be there by 9 am, park at the edge of the village and walk in; if 23 April falls in Lent the feast moves to Easter Monday.

Feast of Agios Georgios, patron of Koufonisi

23 April · Pano Koufonisi (Chora)

St George is the island's protector: after the liturgy on 23 April (or Easter Monday if the date falls in Lent) the icon is taken round the island by caique with the fishing boats escorting it along the coast, the lanes are strewn with flowers, and the community serves boiled beef and patsas with drinks before the party moves to the tavernas. A rare chance to see Koufonisi as a working fishing island before the season.

CorfuReligious

Palm Sunday litany of Agios Spyridon, Corfu town

25 April

The longest of the litanies, commemorating the 1630 plague, takes the saint's body right around the old town on Palm Sunday morning with all eighteen or so philharmonic bands of the island playing; it is the start of Corfu's famous Easter week, which continues with the Holy Saturday litany and the pot-throwing. Come at 11am and stand near the Spianada to hear the bands cross.

PatmosReligious

26 April

Patmos's Easter is among the most spectacular in Greece — the monastery hosts the niptiras ceremony on Holy Thursday, with the abbot ritually washing 12 monks' feet. Pilgrims from across the Orthodox world.

PatmosReligious

Niptiras (Holy Thursday foot-washing)

29 April

Open-air ceremony in Chora's main square: the abbot of the Monastery of Saint John ritually washes the feet of 12 monks, reenacting Christ washing the disciples' feet. One of the few places in the Orthodox world where the rite is still performed publicly.

IosMusic

Omiria (Homer festival)

May

Ios claims Homer's grave — tradition puts it at Plakotos in the north — and honours him each May with the Omiria, the island's main cultural festival under UNESCO's auspices: performances, exhibitions, concerts and contests. A quieter, older side of an island better known for its summer party.

ChiosReligious

Rouketopolemos (Rocket War)

1 May

In Vrondados, two rival parishes — Agios Markos and Panagia Erithiani — fire tens of thousands of homemade rockets at each other's bell towers across a 400m valley, while the midnight Easter liturgies continue inside both churches. Possibly the most spectacular Easter event in Greece. Watch from the slopes of Mount Aepos for safety.

CorfuArts & culture

Botides (pot smashing)

1 May

At exactly 11am on Holy Saturday, signaling the First Resurrection, residents throw large clay water-filled pots from their balconies onto Liston Square below. The crash of thousands of pots, accompanied by philharmonic bands, is meant to drive away evil. Adopted from the Venetians. Arrive by 9am for a viewing spot.

KalymnosCarnival

Easter Sunday dynamite on the hills, Pothia

2–3 May

Kalymnos does Easter louder than anywhere in Greece. Rival groups, each 'owning' one of the two mountains above Pothia, hurl homemade dynamite sticks across the harbour all through the Resurrection liturgy — a deafening barrage that rattles windows in the town below. A sponge-diver's tradition rooted in the island's resistance streak. Watch from a distance: this is genuinely loud and genuinely explosive.

FolegandrosReligious

The icon of the Panagia is carried from house to house across the island for the week after Easter — a procession unique to Folegandros, with locals hosting the icon overnight in turn.

NaxosReligious

Pilgrimage of Panagia Argokoiliotissa, Koronos

6–7 May · Koronos (Argokoili)

On the Friday of Bright Week (five days after Easter) the whole of Naxos climbs to Argokoili above Koronos, where the icon of the Virgin was found in 1836: the icon is carried down from the old church on Thursday evening and back up with a huge procession around 10:30 on Friday morning, with the Metropolitan, a vigil overnight and stalls selling food. Many walk up from Koronos; if you drive, park early on the road below because it fills for kilometres.

Panigiri of Zoodochos Pigi monastery, Chora

6–7 May · Chora (Kastro)

The fortified 17th-century monastery above Kastro celebrates on Bright Friday, the first Friday after Easter: on the eve the sponsoring family lays on a communal meal at the monastery for the whole village, and in return keeps the icon in their house for the year. It's the island's spring feast and, with the Easter week, the best reason to come to Sikinos out of season; climb the steps from Kastro before dusk.

PsaraArts & culture

Commemoration of the Psara Holocaust

Last Sunday of June

Tiny Psara remembers the 1824 Ottoman destruction that wiped out one of the revolution's great naval islands — the last defenders blew up their own powder magazine at Mavri Rachi rather than surrender. The last Sunday of June brings a memorial service, wreaths and cultural events. Solomos's poem about the 'black ridge of Psara' is part of every Greek's schooling.

SkopelosReligious

Throughout summer (June-September)

Not an official festival but a phenomenon: Skopelos hosts a steady stream of Mamma Mia! pilgrimages to Agios Ioannis chapel (the wedding scene). Local tavernas screen the films, host themed nights, and run weekly tribute events through summer. The tourism economy runs on it.

In Agia Paraskevi village, an ancient and unusual ritual: a bull is paraded, blessed, then ritually slaughtered and shared communally. Pre-Christian roots, controversial in modern times, but among Greece's most extraordinary surviving folk traditions.

MilosReligious

Folk Saint John's Eve tradition still alive on Milos: at sunset, unmarried girls collect water from a well in silence and predict their future husbands through tokens placed in it overnight. Bonfires are jumped on the 24th. Smaller and more intimate than the better-known Cretan version.

HydraReligious

Feast of Agios Konstantinos o Ydraios (patron), Hydra Town

13–15 November

Hydra's own saint is Konstantinos the New Martyr, born on the island around 1770 and executed in Rhodes in 1800; his relics lie in the cathedral of the Panagia on the harbour, and 13-15 November is the island's biggest religious weekend, with vespers on the 13th, an episcopal liturgy and a procession of the relics round the port on the 14th. Off-season, so the island is quiet and cheap; the Metropolitan of Hydra presides and the port cafés fill after the procession.

ZakynthosReligious

Feast of Agios Dionysios (his dormition), Zakynthos town

16–18 December

The winter feast of the patron: after the liturgy on 17 December the incorrupt body is carried through the town to Solomos Square and back via Agios Markos with the philharmonics of Zakynthos, Artemision and Alykes, the schools and a dozen visiting bishops; the body stays out for veneration for two more days before being returned to its silver casket.
